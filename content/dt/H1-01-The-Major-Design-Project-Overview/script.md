NARRATOR: Welcome to Year 12, and to the heart of the whole course. For the last three modules you've built the Preliminary toolkit: the design process, DRIPER, define, research, ideate, produce, evaluate, refine; the eleven factors affecting design, FFA EQ NEW LOAd; and the project toolkit from module three, briefs, research, management, communication and evaluation. Everything so far has been preparation. Now you put it to work, on one real project, for the biggest single piece of assessment in this subject: the major design project, the MDP. This first episode is orientation, what the MDP is, how it's structured, and crucially, how it's marked, because understanding the marking changes how you work from day one.

NARRATOR: Today's home topic is the major design project and its folio. There isn't one tight syllabus dot-point here, instead this episode frames the whole of module H1, which delivers outcomes H4 and H5, and it teaches a meta-skill that earns marks across every part of the MDP: the difference between narrating what you did and justifying why. By the end you'll understand the three phases of the MDP, the role of the folio, and how to write for marks. Let me start with what the MDP actually is.

NARRATOR: The major design project is exactly what its name says: a major project in which you identify a real need or opportunity and design, produce and evaluate a quality solution, a product, system or environment, to meet it. It's not an exam answer, it's a genuine, months-long design project that you run yourself, and it's worth a very large share of your HSC mark, so it deserves real care. The syllabus structures it into three phases, and they map neatly onto the design process you already know, so you're not learning something new, you're applying DRIPER at full scale. The phases are in Listing 1.

NARRATOR: Phase one, project proposal and project management. You identify and explore the need, define the areas you'll investigate, set the criteria to evaluate success, and create your management plans for action, time and finance. That's the define and plan stage, DRIPER's Define plus the project management from module three. Phase two, project development and realisation. You research, generate and develop ideas creatively, study industrial and commercial practices, select and safely use resources, apply production techniques, and actually build the thing, communicating throughout. That's DRIPER's Research, Ideate and Produce. Phase three, project evaluation. You evaluate the project against your criteria, continually and finally, and assess its impact on the individual, society and the environment. That's DRIPER's Evaluate and Refine. Three phases, propose and plan, develop and realise, evaluate, the design process run for real.

NARRATOR: Now the folio, because this is where many students misunderstand the task. The folio is the documented record of your entire design process, your research, sketches, decisions, tests, management plans and evaluation, presented as evidence. And here's the point that changes everything: the MDP is marked on the folio and the project together, and the folio rewards evidence of your process, not just a pretty final product. A beautiful object with a thin folio scores poorly; a solid object with a folio that shows rigorous research, justified decisions and continual evaluation scores well. The textbook and the marking guidelines reward process, so document everything as you go. The folio's role is in Listing 2.

NARRATOR: That leads to the single most valuable habit in Year 12, the meta-skill I promised, and it applies to your folio and to the written exam's Section two: the difference between narrating and justifying. A weak folio page or exam answer narrates, it says what you did. "I chose pine. Then I cut the legs. Then I sanded it." A strong one justifies, it says why, against the factors, with evidence. "Pine was selected because its properties, sufficient strength and excellent workability, suited the function of a lightweight hand-assembled stool, and this was confirmed by the joint test in my folio." Hear the difference. The pattern that scores is decision, reason, factor, evidence: state the decision, give the reason, name the relevant factor from module one, and point to the evidence that supports it. Narrate and you cap low; justify and you reach the top band. That contrast is in Listing 3, and it's the through-line of this entire module.

QUESTION: Retrieval, because this habit is everything in Year 12. What's the difference between narrating and justifying in a folio or exam answer, and which one scores? Pause the player, answer both, then play.

NARRATOR: Here's the answer. Narrating describes what you did, the steps you took, and it caps low because anyone can list steps. Justifying explains why you made each decision, with reasons tied to the factors affecting design and supported by evidence such as research or test results, and that scores, because it demonstrates the reasoned, evidence-based process the markers reward. The pattern is decision, reason, factor, evidence. If you said "justify, by giving reasons and evidence, not just what you did", that's exactly it. Carry that into every page you write.

NARRATOR: Let me give you the module map, in Listing 5, so you know where this is going, and plant two more mnemonics you'll use throughout. Over the coming episodes we'll work through the MDP journey: identifying the need and writing the proposal, applying the factors and analysing success and failure, studying designers' practice, researching and experimenting, selecting resources safely, relating industrial practice, managing the project, communicating, and evaluating. Two hooks to hold now: NICe, for what the proposal must contain, the need, the areas of investigation, and the criteria to evaluate success, and ATF, for the management plans, action, time and finance. DRIPER for the process, NICe for the proposal, ATF for the management. Those three are the spine of module H1.

NARRATOR: Let me make the folio concrete, in Listing 4, with a model contents list mapped to DRIPER, so you can see how a folio is organised: a project proposal with the need and criteria; a research section with your investigation and findings; an ideas-generation section with sketches and concepts; a development section with prototypes and the decisions behind them, each justified; a production record; and an evaluation section judging the project against the criteria and assessing its impact. Notice every section is evidence of a stage of the process, that's what the folio is, the design process made visible and justified.

NARRATOR: Let me weave the connections, briefly, because this module is where everything converges. Back to module one: the MDP runs DRIPER and is judged against the eleven factors. Back to module three: the proposal is a design brief, the management uses Gantt charts and finance plans, the evaluation uses the criteria checklist, all module-three skills now for real. And forward: every remaining episode in this module is one phase of the MDP, and the case studies and the innovation module, H2, run alongside it. The MDP is the spine the whole HSC course hangs on.

NARRATOR: One trap to flag, and it's the big one for the whole year. Students pour effort into the final product and treat the folio as an afterthought to write up at the end. That loses marks two ways: the folio is half the assessment, and a folio written from memory at the end can't show the genuine, dated, evidence-based process the markers reward. Document as you go, and justify every significant decision in the moment. Process, evidenced and justified, beats a pretty outcome.

NARRATOR: Recap, the way you'd carry it into the year. One: the MDP is a major project where you design, produce and evaluate a real solution to an identified need, worth a large share of your HSC mark. Two: it has three phases, proposal and management, development and realisation, and evaluation, which map onto DRIPER. Three: the folio is the documented evidence of your process, and it's marked alongside the project, evidence of process beats a pretty product. Four: the meta-skill is to justify, not narrate, decision, reason, factor, evidence. Five: hold DRIPER for the process, NICe for the proposal, and ATF for the management. Hold those five, and you've got the shape of the whole module.

NARRATOR: Now the exam-style questions, real NESA format, get a pen. Four questions, model answers in Listing 6.

QUESTION: Question one. Identify the three phases of the major design project. Three marks. Pause the player, write your answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. The three phases are: project proposal and project management, where the need is identified, criteria for success are set, and action, time and finance plans are made; project development and realisation, where ideas are researched, developed, and the solution is produced; and project evaluation, where the project is evaluated against its criteria and its impact assessed. Three marks, one per phase correctly described. Mapping them to define, develop, evaluate, or to DRIPER, shows you understand them as the design process.

QUESTION: Question two. Explain why the folio is important in the major design project. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. The folio is important because it is the documented evidence of the entire design process, the research, decisions, tests, management plans and evaluation, and the MDP is marked on this process, not only on the final product. A strong folio demonstrates rigorous research, decisions justified against the factors affecting design, and continual evaluation, which is what the marking rewards, whereas a thin folio scores poorly even with a good product. It must be compiled throughout the project so it shows a genuine, dated process. Three marks: state that the folio documents the process, that the MDP is marked on process evidence, and that it must be built as you go. The marker wants process-evidence, not "it shows your work".

QUESTION: Question three. Explain the difference between narrating and justifying a design decision, with an example. Four marks. Pause the player and write a full answer before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Narrating a decision simply describes what was done, for example "I chose pine and cut the legs", which provides no reasoning and earns few marks. Justifying a decision explains why it was made, linking it to relevant factors and supporting it with evidence, for example "pine was selected because its properties of sufficient strength and good workability suited the function of a lightweight stool, as confirmed by a joint strength test recorded in my folio". The difference is that justification follows the pattern decision, reason, factor, evidence, demonstrating a reasoned, evidence-based process, which is what earns the higher bands in both the folio and the written exam. Four marks: define narrating, define justifying, give a contrasting example, and name the decision-reason-factor-evidence pattern. This meta-skill is the most valuable thing in Year 12.

QUESTION: Question four. Explain why a designer should document the major design project throughout rather than at the end. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. A designer should document throughout because the folio must show a genuine, ongoing design process, the real research, decisions and tests as they happened, which cannot be authentically reconstructed from memory at the end. Documenting as you go captures justifications and evidence at the moment decisions are made, supports continual evaluation, and ensures nothing is forgotten, producing a folio that demonstrates the rigorous process the marking rewards. Leaving it to the end risks a thin, inaccurate record and lost marks. Three marks: state that the folio must show a genuine ongoing process, that documenting in the moment captures evidence and justification, and that leaving it to the end loses authenticity and marks. This reinforces process-over-product.

NARRATOR: That's the orientation. The lines to carry into the year: the MDP is a real project in three phases, proposal and management, development and realisation, evaluation, run as DRIPER; the folio is the documented evidence of that process and is marked alongside the product; the meta-skill is to justify, not narrate, decision, reason, factor, evidence; and you document throughout.
