# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the purpose,
the source types, segmentation, the Five Ps, the cautionary examples and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — The purpose of market research (four jobs)
```text
1. Confirm a GENUINE NEED (don't design what nobody wants)
2. Identify OPPORTUNITIES (gaps in the market a new product could fill)
3. Identify & understand the TARGET MARKET (who will buy it)
4. Analyse the COMPETITION (what already exists)

Market research asks "will it SELL, and to whom?"  (vs user/design research = "what does the
user NEED and how do they use it?" — overlapping, but market research has a commercial edge.)
```

### Listing 2 — Primary vs secondary research (the examinable distinction)
| | Primary research | Secondary research |
|---|---|---|
| What | Data you collect yourself, direct from the source | Data someone else already collected |
| Methods/sources | Surveys, interviews, telemarketing, observation, experiments | ABS, CSIRO, Choice magazine, chambers of commerce, reports, internet |
| Strengths | Current, specific to your project | Cheaper, faster |
| Weaknesses | Costs time & money | General; may be dated |

> The test: WHO originally gathered the data. (Always correctly source secondary research; you may
> need permission to use it.)

### Listing 3 — Target market & segmentation
```text
SEGMENTATION = dividing the population into DEMOGRAPHICS (groups with common characteristics)
to identify the TARGET MARKET. Categories:
  age · income · profession · geographic location · gender

Define the demographic -> you know who you're designing for -> feeds the APPROPRIATENESS factor
and the brief.
```

### Listing 4 — The Five Ps (analysing the marketing environment / competitors)
| P | What to research |
|---|---|
| Product | Features, materials, construction the competition offers |
| Price | What competitors charge |
| Promotion | How it's marketed/advertised |
| Place | Where & how it's sold/distributed |
| Packaging | How it's presented |

> Analyse competitors across the Five Ps -> find where you can do better -> that gap is the
> OPPORTUNITY.

### Listing 5 — Cautionary & validating examples
```text
FORD EDSEL (research MISREAD): Ford did extensive market research, then launched the Edsel as a
  "sure thing" — but the market had shifted to smaller/economical cars and the findings were
  misread. One of history's biggest flops. -> research must be ACCURATE, CURRENT, CORRECTLY READ.

FLOW HIVE (research VALIDATED): the creators used a crowdfunding campaign as live market research/
  validation — the public's huge financial response confirmed the need before mass production.
```

### Listing 6 — Headline question: "How does market research identify needs & opportunities?"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "You ask people what they want." -> one technique, no process.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - PRIMARY research (surveys/interviews/observation of the target market) -> unmet want = NEED.
  - SECONDARY research + competitor analysis (Five Ps) -> a market gap = OPPORTUNITY.
  - SEGMENTATION targets the right consumers.
  - CAVEAT: research must be correctly interpreted (Edsel) — a need is only useful if read right.
```

### Listing 7 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (3 marks) — Distinguish primary vs secondary market research.
  Primary = collected yourself, direct (surveys/interviews/observation) — current, specific, costly.
  Secondary = already collected by others (ABS, Choice, reports, internet) — cheap, fast, general.
  Test: who originally gathered the data.

Q2 (3 marks) — Purpose of market research.
  Confirm a genuine need; identify opportunities/gaps; define the target market; analyse competitors;
  -> informs the brief & commercial success. (Breadth, not just "see what people want".)

Q3 (5 marks) — How does market research identify needs & opportunities?
  Primary (survey/interview/observe target market -> unmet want = need); secondary + competitor
  analysis via Five Ps (-> gap = opportunity); segmentation targets the right consumers; CAVEAT —
  must be correctly interpreted (Edsel failed on misread research).

Q4 (2 marks) — The Five Ps.
  Product, Price, Promotion, Place, Packaging. (Analysing competitors across them reveals where a
  new design can do better = an opportunity.)
```
