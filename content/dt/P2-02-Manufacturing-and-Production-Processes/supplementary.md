# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the three
scales of production, the selection trade-off, process families, and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — The three scales of production (JBM: "one, some, millions")
| Scale | Quantity | Key features | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **J**ob / one-off / custom | One (or very few) | Tailored to one client; unique; often = prototyping; 3D printing for small items; large jobs tendered/commissioned | Custom prosthetic, tailored suit, a building |
| **B**atch | A limited, defined run | Jigs & templates reproduce tasks without new tooling; some automation; standard sub-assembled parts; varies between batches | 50 designer chairs, 200 loaves, a limited release |
| **M**ass | Very large (continuous) | Standardisation → interchangeable parts; mechanisation & specialisation; high tooling cost; economies of scale | Drink bottles, smartphones, cars |

### Listing 2 — Selecting the scale: the volume / cost / flexibility trade-off
```text
                 VOLUME      COST PER UNIT     FLEXIBILITY      UPFRONT TOOLING
  Job (one-off)  low         HIGH             maximum          low
  Batch          medium      medium           medium           medium
  Mass           HIGH        LOW (econ. of    minimum          HIGH
                                scale)

Choose by asking: How many do I need?  How much variation?  How cheap must each be?
  one unique item for a client            -> Job
  a few hundred, varied between runs      -> Batch
  millions, identical, as cheap as poss.  -> Mass

Links to module-1 factors: FINANCE (tooling + unit cost) and QUALITY (consistency from
standardisation).
```

### Listing 3 — Production process families (select to suit material, scale, need)
| Family | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Forming | Shape material (often with heat) | Casting, forging, injection/blow moulding, extrusion |
| Cutting & shaping | Remove or shape material | Machining, turning (lathe), laser/CNC cutting |
| Joining | Join parts | Welding, bolting, riveting, adhesives |
| Finishing | Surface treatment | Sanding, painting, coating, polishing |

> A designer selects the technique to suit the **material, scale and need** (e.g. injection
> moulding for mass plastic parts; 3D printing/hand tools for a one-off). **Quality production
> skills** = performing techniques accurately and safely.

### Listing 4 — Mass production & workplace practices (the 2022-style question)
```text
How the trend to mass production reshapes WORK:
  - Division of labour & specialisation -> each worker/machine does ONE narrow repeated task
  - Deskilling -> broad craft skills replaced by repetitive ones
  - Reskilling -> new technical roles maintaining/programming machinery
  - Automation/mechanisation -> higher output & consistency BUT fewer workers (job losses)
  - WHS -> repetitive strain + heavy machinery = new hazards

Balanced judgement: more efficient, consistent, cheaper — but changes the nature of work,
reducing craft skill & employment while demanding new technical skills.
(This is really a TRENDS question -> Year 12 / H2.1.)
```

### Listing 5 — Worked example: matching products to scales
| Product | Scale | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Custom prosthetic limb | Job (one-off) | Must fit one individual exactly; 3D-printed from a body scan; high per-unit cost justified by perfect fit |
| Boutique run of 50 designer chairs | Batch | Jigs/templates reproduce efficiently; limited quantity keeps it exclusive; design tweakable between runs |
| Supermarket plastic drink bottle | Mass | Millions of identical units; injection-moulded, automated, standardised; lowest cost/unit via economies of scale |

### Listing 6 — Headline question: "mass production & workplace practices"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low):
  "Machines replace people." -> one blunt point, ignores the range of effects.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  Name several specific effects on WORK and weigh them:
  division of labour/specialisation · deskilling vs reskilling · automation & job losses · WHS
  -> balanced judgement (efficiency/consistency gained; craft skill & jobs changed/lost).
  Remember: this question is about PEOPLE, not machines.
```

### Listing 7 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (3 marks) — Distinguish batch vs mass production.
  Batch = limited defined run, jigs/templates, varies between runs (flexible). Mass = very large
  continuous output, standardised interchangeable parts, high automation, costly to change.
  Distinction: limited & flexible vs large & standardised (lowest cost/unit via economies of scale).

Q2 (3 marks) — Best scale for a single custom wheelchair + justify.
  Job/one-off — tailored to one user's measurements, a unique product; allows full customisation;
  high per-unit cost acceptable because only one is needed and an exact fit is essential.

Q3 (5 marks) — How does the trend to mass production affect workplace practices?
  Division of labour & specialisation; deskilling (craft -> repetitive) + reskilling (technical
  roles); automation raises output/consistency but cuts jobs; WHS hazards. Balanced judgement:
  efficient & consistent but changes the nature of work. (About people, not just machines.)

Q4 (3 marks) — Why does mass production give a lower cost per unit than job production?
  Large volume + standardised/interchangeable parts + automation; high upfront tooling cost spread
  across many units = economies of scale -> low average cost. Job production concentrates time/skill/
  setup into one unique item -> high per-unit cost.
```
