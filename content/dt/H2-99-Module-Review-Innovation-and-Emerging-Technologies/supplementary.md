### Listing 2 — The mnemonics recap
```text
TEMPLE  Timing · Emerging tech · Marketing · Political · Legal-economic · Era            (success)
GASP-I  Government · Associations/standards · Sponsors/funders · Patent offices · Industry (agencies)
SPEEG   Social · Political · Economic · Environmental · Global                            (trend drivers)
C-CWT   Changing social trends · Cultural diversity · changing nature of Work · Technological change
SIRRI   Sustainable tech · IP · designer Rights/responsibilities · Responsibilities to society · Impact (Aus)
LESEE   Legal · Ethical · Social · Economic · Ecological                                  (emerging tech)

ESSAY ENGINE: "TEMPLE the success, LESEE the tech, SIRRI the ethics, SPEEG & C-CWT the context, then JUDGE."
```

### Listing 3 — The exam structure
```text
SECTION I  (multiple choice) - innovation/emerging-tech heavy; recognise the frameworks in the options.
SECTION II (short answer)    - precise answers with the right terminology, a few marks each.
SECTION III(extended response, 15 marks) - run the ESSAY ENGINE: pick the framework -> apply to your
  innovation (Flow Hive) -> judge, in a thesis -> body -> judgement structure.
  Marked on: relevance to the question · relevant examples · logical & cohesive response.
```

### Listing 5 — Section I/II model answers
```text
Q1 (Section I) — strong product fails because a competitor dominates -> which factor?
  TIMING (+ marketing) within TEMPLE. A strong design can still fail on the innovation-success factors
  (cf. Microsoft Zune vs iPod).

Q2 (2) — Distinguish creativity vs innovation.
  Creativity = generating new ideas (the idea). Innovation = the idea successfully delivered. Creativity
  is necessary but not sufficient.

Q3 (3) — Three factors of innovation success.
  Timing; available & emerging technologies; marketing (+ political/economic-legal/historical-cultural). [TEMPLE]

Q4 (4) — How can an emerging technology have positive AND negative social effects?
  AI: + efficiency, automation of dangerous work, faster diagnosis; - job displacement, bias/fairness,
  privacy/accountability. Two-sided appraisal (LESEE social/ethical).

Q5 (3) — Influence of entrepreneurial activity on a successful innovation.
  Entrepreneur bears risk, develops a creative idea into a venture, brings it to market via agencies.
  Flow Hive: self-funded R&D, patent, crowdfunding ($12M+, validated demand) -> global product. Risk +
  agencies are the marks.

Q6 (3) — How has a trend in society influenced design?
  Sustainability trend (environmental driver) -> recyclable/renewable materials, energy efficiency,
  design for repair/recyclability. Issue: cost (greener = pricier) = environmental vs financial. Classify
  driver (SPEEG) + discuss an issue.
```

### Listing 6 — Full mock Section III (Flow Hive) — marked model
```text
Q (15): "Analyse the factors behind a successful innovation + evaluate its impact on society & environment."

INTRO (thesis): Flow Hive succeeded via several innovation-success factors; impact largely positive but mixed.
BODY 1 — SUCCESS (TEMPLE): timing (save-the-bees), emerging tech (3D printing/injection moulding),
  marketing (crowdfunding $12M+), legal (patent before launch). Marketing + timing decisive.
BODY 2 — IMPACT (SIRRI + ISE + LCA): social (public engagement, hobbyists); environmental (bee welfare,
  home food) vs plastic-component footprint; ethical (IP protected).
CONCLUSION (judgement): highly successful; social/environmental benefits outweigh modest costs.

Marks: named TEMPLE factors + specific evidence; balanced impact across society & environment; logical
cohesive structure + clear judgement. (Story-only without frameworks/judgement caps mid-band.)
```
