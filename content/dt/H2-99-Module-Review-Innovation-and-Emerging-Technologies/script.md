QUESTION: Section one style, question one. An innovation launches with a brilliant design and strong technology, but fails commercially because a competitor already dominates the market and consumers see no reason to switch. Which factor most explains the failure? Pause the player, decide, and justify, then play.

NARRATOR: The answer is timing, and to a degree marketing, within the TEMPLE factors. The innovation failed not on its design or technology, which were strong, but because it entered an already-dominated market at the wrong time, the Microsoft Zune against the iPod is the classic case. This is the kind of reasoning Section one tests: recognising that a strong product can still fail on the innovation-success factors. The lesson, success depends on TEMPLE, not just a good design.

QUESTION: Question two, Section two style. Distinguish between creativity and innovation. Two marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Creativity is the generation of new and original ideas, the idea itself, whereas innovation is the successful development and implementation of a creative idea into a real product or solution that is used, the idea delivered. Creativity is necessary for innovation but not sufficient. Two marks: define each and state the distinction, idea versus delivered idea. Adding "necessary but not sufficient" shows depth.

QUESTION: Question three, Section two style. Identify three factors that influence the success of an innovation. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Three factors are timing, available and emerging technologies, and marketing strategies, with political, economic and legal factors and historical-cultural influences also significant, the six remembered as TEMPLE. Three marks for naming three valid factors. Reach for TEMPLE so you don't accidentally list the design factors instead.

QUESTION: Question four, Section two style. Explain how an emerging technology can have both positive and negative effects on society. Four marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer, using artificial intelligence. Artificial intelligence has significant positive and negative social effects. Positively, it can improve efficiency, automate dangerous or tedious work, and enable advances such as faster medical diagnosis, improving lives. Negatively, it raises social and ethical concerns, the displacement of jobs through automation, issues of bias and fairness in automated decisions, and questions of privacy and accountability. Appraised across the social and ethical implications, AI therefore offers real benefits but carries serious risks that must be managed. Four marks: give positive and negative social effects with examples, drawing on the LESEE implications, and reach a balanced view. The two-sided appraisal is the mark. The Section-two model answers are collected in Listing 5.

QUESTION: Question five, Section two style. Explain the influence of entrepreneurial activity on a successful innovation. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Entrepreneurial activity influences success because an entrepreneur takes a creative idea and, bearing the risk, develops it into a viable venture and brings it to market, drawing on agencies such as funders, patent offices and industry. For the Flow Hive, the inventors self-funded development, protected their design with a patent, and used a crowdfunding agency to raise over twelve million dollars and validate demand, turning a clever idea into a global product. Without that entrepreneurial drive and the supporting agencies, the innovation would not have reached the market. Three marks: define entrepreneurial activity, explain its influence with an example, and reference the agencies. The risk-bearing and the agencies are the marks.

QUESTION: Question six, Section two style. Explain how a trend in society has influenced design and production. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. The environmental trend toward sustainability, driven by growing concern for climate and resources, has influenced design and production by pushing designers toward recyclable and renewable materials, energy efficiency and life-cycle thinking, for example designing products for repair and recyclability rather than disposal. This raises the issue of cost, since sustainable choices are often more expensive, the environmental-versus-financial tension. Three marks: name the trend and its driver, explain how it shifts design with an example, and discuss an issue it raises. Classify the driver with SPEEG and discuss an issue, not just name the trend.

QUESTION: Question seven, the big one, Section three style. Analyse the factors that contributed to the success of an innovation you have studied, and evaluate its impact on society and the environment. Fifteen marks. Pause the player and plan and write a full response before you play, the full model is in Listing 6.

NARRATOR: Model answer skeleton, using the Flow Hive. Introduction, thesis: the Flow Hive succeeded through several innovation-success factors and has had a largely positive but mixed impact. Body one, success, run TEMPLE: timing during the save-the-bees movement, emerging technologies, three-D printing and injection moulding, enabling the design, and a record crowdfunding campaign raising over twelve million dollars, with the patent protecting it legally, marketing and timing decisive. Body two, impact, run SIRRI and the three impact levels: socially it engaged the public with sustainable beekeeping and supported hobbyists; environmentally it promotes bee welfare and home food production, weighed against the footprint of its plastic components, assessed on a life cycle analysis; ethically the inventors protected their intellectual property responsibly. Conclusion, judgement: the Flow Hive is a highly successful innovation whose social and environmental benefits outweigh its modest costs. Fifteen marks: analyse success with named TEMPLE factors and specific evidence, evaluate impact across society and environment with balance, and present a logical, cohesive response with a clear judgement. Telling the Flow Hive story without frameworks or judgement caps in the middle bands, the frameworks and the judgement are what reach the top.
