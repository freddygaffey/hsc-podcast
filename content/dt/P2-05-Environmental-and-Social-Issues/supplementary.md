# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the three impact
levels, the seven issues, the key definitions, the worked example and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — The three levels of impact (the answer structure)
```text
Assess ANY design's impact at THREE levels — always split your answer this way:
  INDIVIDUAL  - how it affects the person using it
  SOCIETY     - how it affects the wider community / society
  ENVIRONMENT - how it affects the natural environment

"Assess the impact" -> immediately write three headings: Individual / Society / Environment.
```

### Listing 2 — The seven environmental & social issues (PC SCIE)
| Issue | Meaning | Design relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **P**ersonal values | Differing values from upbringing, lifestyle, culture, experience | Affect whether the target market accepts a design |
| **C**ultural beliefs | Shaped by religion, history, tradition, laws | Sensitivity needed (e.g. sacred Indigenous artwork can be taboo) |
| **S**ustainability | Meet today's needs without compromising the future | Avoid finite resources; LCA; minimise carbon footprint |
| **S**afety & health | Of production workers AND end users | Comply with SafeWork NSW / Standards Australia / WHS |
| **C**ommunity needs | Needs of a group / wider society | Shared facilities, jobs, public spaces |
| **I**ndividual needs | Needs of one person | Comfort, function, affordability (can conflict with community needs) |
| **E**quity | Fairness — equal access for all | Inclusive / universal design (ramps, step-free, accessible) |

### Listing 3 — The two heavyweight definitions (near word-for-word examinable)
```text
SUSTAINABILITY:
  Meeting the needs of people TODAY without negatively affecting the resources available to
  people in the FUTURE.
  -> avoid finite/non-renewable resources; consider disposal; use LIFE CYCLE ANALYSIS to monitor
     impact at every stage; minimise CARBON FOOTPRINT (total greenhouse-gas emissions).
  (Sustainability is the GOAL; life cycle analysis is the TOOL.)

EQUITY:
  Fairness — designing so ALL people (regardless of ability, age, income, background) have fair
  and EQUAL ACCESS to and benefit from a design.
  -> the basis of INCLUSIVE / UNIVERSAL design.
```

### Listing 4 — Worked example: fast fashion across the three levels
| Level | Positives | Negatives |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Affordable, fashionable, self-expression | Throwaway mindset; low-quality garments |
| Society | Provides employment | Poor labour conditions, low wages; equity concerns over who bears the cost |
| Environment | — | Textile waste; high water/energy use; microplastic pollution → fails sustainability |

> Balanced judgement: affordability & choice for individuals, BUT social (labour) and environmental
> (waste/emissions) costs make it hard to justify as responsible design.

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "Assess the impact on individual, society & environment"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low):
  "It's bad for the environment." -> one vague point, one level, no judgement.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - Work ALL THREE levels explicitly: Individual / Society / Environment.
  - Draw in the relevant ISSUES (sustainability, equity, safety, cultural beliefs).
  - Give POSITIVES and negatives.
  - Reach a balanced JUDGEMENT ("assess"/"evaluate" demand a verdict).
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — Define sustainability in design.
  Meeting present needs without negatively affecting future resources; avoid finite resources,
  consider disposal, minimise carbon footprint (via life cycle analysis). (Not just "being green".)

Q2 (3 marks) — Explain equity as a social issue, with an example.
  Fairness — all people get fair/equal access regardless of ability/background; basis of inclusive/
  universal design. E.g. ramps + step-free entrances give wheelchair users equal access.

Q3 (6 marks) — Assess a product's impact on individual, society & environment.
  Fast fashion: Individual = affordable/expressive but disposable/low quality; Society = jobs but
  poor labour conditions + equity concerns; Environment = textile waste, water/energy, microplastics
  (fails sustainability). Balanced verdict: hard to justify as responsible design. Work all 3 levels.

Q4 (3 marks) — How do cultural beliefs influence design?
  Shaped by religion/history/tradition/law; determine what designs are acceptable; need sensitivity.
  E.g. using sacred Aboriginal/Torres Strait Islander artwork without permission may be taboo;
  designers research & respect the cultural context of the target market.

Q5 (3 marks) — How can community needs and individual needs conflict?
  Community needs = a group's needs; individual needs = one person's. They can pull against each
  other. E.g. high-density housing serves the community (affordable housing, land use) but conflicts
  with an individual's privacy/space/sunlight. Designer balances them (acoustic separation, shared
  green space). Design often = trade-offs between group and individual.
```

## Glossary

| Term | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| LCA | Life Cycle Analysis | Evaluation of the resources and impacts across a product's whole life (cradle-to-grave); one of the factors affecting design |
| NSW | New South Wales | The Australian state (e.g. the NSW WHS Act 2011) |
| **PC SCIE** | Personal values, Cultural beliefs, Sustainability, Safety & health, Community needs, Individual needs, Equity | The seven environmental & social issues (P2.2) |
| WHS | Work Health and Safety | Rights & responsibilities for a safe workplace; the WHS Act 2011 sets the framework of duties workplaces must comply with |
