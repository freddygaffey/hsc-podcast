# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the
characteristics/properties distinction, the selection criteria, the materials comparison and
the model answers.

### Listing 1 — Characteristics vs properties
| | Characteristics | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| What they are | Descriptive qualities — what a material is **like** | How a material **behaves / performs** |
| Examples | Appearance, colour, texture, kind of material | Strength, hardness, durability, flexibility, conductivity, workability |

```text
Property categories you can name:
  Physical    - density, melting point
  Mechanical  - strength, hardness, toughness, elasticity/flexibility
  Thermal/electrical - thermal & electrical conductivity, insulation
  Working     - workability/machinability (how easily cut, shaped, joined)

DURABILITY (define precisely): the ability of a product to repeatedly perform its designed
function for an acceptable period WITHOUT failure — i.e. long-lasting under real use.
```

### Listing 2 — Selection criteria: CPA FACE (+ ethics & supply chain)
| Letter | Criterion | Links to factor |
|---|---|---|
| C | Characteristics (what it is) | — |
| P | Properties (how it performs) | Function |
| A | Availability (can you get it; timeline risk) | — |
| F | Function (suits what the product must do) | Function |
| A | Appearance (colour, texture, fit) | Aesthetics |
| C | Cost (material + processes) | Finance |
| E | Environmental (energy, waste, recyclability) | Environmental / LCA |

> Beyond CPA FACE: **ethical supply** (conflict/"blood" materials, black market, child labour)
> and **supply-chain issues** (country of origin, transport distance/logistics).

### Listing 3 — The central principle: match PROPERTIES to FUNCTION
```text
FUNCTION (what the product must do)  ->  decides WHICH PROPERTIES matter  ->  choose a MATERIAL
                                                                              that has them.

Examples:
  must conduct electricity      -> property: conductivity  -> metal (copper), not a polymer
  must be light & waterproof    -> properties: low density, water-resistant -> polymer/aluminium
  must bear heavy load & last   -> properties: high strength, durability -> steel/hardwood

This property-to-function match is the SPINE of every material justification.
```

### Listing 4 — Materials comparison (designing a chair) — approximate/typical values
| Material | Density (approx.) | Key properties | Best use | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timber (pine) | ~500 kg/m³ | Moderate strength, good workability, attractive, renewable | Premium indoor chair | Can warp; not waterproof |
| Polymer (polypropylene) | ~900 kg/m³ | Light, easily moulded (suits mass production), weather-resistant, electrical insulator | Mass-produced outdoor chair | Petroleum-based; not biodegradable |
| Metal (mild steel) | ~7850 kg/m³ | Very strong & durable, conducts heat/electricity | Heavy-duty/industrial frame | Heavy; can corrode; energy-intensive |

> Same product category, three materials — the choice follows the **properties matched to the
> specific function and context** (and the scale: mould polymer for mass; hand-work timber for one-off).

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "Justify the selection of materials"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low):
  "I used pine because it was cheap and available." -> 1-2 criteria, no reasoning, no function link.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - LEAD with properties matched to FUNCTION (e.g. strength + workability suit a hand-assembled stool).
  - Cite EVIDENCE from testing/experimentation.
  - Address SEVERAL criteria (CPA FACE): cost, availability, appearance, environmental.
  - Reach an overall justification.
  Justify = give reasons (ideally tested), not just name the material.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — Distinguish characteristics from properties.
  Characteristics = descriptive qualities (appearance, colour, texture — what it's like).
  Properties = how it behaves/performs (strength, durability, conductivity, workability).

Q2 (2 marks) — Four material-selection criteria.
  Any four of: properties; suitability to function; cost; availability; appearance; environmental
  impact (use CPA FACE). Bonus: ethical supply / supply-chain.

Q3 (5 marks) — Justify a material choice for a product.
  Stainless-steel bottle: LEAD with properties->function (strong/durable for repeated use; corrosion-
  resistant/food-safe; formable into insulating double wall). Then criteria: higher cost & upfront
  environmental impact justified by long life displacing single-use bottles (LCA); premium appearance
  suits market. Defend across criteria, not just name it.

Q4 (3 marks) — Why is experimentation important in selecting materials?
  Tests how a material actually behaves (not just assumptions); gathers evidence about properties &
  suitability for the function; informs & justifies the final selection and reduces risk of failure
  later. (Links to the research & evaluate stages of the design process.)
```

## Glossary

| Term | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **CPA FACE** | Characteristics, Properties, Availability, Function, Appearance, Cost, Environmental | Criteria for selecting a material (P4.2) |
| LCA | Life Cycle Analysis | Evaluation of the resources and impacts across a product's whole life (cradle-to-grave); one of the factors affecting design |
