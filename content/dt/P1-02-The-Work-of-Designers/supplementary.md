# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken in the audio — it reinforces
the designer profiles, contrasts and model answers from the narration.

### Listing 1 — The nature of a designer's work (the skeleton of a "describe" answer)
| Feature | What it means |
|---|---|
| Range of fields | Fashion, industrial, graphic, multimedia, architecture, engineering… |
| Creativity + technology | Designers solve problems by combining both |
| Shaped by influences | Culture, history, environment, personal experience |
| Collaborative | Work with engineers, clients, manufacturers and other designers |
| Process-driven | Apply and adapt the design process (DRIPER) to each problem |

### Listing 2 — Interaction versus overlap of design professions
| | **Interaction** | **Overlap** |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Different professions working **together** on one project | Boundaries between professions **blurring** |
| Why | A project needs many specialised skills | The design process is adaptable to any situation |
| Example | Industrial designer + engineer + ergonomist + marketer on one car | An architect designing furniture as well as buildings |

### Listing 3 — Headline question: "Describe a designer's work and processes"
```text
WEAK answer (capped — biography):
  Lists who the designer is and what products they made. No process, no syllabus language.

STRONG answer (band ~6 — leads with process):
  - Names a REAL designer: field + a real product (e.g. Newson, Qantas Skybed).
  - Describes the NATURE & VARIETY of their work.
  - Describes their PROCESS in design-process language: identify need/opportunity ->
    research -> generate & develop ideas -> prototype -> evaluate & refine -> produce.
  - Notes influences and collaboration.
  - Explains how the adaptable process enables their range.

Say HOW they work, not just WHAT they made. (This is exactly what HSC outcome H1.2 rewards.)
Generic, un-named answers get CAPPED — you must name a real designer with specific detail.
```

### Listing 5 — Career opportunities in design and production
```text
TRADITIONAL pathways:
  Industrial/product designer · Graphic/multimedia designer · Fashion/textile designer
  Architect/interior designer · Engineer/technologist

EMERGING fields (created by new technology):
  UX/UI design · Sustainability consulting · Digital fabrication (3D printing, CNC) · AR/VR design

Two points markers reward:
  - Designers must balance CREATIVITY with practical PRODUCTION skills.
  - Design is a field of LIFELONG LEARNING — careers evolve with new tech & global trends.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (3 marks) — Identify three career opportunities in design and production.
  e.g. industrial/product designer; graphic/multimedia designer; an emerging field such as
  UX designer. (Include at least one emerging field to show the field is evolving.)

Q2 (3 marks) — Explain interaction and overlap of design professions.
  Interaction = professions working together on one project (e.g. designer + engineer +
  ergonomist + marketer on a car). Overlap = boundaries blurring because the design process is
  adaptable (e.g. an architect designing furniture). Distinguish the two.

Q3 (4 marks) — Describe a designer's work and the processes they use.
  Newson: Australian industrial designer; furniture, products, Qantas Skybed. Nature of work =
  flexibility/adaptability; applies the design process as a framework (research unfamiliar
  materials -> generate & develop -> prototype -> evaluate & refine -> produce); the adaptable,
  iterative process enables his range. Lead with process, not products.

Q4 (5 marks) — Analyse collaboration in the design process, referring to two designers.
  Define collaboration (complementary skills + communication -> better outcome). P.E Nation:
  Edwards & Tregoning split design vs marketing by strength. Tin & Ed: coordinated ~20 designers
  on a mural, bringing in the right person when needed. Analyse: pooling specialised skills and
  clear roles produces a better result than working alone. Name TWO real designers.
```
