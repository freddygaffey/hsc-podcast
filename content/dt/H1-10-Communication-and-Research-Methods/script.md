QUESTION: Retrieval. What are the four stages of the research data cycle, and what's the one thing H5.2 requires you to do with your conclusions? Pause the player, answer both, then play.

NARRATOR: Here's the answer. The four stages are collect the data, analyse it, interpret it, and apply the conclusions. And the one thing you must do with your conclusions is apply them, justify and make your design decisions based on the analysis of the research, so the research actually shapes the project and the report. If you got collect-analyse-interpret-apply and "apply the conclusions to justify decisions", that's the mark. Research that's collected but never applied earns little, the apply step is the point.

NARRATOR: Now the headline question: "Justify the research and communication methods used in your major design project." Here's the move. A weak answer says "I did a survey and made a poster", naming methods with no reasons and no audience, capping low. A strong answer matches each method to its purpose and audience, and justifies it: "I used a survey to gather quantitative data on user preferences because I needed measurable evidence from a large sample, and I analysed and applied the results to refine the design; to present to my client, a non-technical audience, I chose a rendered three-dimensional model rather than technical drawings, because it communicated the appearance clearly and was easy for them to interpret." See the difference, the weak answer lists, the strong answer matches method to purpose and audience and justifies from the criteria. The contrast is in Listing 5. Match, apply, justify.

NARRATOR: One trap to flag. Students name research and communication methods without justifying them, "I surveyed people and made a model". H5.2's verb is select and justify, and the syllabus stresses methods appropriate to the target market and decisions justified from analysis of research. So always say why a method suited its purpose and audience, and how the findings were applied. Naming is not justifying, the same lesson as the whole module.

NARRATOR: Now the exam-style questions, real NESA format, get a pen. Four questions, model answers in Listing 6.

QUESTION: Question one. Outline the stages involved in using research data in a design project. Three marks. Pause the player, write your answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Using research data involves collecting the data through appropriate methods such as surveys, interviews or experiments; analysing the data to identify patterns and key findings; interpreting it to determine what it means for the design; and applying the conclusions by using them to inform and justify design decisions. Three marks: cover collect, analyse, interpret and apply. The application step, that the data must change decisions, is what the marker most wants to see.

QUESTION: Question two. Explain how a designer selects an appropriate communication technique for a particular audience. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. A designer selects a communication technique by matching it to the audience, the message and the purpose, and judging it against clarity, appropriateness and ease of interpretation. For a non-technical client who needs to understand a product's appearance, a rendered model or virtual walkthrough is appropriate because it is clear and easy to interpret, whereas for a manufacturer who needs to build the product, precise orthographic drawings are appropriate. The technique that best communicates the message to that specific audience is chosen and justified against the criteria. Three marks: state selection by audience, message and purpose, name the criteria, and give audience-matched examples. The marker wants the method matched to the target market.

QUESTION: Question three. Justify the research and communication methods used in a major design project. Five marks. This is the headline question, so pause the player and write a full answer before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer, using an example MDP. I selected a survey to gather quantitative data on user preferences because I needed measurable evidence from a large sample, and I analysed and applied the results to set my design criteria, and I used material experiments to gather objective performance data, applying the findings to select materials. For communication, I chose techniques suited to each audience: to gain client approval I presented a rendered three-dimensional model, justified because it communicated the appearance clearly and was easy for a non-technical client to interpret, while I produced orthographic drawings for the maker because they precisely specified dimensions. Each method was selected for its purpose and audience, and its findings or message applied to the project, rather than chosen arbitrarily. Five marks: justify research methods by purpose and the application of findings, and communication methods by audience and the criteria, with examples. Listing methods without justification caps low.

QUESTION: Question four. Explain the purpose of the investigative report on an innovation. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. The purpose of the investigative report is to research and critically examine a real innovation, addressing what the innovation is, how it was developed, the factors behind its success, how it has impacted society, and its likely future. It develops the student's research and communication skills, collecting, analysing and presenting information, and deepens their understanding of innovation, which supports both their major design project and the study of innovation and emerging technologies in the HSC course. Three marks: state that it researches and examines a real innovation across those questions, and that it builds research and communication skills and understanding of innovation. This is the bridge to module H2.
