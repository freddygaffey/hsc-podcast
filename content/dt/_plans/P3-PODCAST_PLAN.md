# P3 — Management, Communication & Research podcast plan

**Module:** P3 (Year 11, Preliminary) · **Outcomes:** P3.1, P4.1, P4.3, P5.1, P5.2, P5.3 —
creative & collaborative approaches; project analysis & marketing; evaluation; project
management; communication; research methods.

This module is the **"running a project" toolkit**. P1 taught *what design is*, P2 taught *how
things are made*; P3 teaches the **skills that wrap around a project**: generating ideas
creatively, working in teams, writing a brief, doing market and user research, managing time and
money, communicating solutions, and evaluating against criteria. Almost every one of these is a
*method the student physically does in their folio* — so this module is where vague projects
become disciplined ones, and where the Year-12 MDP (H4/H5) gets its foundations.

**The extended-response problem these episodes fix:** students treat research, management and
evaluation as box-ticking ("I did a survey, I made a timeline"). Markers want *method chosen for
a reason* and *findings acted on*. A strong answer says "qualitative interviews were chosen over a
survey because the need was poorly understood, and the finding that users wanted X redirected the
design" — a method, a justification, and a consequence.

**Target listener:** a Year 11 student writing the research, management and evaluation sections of
a preliminary folio, and about to be handed a group design task.

## Marquee mnemonics coined in this module (record once, reuse — STYLE §5.4)
- **Factors affecting management (P5.1) — "RV GS-CDT D"**: **R**esources (identify available),
  **V**alues (clarify), **G**oals (set), **S**tandards (set), **C**osts/benefits of each
  alternative (evaluate), **D**ecisions (make & implement), **T**asks (describe & sequence),
  **D**ocumentation of plans. Hook: *"Manage with **RV GS-CDT D** — Resources & Values first, plan
  last."*
- **Research methods (P5.3) — "QISOTS"**: **Q**uestionnaires, **I**nterviews, **S**urveys,
  **O**bservation, **T**ests & experiments, **S**tatistical analysis — over the two pillars
  **qualitative vs quantitative.** Hook: *"Quiz with **QISOTS**."*
- **Elements of the communication process — "SMMR"**: **S**ender → **M**essage → **M**edium →
  **R**eceiver. Plus the evaluation criteria: **clarity, appropriateness, ease of interpretation.**
- **Forms of communication — "V-WGVA"**: **V**erbal, **W**ritten, **G**raphical, **V**isual,
  **A**udio.

## Episodes (P3 module)

### P3-01 — Creative Approaches & Cognitive Organisers
- **Theory focus:** generating ideas deliberately — **cognitive organisers** (mind maps,
  flowcharts, matrices), **strategies for problem solving and solution creating** (brainstorming,
  SCAMPER, lateral thinking, morphological analysis), and **ideas generation**. Creativity as a
  *trainable process*, not a gift (sets up H3.2).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Describe a technique used to generate creative design ideas."*
  Weak: "I brainstormed." Strong: names the technique, explains *how* it works to break fixation,
  and shows the ideas it actually produced in a project.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "creative approaches including: cognitive organisers;
  strategies for problem solving and solution creating; ideas generation" + "select and apply a
  variety of cognitive organisers"; "apply problem solving techniques to identified problems."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **SCAMPER** (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to
  other use, Eliminate, Reverse) — already a clean acronym; teach it as the marquee ideation tool.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-03 (the *ideate* stage of DRIPER); forward to H3.2
  (creative & innovative approaches for the MDP) and H3.1 (creativity as a success factor); cash
  in `case_flow_hive` (the "split the honeycomb cell vertically" creative leap).
- **Worked examples / values:** run one design problem through SCAMPER and a mind map, showing the
  ideas each produces. Use the Cambridge cognitive-organiser examples.

### P3-02 — Collaborative Approaches & Design Teams
- **Theory focus:** **design teams — roles and tasks of members, communication between and within
  teams, team responsibilities** — and what makes collaboration succeed. The overlap of design
  professions (back to P1-02) realised as a working team.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Identify the factors that contribute to successful work and
  collaboration in a design team."* Weak: "everyone helps." Strong: names defined roles, clear
  communication channels, shared goals/standards and accountability, with an example of a role and
  its responsibility.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "collaborative approaches — design teams: roles and
  tasks of members; communication between and within design teams; team responsibilities" +
  "collaborate and participate in design teams"; "work cooperatively"; "identify the factors that
  contribute to successful work and collaboration."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** none new (roles taught structurally); reuse **SMMR**
  (communication elements) as the lens for "communication within teams."
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-02 (interaction & overlap of professions); forward to
  H6.1 (collaborative designing and design teams in industrial/commercial practice); cross-link to
  P3-06 (communication) and P3-05 (management of the team).
- **Worked examples / values:** map a real product's design team (e.g. roles on an industrial-
  design project: industrial designer, engineer, ergonomist, marketer) to tasks. Use a textbook
  team example.

### P3-03 — Project Analysis & Design Briefs
- **Theory focus:** **design briefs, appropriateness of design solutions, criteria for
  evaluation and factors to consider** — formulating and analysing a brief, identifying the
  *parameters* of design, and setting **criteria for success**. The brief as the contract the
  whole project is judged against.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain the purpose of a design brief and the criteria for
  success."* Weak: "it says what to make." Strong: defines the brief as the statement of need +
  constraints/parameters, and shows how measurable success criteria are derived from it and used
  in evaluation (forward link to P3-08).
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "project analysis — design briefs; appropriateness of
  design solutions; criteria for evaluation and factors to consider" + "formulate and analyse
  design briefs"; "identify the parameters of design"; "identify criteria for success of design
  projects."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** brief contents hook — a brief states the **NCC**:
  **N**eed, **C**onstraints/parameters, **C**riteria for success.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-04 (appropriateness factor); forward to H4.1 (the MDP
  *proposal* is a brief written for real — need, areas of investigation, criteria to evaluate
  success) and P3-08 (evaluation against these criteria); cross-link to P3-04 (research informs
  the brief).
- **Worked examples / values:** a model design brief for one product with explicit success
  criteria. Use the Cambridge brief template.

### P3-04 — Marketing & Market Research
- **Theory focus:** **purpose of market research, sources of data and information gathering
  techniques, the marketing environment** — identifying needs and opportunities by understanding
  the market and user. Distinguish *market* research (will it sell?) from *user/design* research
  (what does the user need?).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain how market research can identify needs and
  opportunities."* Weak: "ask people what they want." Strong: names primary vs secondary sources,
  a gathering technique, and shows how a finding revealed a gap/opportunity — and warns (via the
  Edsel) that research read *wrongly* causes failure.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "marketing and market research — purpose of market
  research; sources of data and information gathering techniques; marketing environment" +
  "conduct market research to identify needs and opportunities."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** sources hook — **primary** (you gather: surveys,
  interviews, observation) vs **secondary** (already exists: reports, sales data, competitor
  analysis). Reuse **QISOTS** for the gathering techniques.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** forward to P3-05 (research methods in depth) and H3.1
  (marketing strategies as a TEMPLE success factor); cash in `case_ford_edsel` (market research
  misread → $250M flop) and `case_flow_hive` (crowdfunding *as* market validation).
- **Worked examples / values:** the Edsel as the cautionary worked example (research said "buyers
  want choice"; Ford built the wrong choice). Pull detail from `_plans/CASE-PODCAST_PLAN.md`.

### P3-05 — Research Methods, Data & Ethics
- **Theory focus:** **qualitative and quantitative research; questionnaires, surveys, interviews,
  observation, tests and experiments; statistical analysis; information research (print &
  electronic)**; plus **interpreting and presenting data** and **ethics in research.** Choosing
  the method to suit the question, and acting on the findings.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Outline how testing and experimenting can improve a design
  project."* (real 2022 HSC). Weak: "you test it to see if it works." Strong: frames testing as a
  *method that produces data*, which is interpreted against criteria and *fed back* into the
  design (DRIPER loop) — with an ethics note where human participants are involved.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "research methods — qualitative and quantitative
  research; questionnaires; surveys; interviews; observation; tests and experiments; statistical
  analysis; information research including print and electronic sources"; "interpreting and
  presenting data; ethics in research" + "select and use a variety of research methods to inform
  the generation, modification, and development of design ideas"; "analyse, interpret and apply
  research data to the development of design projects."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **QISOTS** (coined above) over the qual/quant split;
  small hook for ethics — **"CVA"**: informed **C**onsent, **V**oluntary participation,
  **A**nonymity/privacy.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-03 (research stage of DRIPER) and P3-04 (market
  research is one application); forward to H4.1/H5.2 (research methods for the MDP and the
  innovation case study); cross-link to P2-06 (electronic/digital research tools).
- **Worked examples / values:** match three research questions to the right method (e.g.
  "how strong is this joint?" → test; "do users find it comfortable?" → interview/observation;
  "how big is the market?" → survey/secondary data) and show a simple data-presentation choice
  (table vs graph). Use textbook research-methods content.

### P3-06 — Communication & Presentation
- **Theory focus:** **forms of communication (verbal, written, graphical, visual, audio); the
  elements of the communication process (sender, receiver, medium, message); criteria for
  evaluating communication (clarity, appropriateness, ease of interpretation); communicating
  through media; visualising solutions; the purpose of prototypes and/or models; presentation
  techniques** suited to clients and projects.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Describe how technology can assist designers in overcoming
  communication barriers."* (real 2022 HSC). Weak: "they email." Strong: uses the **SMMR** model
  to locate the *barrier* (noise in the medium, mismatch to receiver) and names technologies/forms
  that fix it, judged against the three criteria.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "communication — forms of communication including
  verbal, written, graphical, visual, audio; elements of the communication process which include
  sender, receiver, medium, message; criteria for evaluating communication including clarity of
  message, appropriateness of method chosen, ease of interpretation; … the purpose of prototypes
  and/or models; presentation techniques suited to the needs of design clients and design
  projects" + "experiment with a range of techniques and forms to visualise and communicate ideas";
  "justify the selection and use of communication techniques."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **SMMR** (process elements) and **V-WGVA** (forms),
  both coined above; criteria hook — **"CAE"**: Clarity, Appropriateness, Ease of interpretation.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P2-06 (computer-based communication & presentation);
  forward to H5.2 (communication techniques for the MDP, matched to the target market); cross-link
  to P3-02 (communication within teams) and P3-03 (presenting the brief).
- **Worked examples / values:** the same idea communicated four ways (sketch / orthographic
  drawing / rendered model / written spec) and which suits which audience. Note the role of a
  prototype/model as communication, not just testing. Use textbook drawing-convention examples.

### P3-07 — Project Management
- **Theory focus:** **management techniques and tools** (including those used in industrial/
  commercial settings) and the **factors affecting management** — identifying resources,
  clarifying values, setting goals and standards, evaluating costs/benefits, making and
  implementing decisions, task descriptions and sequencing, documentation. **Gantt charts**,
  action/time/finance planning (direct seed for H5.1).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain how management tools are used to plan and monitor a design
  project."* Weak: "I made a timeline." Strong: names a tool (Gantt/critical path), shows how it
  sequences dependent tasks, sets standards and tracks against a schedule and budget, and how it's
  *revised* when reality bites.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "project management — management techniques and tools,
  including those used by designers in industrial and commercial settings"; "factors affecting
  management including: identifying available resources; clarifying values; setting goals; setting
  standards; evaluating the costs and benefits of each alternative; making and implementing
  decisions; task descriptions and sequencing; documentation of plans" + "plan, implement and
  evaluate a sequence of operations for the completion of design projects."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **RV GS-CDT D** (factors affecting management, coined
  above) — the marquee list of this episode.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P2-01 (bigger settings need more management); forward to
  H5.1 (managing action, time & finance for the MDP — the same skill, examined) and H3.1
  (management & entrepreneurial activity); cross-link to P3-03 (the brief sets the goals being
  managed to).
- **Worked examples / values:** a worked Gantt chart for a short project with dependent tasks and
  a critical path; a simple finance plan (budget vs actual). Use the Cambridge management-tools
  example.

### P3-08 — Evaluation
- **Theory focus:** **developing and refining ideas; criteria for evaluation; methods of
  evaluation** — and that evaluation is **continual**, throughout design and production, not just
  at the end. Evaluate against the success criteria set in the brief (P3-03), and assess impact on
  society and environment (link to P2-05).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain why evaluation should be ongoing throughout a design
  project."* Weak: "to check it works at the end." Strong: argues that continual evaluation against
  criteria catches problems early (cheaper to fix), drives the DRIPER refine-loop, and produces the
  evidence trail markers reward — with an example of a mid-project evaluation changing a decision.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "evaluation — developing and refining ideas; criteria
  for evaluation; methods of evaluation" + "establish the parameters for design and identify
  criteria for the evaluation of design projects"; "conduct continual evaluation throughout design
  and production"; "test and evaluate the appropriateness of design solutions."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** evaluation timing hook — **"BMA"**: **B**efore (set
  criteria), **M**id (continual checks), **A**fter (final judgement against criteria).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P3-03 (criteria come from the brief) and P3-05 (testing
  produces the evaluation data); forward to H4.3 (evaluating the MDP and its impact) and H1.1
  (success vs failure judged by evaluation); cash in `case_central_park_sydney` (evaluated as a
  success on paper, under-delivered in use — the gap between predicted and actual).
- **Worked examples / values:** a before/mid/after evaluation of one project against three success
  criteria, showing a mid-project criterion failing and the redesign it triggered.

### P3-99 — Module Review: Management, Communication & Research
- **Theory focus:** assemble the project toolkit around DRIPER — **ideate (SCAMPER) → research
  (QISOTS) → brief (NCC) → manage (RV GS-CDT D) → communicate (SMMR / V-WGVA) → evaluate (BMA).**
  Re-surface every term and mnemonic in one pass (STYLE §5.3). This module has the most lists, so
  the review leans hardest on hooks.
- **Extended-response prep:** an integrated, multi-topic HSC-style question, e.g. *"For a design
  project you have studied, analyse how research, management and evaluation worked together to
  improve the outcome."* Model a band-6 answer threading method + justification + consequence.
- **Syllabus dot-points served:** all of P3.1/P4.1/P4.3/P5.1/P5.2/P5.3 (revisited).
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** full recap of **SCAMPER, QISOTS, NCC, RV GS-CDT D,
  SMMR, V-WGVA, CAE, BMA.**
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** full-scale interleave to H4/H5 (the MDP runs this whole toolkit
  for real) and H5.2 (research + communication for the case study). Flag the trap: confusing
  *market research* (the market/commercial viability) with *design/user research* (the user's
  need) — both are "research," different questions.
- **Worked examples / values:** quick-fire recap of the module's worked examples + 4–5 exam-style
  questions spanning ideation, research, management, communication and evaluation.
