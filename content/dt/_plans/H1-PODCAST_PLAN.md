# H1 — Designing & Producing (Major Design Project) podcast plan

**Module:** H1 (Year 12, HSC) · **Outcomes:** H1.1, H1.2, H4.1, H4.2, H4.3, H5.1, H5.2, H6.1 —
the factors affecting design and success/failure; the work of designers; identifying the need &
project proposal; selecting & using resources safely; managing action/time/finance; research &
communication; industrial & commercial practices; evaluating the major design project.

This is the **engine room of the HSC course**: the **Major Design Project (MDP)** is 60% of the
HSC mark (40% project + 20% folio in the school-based component) and these outcomes are examined
in **Section II** of the written paper. Every Year-11 skill now returns "for real and at stake."
The job of this module is not to teach new theory so much as to **run the Preliminary toolkit at
HSC depth on one real project** — needs analysis, factors, designers' practice, research,
resources & safety, management, communication, and evaluation — and to teach the student to
*write about their own project in mark-earning language.*

**The extended-response problem these episodes fix:** in Section II students describe *what they
did* ("then I cut the timber") instead of *justifying decisions against criteria* ("timber was
selected over polymer against the factors of finance and environmental consequence, confirmed by
the test in my folio"). The MDP marking guidelines reward *justification, evidence and
evaluation*. This module trains every answer to read: decision → reason → factor → evidence.

**Target listener:** a Year 12 student in the thick of their MDP and folio, who will be examined
on the *process* of designing and producing it.

## Marquee mnemonics (reuse Year-11 wording identically — STYLE §5.4)
- **The 11 factors affecting design — "FFA EQ NEW LOAd"** — *the same list and wording from P1 and
  the CASE plan.* Appropriateness, Needs, Function, Aesthetics, Finance, Ergonomics, WHS, Quality,
  Short- & long-term environmental consequences, Obsolescence, Life cycle analysis. In H1.1 these
  become the lens for **success and failure.**
- **The design process — "DRIPER"** (from P1-03): Define → Research → Ideate → Produce → Evaluate
  → Refine. The MDP *is* DRIPER run end-to-end.
- **Management plans — "ATF"**: **A**ction, **T**ime, **F**inance — the three plans H5.1 names
  explicitly. Hook: *"manage the MDP with ATF — what, when, how much."* (Builds on P3-07's
  RV GS-CDT D.)
- **Project proposal contents (H4.1) — "NICe"**: identification & exploration of the **N**eed,
  areas of **I**nvestigation, **C**riteria to evaluate success. (Builds on P3-03's NCC brief.)

## Episodes (H1 module)

### H1-01 — The Major Design Project: What It Is & How It's Marked
- **Theory focus:** orientation — what the MDP is, the folio's role, the components and *how marks
  are awarded* (project + folio; process evidence, not just the artefact). Map the MDP onto DRIPER
  so the student sees the whole journey before starting. Set the standard: *evidence of process
  beats a pretty outcome.*
- **Extended-response prep:** not a content question — instead, teach the *meta-skill*: how
  Section II / folio marking rewards justification + evidence + evaluation. Contrast a folio page
  that *narrates* ("I did X") with one that *justifies* ("X was chosen because… as shown by…").
- **Syllabus dot-points served:** course-structure HSC "Designing and Producing — project proposal
  & management; project development & realisation; project evaluation" (the MDP spine).
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** plant **DRIPER**, **ATF**, **NICe** as the module's map.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-03 (DRIPER) and the whole of P3 (the toolkit now runs
  for real); forward to every other H1 episode.
- **Worked examples / values:** a model folio contents list mapped to DRIPER stages; one
  "narrate vs justify" rewrite as the template for the module.

### H1-02 — Identifying the Need & the Project Proposal
- **Theory focus:** **needs analysis; researching and developing ideas; identifying
  opportunities; formulating an individual design proposal.** Turning a vague interest into a
  defensible proposal: the **need**, the **areas of investigation**, and the **criteria to evaluate
  success** (NICe). The proposal is the contract the project is marked against.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain how a need or opportunity was identified and developed into
  a project proposal."* Weak: "I wanted to make a lamp." Strong: shows the need *analysed* (who,
  what gap, evidence), the opportunity justified, and measurable success criteria derived from it.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "needs analysis; researching and developing ideas;
  identifying opportunities; formulating an individual design proposal" + "develop a major design
  project proposal that clearly outlines: identification and exploration of the need; areas of
  investigation; criteria to evaluate success."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **NICe** (proposal contents, coined above), built on
  P3-03's **NCC** brief.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P3-03 (design brief) and P3-04 (market research finds the
  need); forward to H1-10 (the same criteria are used to evaluate at the end — close the loop);
  forward to H4.1 research/experimentation (next episode).
- **Worked examples / values:** a worked MDP proposal (need + areas of investigation + 3–4 success
  criteria) for a sample project. Use the HSC course-structure wording as the template.

### H1-03 — Factors Affecting Design, & Success and Failure
- **Theory focus:** the **eleven factors (FFA EQ NEW LOAd)** applied to the MDP, and the H1.1
  headline skill — **critically analysing examples of success and failure in design.** What
  separates a design that succeeds from one that fails, read through the factors. This is the most
  examined idea in the subject.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Critically analyse an example of success and an example of failure
  in design."* Weak: "the iPhone was good, the Edsel was bad." Strong: judges each against *named
  factors* (the Edsel failed on *needs/market read*; the Newton on *timing/technological
  obsolescence*), reaching a reasoned verdict — exactly the case-study material.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "factors affecting designing and producing, including:
  appropriateness… needs… function… aesthetics… finance… ergonomics… work health and safety…
  quality… short-term and long-term environmental consequences… obsolescence… life cycle analysis";
  "examples of success and failure in design" + "apply factors affecting design to the development
  of the major design project"; "critically analyse examples of success and failure in design
  solutions."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **FFA EQ NEW LOAd** — reused *verbatim* from P1; "write
  all eleven down the second the exam starts."
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-04/05 (the factors, introduced); cash in **all five
  case studies** here — `case_ford_edsel`, `case_apple_newton`, `case_microsoft_zune`,
  `case_central_park_sydney` (failures) and `case_flow_hive` (success); forward to H3.1 (factors of
  *innovation* success, TEMPLE) and H2.2 (impact).
- **Worked examples / values:** a factor-by-factor success/failure table for two products (one
  success, one failure) drawn from the failed-design notes. Reuse the CASE plan's factor wording.

### H1-04 — The Work of Designers (and Emulating Their Practice)
- **Theory focus:** **the work of designers — design practice; processes used by designers** — and
  the H1.2 skill: *emulate, where appropriate, the practices and processes used by designers* in
  your own MDP. Studying how real designers research, prototype and iterate, then borrowing that
  discipline.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Relate the practices and processes of a designer you have studied
  to your major design project."* Weak: biography of the designer. Strong: identifies a specific
  *practice* (e.g. iterative prototyping, user-testing, sustainable material sourcing) and shows it
  *adopted* in the student's own process, with evidence.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "the work of designers — design practice; processes
  used by designers" + "emulate, where appropriate, the practices and processes used by designers
  to assist in the development of the major design project."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** none new — frame the designer's method as a DRIPER
  variant so it slots into the student's existing model.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-02 (Year-11 study of designers — now applied);
  forward to H3.2 (creative & innovative practice) and H2.1 (designers as agents of trends); cash
  in `case_flow_hive` (the Anderson father-and-son's decade of prototyping as a model practice).
- **Worked examples / values:** one designer's documented process broken into stages the student
  can copy, with a concrete "how I emulated this in my MDP" example.

### H1-05 — Research & Experimentation for the MDP
- **Theory focus:** **research and methods of experimentation to generate ideas** — applying P3-05's
  research toolkit (QISOTS) at HSC depth to the real project, and *responding to the findings*.
  Experimenting with materials, tools and technologies as a way of *generating and testing* ideas,
  not just confirming them.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Describe how research and experimentation informed the development
  of your major design project."* Weak: "I researched online." Strong: names methods, shows a
  specific finding, and shows the design *changed* in response (the DRIPER refine-loop), with folio
  evidence.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "research and methods of experimentation to generate
  ideas" + "respond to the findings of experimentation and research"; "experiment with materials,
  tools and technologies when designing."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** reuse **QISOTS** (research methods) and **DRIPER**
  (research → ideate → refine).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P3-05 (research methods & ethics) and P2-03
  (material experimentation/testing); forward to H5.2 (research methods for MDP *and* case study)
  and H4.2 (results of research justify resource choices).
- **Worked examples / values:** a worked material/joint experiment (variables, method, result,
  decision) as the model folio "experimentation" page.

### H1-06 — Mid-Module Review: Proposal to Research (STYLE §5.3)
- **Theory focus:** tie the front half together — **proposal (NICe) → factors & success/failure
  (FFA EQ NEW LOAd) → designers' practice → research & experimentation** — all feeding the MDP.
  Re-surface the terms and the "decision → reason → factor → evidence" writing pattern.
- **Extended-response prep:** an integrated Section-II-style question spanning proposal + factors +
  research, e.g. *"Explain how identifying the need, applying the factors affecting design, and
  researching ideas shaped the early development of a design project."* Model an integrated answer.
- **Syllabus dot-points served:** H4.1, H1.1, H1.2 (revisited).
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** recap **NICe, FFA EQ NEW LOAd, DRIPER, QISOTS.**
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** flag the trap: confusing the *factors affecting design* (H1.1)
  with the *factors affecting innovation success* (H3.1, TEMPLE) — different lists, both examined.
- **Worked examples / values:** quick-fire recap of the front-half worked examples + 3–4 exam
  questions.

### H1-07 — Selecting & Using Resources Responsibly and Safely
- **Theory focus:** **factors to be considered when selecting resources — safety, ethical issues,
  environmental issues** — and the H4.2 skill: identify, select, use and *justify* resources from
  the *results and analysis of research*. Safe working practices and environments (the principles,
  not just the PPE).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Justify the selection and safe use of resources in your major
  design project."* Weak: "I used MDF because it was available." Strong: justifies against safety +
  ethical + environmental factors *and* research evidence, and explains the safe-work principles
  applied (back to P2-04's hierarchy of control).
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "factors to be considered when selecting resources
  including: safety; ethical issues; environmental issues" + "explain the principles underlying
  safe working practices and environments"; "identify, select, use and justify the use of resources
  based on the results and analysis of research."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** resource-selection hook — **"SEE"**: **S**afety,
  **E**thical, **E**nvironmental; reuse **ES-EAP** (hierarchy of control) from P2-04 and **CPA
  FACE** from P2-03.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P2-03 (material selection) and P2-04 (WHS); forward to
  H6.1 (safe work practices using selected resources in industrial/commercial practice) and H2.2
  (ethical & environmental issues at innovation scale).
- **Worked examples / values:** a resource-selection justification for one MDP material, scored
  against SEE with a research finding cited. Reference WHS principles from the textbook.

### H1-08 — Industrial & Commercial Practices
- **Theory focus:** **practices in industrial and commercial settings as they relate to the MDP —
  safe work practices using selected resources; production techniques; selection of processes
  appropriate to a need or opportunity; collaborative designing and design teams.** Relating
  professional practice (P2 settings/scales) to the student's own production.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Relate a production technique used in industrial settings to your
  major design project."* Weak: "factories use machines, I used a drill." Strong: names a real
  industrial/commercial practice (jig-based repeatability, quality control, lean production) and
  shows how the student *adapted its principle* to their own production, justified.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "practices in industrial and commercial settings as
  they relate to the major design project including: safe work practices using selected resources;
  production techniques; selection of processes appropriate to an identified need or opportunity;
  collaborative designing and design teams" + "identify design and production processes used in
  domestic, community, industrial and commercial settings"; "implement safe work practices using
  selected materials and techniques."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** reuse **DCIC** (settings) and **JBM** (scales) from P2;
  practices hook — **"SPPC"**: **S**afe work, **P**roduction techniques, **P**rocess selection,
  **C**ollaborative teams.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P2-01/02 (settings & scales) and P3-02 (design teams);
  forward to H6.2 (emerging tech changing industrial practice) and H2.1 (trends like automation);
  cash in `case_flow_hive` (injection moulding / scaling to manufacture).
- **Worked examples / values:** map one industrial practice (e.g. batch jigs, CNC repeatability,
  QC sampling) to a step in the student's MDP. Use a textbook industrial-practice example.

### H1-09 — Managing the Project: Action, Time & Finance
- **Theory focus:** **methods of managing action, time and finance appropriate to the project;
  documentation procedures for management plans** — the H5.1 skill: *formulate, apply and evaluate*
  management plans (ATF) and manage a quality MDP that meets the need. Gantt charts, critical path,
  budget tracking, at HSC stakes.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain how management plans were used to ensure the successful
  completion of a major design project."* Weak: "I made a timeline." Strong: shows an **A**ction +
  **T**ime + **F**inance plan, how it was *monitored and revised* when a task slipped, and how it
  protected quality and the deadline.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "project management — including methods of managing
  action, time and finance appropriate to the nature of individual design projects; documentation
  procedures for developing management plans" + "formulate management plans, including: action;
  time; finance"; "apply and evaluate management plans"; "manage a quality major design project."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **ATF** (coined above) — Action, Time, Finance; reuse
  **RV GS-CDT D** (factors affecting management) from P3-07.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P3-07 (project management — the skill); forward to H3.1
  (management & entrepreneurial activity); cross-link to H1-01 (the folio documents these plans).
- **Worked examples / values:** a worked MDP Gantt + finance plan with a slipped task and the
  re-plan it forced. Use the Cambridge management example.

### H1-10 — Communication & Research Methods for the MDP and Case Study
- **Theory focus:** **research methods — data collection, analysis, interpretation, application of
  conclusions; communication — presenting information, visualising solutions, methods appropriate
  to the target market.** The H5.2 skill: select appropriate research *and* communication for *both*
  the MDP and the innovation case study, and *justify* decisions from analysis of research.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Justify the research and communication methods used in your major
  design project."* Weak: "I made a poster and a survey." Strong: matches method to purpose and
  audience (SMMR / target market), shows conclusions *applied*, and justifies each choice from the
  research — and notes this same skill produces the case-study report.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "research methods — data collection, analysis,
  interpretation and application of conclusions"; "communication — presenting information;
  visualising solutions; communication and presentation methods appropriate to the target market" +
  "conduct research to examine the success of an innovation and produce an investigative report";
  "select and use appropriate communication techniques for the development of the major design
  project."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** reuse **QISOTS** (research), **SMMR / V-WGVA / CAE**
  (communication) from P3.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P3-05/06 (research & communication); forward to H2 / the
  CASE module (the investigative report on an innovation *is* H5.2 applied — bridge to H2-08);
  cross-link to H1-05 (research generating ideas).
- **Worked examples / values:** a method-to-purpose-to-audience matrix for the MDP; note how the
  same research discipline produces the case-study investigative report.

### H1-11 — Evaluating the Major Design Project
- **Theory focus:** **ongoing evaluation; criteria to evaluate success; analysing criteria;
  implementation of design solutions** — and the **impact of the MDP on the individual, society and
  the environment (local and global), and its social/environmental costs and benefits.** The H4.3
  skill: continual evaluation against the success criteria set in H1-02, and a final judgement of
  the project *and* its impact.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Evaluate your major design project against its criteria for success
  and assess its impact on society and the environment."* Weak: "it worked and people liked it."
  Strong: judges the artefact against each named success criterion (from the proposal), then assesses
  impact across individual/society/environment with costs *and* benefits — two higher-order verbs
  answered.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "project evaluation — ongoing evaluation; criteria to
  evaluate success; analysing criteria for evaluation; implementation of design solutions"; "the
  impact of the major design project — on the individual; on society; on the environment (local and
  global); in relation to potential social or environmental costs or benefits" + "test possible
  solutions"; "conduct continual evaluation"; "evaluate the impact… on the individual, society and
  the environment"; "evaluate the major design project in terms of the identified criteria for
  success."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** reuse **BMA** (before/mid/after evaluation, from P3-08)
  and the three impact levels — **"ISE"**: Individual, Society, Environment.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to H1-02 (the criteria being evaluated against — closes the
  loop) and P3-08 (evaluation as a skill); forward to H2.2 (evaluating impact at innovation scale);
  cash in `case_central_park_sydney` (predicted vs actual performance — the impact-evaluation gap).
- **Worked examples / values:** a criterion-by-criterion evaluation table for a sample MDP plus an
  ISE impact assessment with one cost and one benefit at each level.

### H1-99 — Module Review: Designing & Producing
- **Theory focus:** run the whole MDP journey as one arc — **propose (NICe) → analyse via factors &
  success/failure (FFA EQ NEW LOAd) → emulate designers → research & experiment → select resources
  safely (SEE) → relate industrial practice (SPPC) → manage (ATF) → communicate → evaluate (BMA,
  ISE).** Re-surface every term and the "decision → reason → factor → evidence" pattern (STYLE §5.3).
- **Extended-response prep:** a full Section-II-style sweep — several short questions spanning the
  module the way the real paper does, each with a model answer; plus one integrated longer answer.
- **Syllabus dot-points served:** all of H1.1/H1.2/H4.1/H4.2/H4.3/H5.1/H5.2/H6.1 (revisited).
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** full recap of **NICe, FFA EQ NEW LOAd, DRIPER, SEE,
  SPPC, ATF, BMA, ISE.**
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** full-scale interleave to H2 (innovation & emerging tech — the
  other half of the HSC) and the CASE module (the case study runs alongside the MDP). Flag the two
  most common Section II traps: narrating instead of justifying, and confusing the design-factors
  list (H1.1) with the innovation-success list (H3.1).
- **Worked examples / values:** quick-fire recap of the module's worked examples + 5 exam-style
  Section II questions with model answers.
