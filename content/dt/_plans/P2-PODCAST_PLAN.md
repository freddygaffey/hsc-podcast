# P2 — Design Processes & Production podcast plan

**Module:** P2 (Year 11, Preliminary) · **Outcomes:** P2.1, P2.2, P4.2, P6.1, P6.2 —
design & production processes across settings; the impact of design activity on the individual,
society and environment; materials/tools/resources & safety; manufacturing & production;
computer-based technologies.

If P1 was *how a designer thinks*, P2 is *how things actually get made*. It moves the student
from sketching to producing: where production happens (domestic → community → industrial →
commercial), how scale changes everything (one-off vs batch vs mass), how materials and tools are
chosen and used **safely**, the environmental and social consequences of all of it, and how
computers thread through the whole pipeline. This is the most "workshop" of the preliminary
modules and the one that earns marks on the Section II short-answer questions about production,
materials and safety.

**The extended-response problem these episodes fix:** students describe production vaguely
("they make it in a factory"). Markers want the *correct setting and scale named* and the
*consequences justified*. A strong answer says "this moved from batch production in a community
setting to mass production in an industrial setting, which improved consistency and unit cost but
increased tooling cost and environmental load" — setting + scale + trade-off, every time.

**Target listener:** a Year 11 student mid-way through their first design project, choosing
materials and a production method and writing the "production" and "impact" pages of the folio.

## Marquee mnemonics coined in this module (record once, reuse — STYLE §5.4)
- **The four production settings — "DCIC"**: **D**omestic → **C**ommunity → **I**ndustrial →
  **C**ommercial. Teach as a ladder of *scale and formality*, not random categories.
- **The three scales of production — "JBM"**: **J**ob (one-off / custom) → **B**atch → **M**ass
  (continuous). Hook: *"one, some, millions."* Drill which suits which need (bespoke vs volume).
- **Environmental & social issues (P2.2) — "PC SCIE"**: **P**ersonal values, **C**ultural
  beliefs, **S**ustainability, **S**afety & health, **C**ommunity needs, **I**ndividual needs,
  **E**quity. Hook: *"design is **PC** — it has to respect everyone."*
- **Criteria for selecting a material — "CPA FACE"**: **C**haracteristics, **P**roperties,
  **A**vailability, **F**unction/use, **A**ppearance, **C**ost, **E**nvironmental consequence.
  (Aligns with the FFA EQ NEW LOAd factors from P1 — call that back.)

## Episodes (P2 module)

### P2-01 — Design & Production Across Settings
- **Theory focus:** the four settings — **domestic, community, industrial, commercial** (DCIC) —
  and how the *same* design process (DRIPER, from P1-03) plays out differently at each scale.
  Define each setting with a concrete example and contrast resources, formality and volume.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Compare design and production processes used in two different
  settings."* Weak: "factories are bigger than homes." Strong: names the two settings, contrasts
  them on scale, division of labour, equipment, documentation and quality control, with an
  example for each.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "design and production processes in domestic,
  community, industrial and commercial settings" + "compare and contrast technologies and
  processes used in design projects to activities of design and production in industrial and
  commercial settings."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **DCIC** (coined above).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-03 (DRIPER scales across settings); forward to P2-03
  (scales of production map onto settings) and H6.1 (industrial & commercial practices for the
  MDP); cross-link to P3 project management (bigger settings need more management).
- **Worked examples / values:** one product made three ways — handmade at home, batch-made by a
  small maker/community workshop, mass-made industrially — naming the real differences. Use a
  Cambridge textbook production example.

### P2-02 — Manufacturing & Production Processes
- **Theory focus:** the **three scales — Job, Batch, Mass (JBM)** — and *selecting the process
  appropriate to a need*. Define each, give the volume/cost/flexibility trade-off, and introduce
  core process families (forming, cutting/shaping, joining, finishing) plus quality production
  skills.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain how the trend towards mass production may affect workplace
  practices."* (real 2022 HSC). Weak: "machines replace people." Strong: links mass production to
  division of labour, deskilling/reskilling, automation, consistency and WHS, with a judgement on
  the net effect on workers.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "manufacturing and production — selection of
  processes appropriate to a need; development of appropriate skills and techniques" + "account
  for practices undertaken in industrial and commercial settings"; "demonstrate quality
  production skills in the development of design projects."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **JBM** (coined above) — "one, some, millions."
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P2-01 (settings); forward to H6.1 (justifying
  production techniques in the MDP) and H2.1 (mass production as a *social/economic trend*); cash
  in `case_microsoft_zune` / `case_ford_edsel` lightly (mass-producing the wrong product is still
  a failure).
- **Worked examples / values:** match three real products to job/batch/mass and justify each
  (a custom prosthetic = job; a furniture run = batch; drink bottles = mass). Pull process detail
  from the textbook.

### P2-03 — Materials, Tools, Techniques & Resources
- **Theory focus:** how resources are chosen and used — **characteristics and properties,
  functions and uses, experimentation, criteria for selection, consequences of use.** Teach the
  difference between a *characteristic* (what it is) and a *property* (how it behaves — strength,
  durability, conductivity, workability) and how experimentation/testing informs selection.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Justify the selection of materials for a design project you have
  studied."* Weak: "I used pine because it's cheap." Strong: applies selection criteria
  (CPA FACE), cites *properties* against the *function*, references testing/experimentation, and
  weighs the *consequences of use* (including environmental).
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "using materials, tools, techniques and other
  resources — characteristics and properties; functions and uses; experimentation; criteria for
  selection; consequences of use"; "the realisation of ideas through the manipulation of
  materials, tools and techniques and other resources" + "select appropriate materials…";
  "justify and explain the selection and use of resources in design projects."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **CPA FACE** (selection criteria, coined above).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-04/05 (factors: finance, function, environmental);
  forward to H4.2 (selecting & using resources responsibly and safely for the MDP); forward to
  P2-05 (safe use of those tools/materials).
- **Worked examples / values:** a properties table for 2–3 common materials (timber vs polymer vs
  metal) with real values for one property each (e.g. relative density, workability), taken from
  the Cambridge textbook material charts.

### P2-04 — Safety & Work Health and Safety
- **Theory focus:** **safe work practices** when designing and producing — safety in the use of
  materials/tools/techniques and the **legislative requirements including WHS**. Hierarchy of
  control, risk assessment, PPE, and *why* WHS is a designed-in factor (not an afterthought).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain the principles underlying safe working practices and
  environments."* (mirrors H4.2/H6.1 wording). Weak: "wear goggles." Strong: states the duty of
  care under WHS law, walks a risk-assessment process, applies the hierarchy of control to a real
  workshop hazard, and links safety back to the *WHS factor* from P1.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "safety — safety in the use of materials, tools and
  techniques; legislative requirements including work health and safety" + "implement safe work
  practices when designing and producing."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** the **hierarchy of control — "ES-EAP"**: Eliminate,
  Substitute, Engineering controls, Administrative controls, PPE (in order of effectiveness —
  PPE is the *last* resort, a classic exam point).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-05 (WHS is one of the eleven factors); forward to
  H4.2 and H6.1 (safe work practices in the MDP — same wording recurs); cross-link to P2-03
  (consequences of using a material safely).
- **Worked examples / values:** a worked risk assessment for one workshop process (e.g. using a
  bandsaw or a soldering iron): hazard → risk → control via ES-EAP. Reference WHS Act duty-of-care
  language from the textbook.

### P2-05 — Environmental & Social Issues
- **Theory focus:** the impact of design and technology activity on **the individual, society and
  the environment** — **personal values, cultural beliefs, sustainability, safety & health,
  community needs, individual needs, equity** (PC SCIE). Define sustainability and equity
  precisely; connect to life-cycle thinking from P1-05.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Assess the impact of a design project on the individual, society
  and the environment."* / 2022's *"Discuss the long-term environmental impacts…"* Weak: "it's bad
  for the environment." Strong: separates the three impact levels (individual / society /
  environment), uses sustainability + equity as lenses, and reaches a *judgement* (assess/discuss
  are higher-order verbs).
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "environmental and social issues including: personal
  values; cultural beliefs; sustainability; safety and health; community needs; individual needs;
  equity" + "assess the impact of the activities undertaken in the development of design projects
  on the individual, society and the environment"; "evaluate examples of design and production and
  relate these to environmental and social issues."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **PC SCIE** (coined above) — "design is PC."
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P1-05 (environmental consequences, LCA); forward to
  H2.2 (evaluates the impact of design and innovation on society & environment — the HSC version)
  and H6.2 (impact of emerging technologies); cash in `case_central_park_sydney` (green claims vs
  real impact) and `case_flow_hive` (sustainability as a *selling point*).
- **Worked examples / values:** one product evaluated across all three impact levels (e.g. fast
  fashion: individual cost, social labour conditions, environmental waste). Anchor with textbook
  sustainability content.

### P2-06 — Computer-Based Technologies
- **Theory focus:** computer-based technologies and their application — **modelling, research,
  simulation and graphics, communication, presentation** — and *discriminating* in their choice
  (the right tool for the job, not "use a computer for everything"). CAD/CAM/CAE, 3D printing,
  rapid prototyping as the bridge to H6.2 emerging tech.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Describe how technology can assist designers in overcoming
  communication barriers."* (real 2022 HSC). Weak: "they use computers." Strong: names *specific*
  technologies against *specific* tasks (CAD for visualising, simulation for testing without a
  physical prototype, shared digital folios for collaboration) and judges their appropriateness.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "computer-based technologies and their application
  including: modelling; research; simulation and graphics; communication; presentation" +
  "discriminate in the choice and use of computer-based technologies to develop, communicate and
  present design ideas and processes."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** the five applications — **"MR SCP"** (Modelling,
  Research, Simulation & graphics, Communication, Presentation).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** forward to P3 communication (P5.2) and research (P5.3 — digital
  research); forward to H6.2 (emerging technologies — 3D printing, AI, simulation) and the Flow
  Hive's reliance on 3D printing & injection moulding (`case_flow_hive`); back to P2-02 (CAM links
  CAD to production).
- **Worked examples / values:** map each of the five applications (MR SCP) to a real tool and a
  real design task; note where simulation *replaces* a physical prototype (cost/time saving).

### P2-99 — Module Review: Design Processes & Production
- **Theory focus:** assemble the pipeline — **setting (DCIC) → scale (JBM) → materials (CPA FACE)
  → safe production (ES-EAP) → impacts (PC SCIE) → digital tools (MR SCP).** Re-surface every
  mnemonic and the mark-earning phrasing in one pass (STYLE §5.3).
- **Extended-response prep:** an integrated, multi-topic HSC-style question, e.g. *"For a product
  you have studied, analyse how the choice of production setting, materials and processes affected
  its impact on society and the environment."* Model a band-6 answer threading settings + scale +
  materials + impact together.
- **Syllabus dot-points served:** all of P2.1/P2.2/P4.2/P6.1/P6.2 (revisited).
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** full recap of **DCIC, JBM, CPA FACE, ES-EAP, PC SCIE,
  MR SCP** — five-plus lists, so this review leans hard on the hooks.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** full-scale interleave to H1 (MDP production & resources) and H2
  (trends, emerging tech, impact). Flag the trap: confusing *production setting* (where/who) with
  *production scale* (how many) — they're independent axes.
- **Worked examples / values:** quick-fire recap of the module's worked examples + 4–5 exam-style
  questions spanning settings, materials, safety and impact.
