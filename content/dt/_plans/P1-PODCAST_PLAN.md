# P1 — Design Fundamentals podcast plan

**Module:** P1 (Year 11, Preliminary) · **Outcome:** P1.1 — *examines design theory and
practice, and considers the factors affecting designing and producing in design projects.*

This is the **first module of the whole course** and the foundation everything else stands on.
Three things get planted here and re-watered for the next two years: (1) what *design* actually
is and how designers work, (2) the **design process** the student will run on every project, and
(3) the **eleven factors affecting design** — the single most reusable list in the subject,
which returns in H1.1 to be sharpened into "success and failure." Get these solid in audio now
and the HSC module (H1/H2) becomes revision, not new learning.

**The extended-response problem these episodes fix:** beginners write about design as if it were
just "making things look nice." HSC markers want design treated as a *deliberate, factor-driven
process* with trade-offs justified. A band-6 answer never says "I chose timber because I like
it" — it says "timber was selected against the factors of *function, finance, aesthetics and
short- and long-term environmental consequences*…". This module trains that reflex from day one.

**Target listener:** a Year 11 student who has just picked the subject, is about to start their
first preliminary design project, and has never heard the word "ergonomics" used seriously.

## Marquee mnemonics coined in this module (record once, reuse identically — STYLE §5.4)
- **The 11 factors affecting design — "FFA EQ NEW LOAd"** (the master list; reused verbatim from
  the CASE plan and in H1.1). The eleven, in the syllabus's own words: **Appropriateness of the
  design solution, Needs, Function, Aesthetics, Finance, Ergonomics, Work health & safety,
  Quality, Short- and long-term environmental consequences, Obsolescence, Life cycle analysis.**
  Taught as a story: *a design has to do its job (Function, Needs, Appropriateness), look and
  feel right (Aesthetics, Ergonomics), be affordable and safe (Finance, WHS), be well made
  (Quality), and not wreck the planet across its life (Environmental, Obsolescence, Life cycle
  analysis).* "Write all eleven down the second the exam starts."
- **The design process — "DRIPER"**: **D**efine the need/brief → **R**esearch → **I**deate
  (generate ideas) → **P**roduce/realise → **E**valuate → **R**efine (loop back). Stress it is a
  *cycle*, not a straight line — you re-enter it constantly. (Reused in P3 and all of H1.)

## Episodes (P1 module)

### P1-01 — What Design Is: Theory & Practice
- **Theory focus:** design as purposeful problem-solving — the difference between *art* (self-
  expression) and *design* (solving an identified need for a user). Introduce design theory vs.
  design practice, and the idea that good design is *intentional* and *justified*. Assume no
  prior vocab; this episode supplies it.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain the relationship between design theory and design
  practice."* Weak answer: "theory is ideas and practice is making" (a bare restatement, band 3).
  Strong answer: defines theory as the *principles and knowledge* that inform decisions and
  practice as the *application* of those principles in real projects, then shows the two-way
  feedback — practice tests theory, theory disciplines practice — with one concrete example.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "design theory and practice"; "range of design
  professions."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** none new (definitions, not a list). Plant **DRIPER**
  here as a teaser for P1-03.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** forward to P1-04 (factors are *how* design gets justified);
  forward to H3.2 (creativity); back-reference nothing (this is episode one).
- **Worked examples / values:** contrast a designed object vs. an artwork (a chair you can sit in
  vs. a sculpture of a chair). Use a Cambridge textbook designer example.

### P1-02 — The Work of Designers
- **Theory focus:** the *range* of design professions (industrial, graphic, fashion, interior,
  engineering, UX/digital), the **nature and variety** of their work, and how professions
  **interact and overlap** on a real project. One named Australian designer + one international,
  studied properly (the syllabus requires investigating *at least one* designer).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Describe the work of a designer you have studied and the
  processes they use."* Weak: lists biography facts. Strong: ties the designer's *process and
  decisions* to syllabus language (how they identify needs, research, prototype, evaluate) — i.e.
  treats the designer as evidence for the design process, which is exactly what H1.2 asks.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "nature and variety of work of a range of design
  professions"; "interaction and overlap of design professions"; "Australian and international
  designers and their work" + "investigate at least one designer and the nature of their work";
  "identify a range of career opportunities in design and production."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** the design professions — teach by *grouping*
  (objects / images / spaces / systems) rather than an acronym (STYLE §5.4: a structure beats a
  forced acronym).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** forward to H1.2 ("relate the practices and processes of
  designers… to the major design project") — flag that this episode is the Year-11 seed of a
  Year-12 outcome. Cross-link to P3 collaborative design teams (overlap of professions).
- **Worked examples / values:** pull a concrete designer profile from
  `resources/extracted/notes/work-of-designers.md` and the Cambridge textbook; record the
  designer's name, field, one signature product, and the process steps the notes attribute to
  them.

### P1-03 — The Design Process
- **Theory focus:** the design process end-to-end, "from initial contact with clients to final
  presentation," and that it is **iterative** (you loop, you don't march). Walk **DRIPER** stage
  by stage. Note the same process scales across domestic, community, industrial and commercial
  settings (seed for P2).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Describe and analyse the processes undertaken when designing."*
  Weak: lists the stages as a fixed recipe. Strong: names the stages *and* analyses why it loops
  — evaluation feeds back into research; a failed prototype sends you back to ideation — using
  one worked example.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "design processes used in domestic, community,
  industrial and commercial settings from initial contact with clients to final presentation" +
  "describe and analyse the processes undertaken when designing"; "apply a design process when
  developing design projects."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **DRIPER** (coined above) — this is the episode that
  cements it. Have them write the six-stage cycle on paper.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** forward to P3 (each stage maps to a P3 skill — research methods,
  evaluation, project management); forward to H4.1/H5.1 (the MDP *is* this process run for real);
  back to P1-01 (design as intentional process).
- **Worked examples / values:** trace one familiar product (e.g. a water bottle) through all six
  DRIPER stages with a real decision at each. Use the Cambridge process diagram for the canonical
  stage names so wording matches the textbook.

### P1-04 — Factors Affecting Design — Part 1 (does its job, fits the user)
- **Theory focus:** the first block of the eleven factors — **Appropriateness, Needs, Function,
  Aesthetics, Ergonomics**. What each means, how they trade off, and how to *write* about them.
  Define ergonomics and anthropometrics properly (a common exam mark-loser).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Analyse how function and aesthetics were balanced in a product
  you have studied."* Weak: "it looks good and works" (asserts, band 3). Strong: defines both
  factors, identifies the *tension* (a design decision that traded one against the other) and
  judges whether the balance was appropriate for the user/need.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "factors affecting designing and producing
  including: appropriateness of the design solution; needs; function; aesthetics; ergonomics" +
  "identify factors affecting design"; "appraise the aesthetic and functional qualities of a
  variety of design products, systems and/or environments."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** introduce **FFA EQ NEW LOAd** as the full set of
  eleven (so they meet the whole list now), then teach the first five in depth. Use the
  "*does its job, looks and feels right*" half of the story.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** forward to P1-05 (the other six factors); forward to H1.1
  (success & failure analysed through these same factors — the Edsel failed on *needs* and
  *market read*; cash in `case_ford_edsel`); cross-link ergonomics → P2.2 safety/health.
- **Worked examples / values:** an ergonomics worked example with real anthropometric reasoning
  (e.g. a chair/handle sized to a percentile range); pull figures from the Cambridge textbook.

### P1-05 — Factors Affecting Design — Part 2 (affordable, safe, well-made, sustainable)
- **Theory focus:** the remaining six — **Finance, Work health & safety, Quality, Short- and
  long-term environmental consequences, Obsolescence, Life cycle analysis.** Define *life cycle
  analysis* (cradle-to-grave) and the three kinds of *obsolescence* (functional, technological,
  planned/style) — both heavily examined.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Discuss the long-term environmental impacts of a design you have
  studied."* (real 2022 HSC stem). Weak: "it creates waste." Strong: uses *life cycle analysis*
  as the frame — materials, manufacture, use, disposal — and distinguishes short- vs long-term
  consequences, naming planned obsolescence where relevant.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "finance; work health and safety; quality;
  short-term and long-term environmental consequences; obsolescence; life cycle analysis" +
  "compare and contrast the factors to be considered in the design and production of design
  projects."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** complete **FFA EQ NEW LOAd**; drill the three types of
  obsolescence (mini-hook: *"works fine but feels old"* = style; *"can't get parts"* =
  technological; *"designed to die"* = planned).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** forward to P2.2 (sustainability, env/social issues) and H2.2
  (evaluating impact on society & environment); cash in `case_central_park_sydney` (looks green,
  under-delivers — life cycle analysis vs marketing) and `case_apple_newton` (technological
  obsolescence / "too early").
- **Worked examples / values:** a full life-cycle analysis of one everyday product (the 2022
  "drink bottle" comparison is a perfect template — glass vs plastic across the four life stages).

### P1-99 — Module Review: Design Fundamentals
- **Theory focus:** tie the whole module together — design-as-process (DRIPER) *driven by* the
  eleven factors, evidenced by how real designers work. Re-surface every key term and the two
  marquee mnemonics in one pass (STYLE §5.3).
- **Extended-response prep:** one integrated, multi-topic question in real HSC style, e.g.
  *"Analyse how a designer you have studied applied the design process and the factors affecting
  design to develop a successful product."* Model a band-6 answer that weaves designer + DRIPER +
  named factors together — the shape H1 will demand.
- **Syllabus dot-points served:** all of P1.1 (revisited, not re-lifted).
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** full recap of **FFA EQ NEW LOAd** and **DRIPER** —
  the wording students will reuse for two years.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** full-scale interleave — forward-map every P1 idea to where it
  returns (P2 production/settings, P3 management/research, H1 MDP, H2 innovation). Flag the
  classic trap: confusing *function* (does it work) with *appropriateness* (is it the right
  solution for this need).
- **Worked examples / values:** reuse the Part-1/Part-2 worked examples as a quick-fire recap;
  add 3–5 exam-style questions spanning the module.
