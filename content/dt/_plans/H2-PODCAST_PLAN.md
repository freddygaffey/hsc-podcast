# H2 — Innovation & Emerging Technologies podcast plan

**Module:** H2 (Year 12, HSC) · **Outcomes:** H2.1, H2.2, H3.1, H3.2, H6.2 — trends in society;
ethical & environmental issues; the factors that influence the success of innovation;
creativity & innovative practice; emerging technologies.

This is the **written-exam powerhouse**. The HSC paper is **Section I** (10 marks, multiple
choice, dominated by innovation/emerging-tech stems), **Section II** (15 marks short answer, much
of it H2/H3 material), and **Section III** (15 marks, the **extended response** — almost always
"discuss/analyse innovation, ethics, trends or emerging technology, with examples"). Where H1 was
*do the project*, H2 is *write the essay*. The single most valuable asset a student can walk in
with is one innovation they can tell cold and grade against the six success factors — which is
exactly what the **CASE module** (Flow Hive + four flops) was built to supply. This module
arms the essay.

**The extended-response problem these episodes fix:** Section III answers that *describe* an
innovation without *analysing the factors*, or that moralise about ethics without a *framework*.
Real Q15 stems — *"Analyse the ethical and social implications for designers in creating solutions
to world issues"* (2023), *"Analyse the effects of TWO alternative energy systems on society and
the environment"* (2022) — reward a *named framework + two worked examples + a judgement.* This
module gives the frameworks (TEMPLE, the impact lenses) and drills the two-example essay shape.

**Target listener:** a Year 12 student preparing for the written HSC, who needs a repeatable essay
structure and a stock of examples (their case study + emerging-tech instances) to deploy.

## Marquee mnemonics (reuse the CASE wording identically — STYLE §5.4)
- **The 6 factors affecting the success of an innovation (H3.1) — "TEMPLE"** *(verbatim from the
  CASE plan):* **T**iming · **E**merging/available technologies · **M**arketing strategies ·
  **P**olitical factors · **L**egal & economic factors · **E**ra (historical & cultural
  influences). *"An innovation succeeds inside a TEMPLE."* Every case study is graded against these.
- **Emerging-technology implications (H6.2) — "LESEE"**: **L**egal, **E**thical, **S**ocial,
  **E**conomic, **E**cological — *"appraise the ecological, economic, social, ethical and legal
  implications of new and emerging technologies"* (syllabus verbatim). Hook: *"weigh new tech in
  the LESEE."*
- **Trends in society (H2.1) — "SPEEG"**: **S**ocial, **P**olitical, **E**conomic,
  **E**nvironmental, **G**lobal — the five trend drivers the syllabus lists.
- **Historical & cultural influences (H2.1) — "C-CWT"**: changing social **C**hange/trends,
  **C**ultural diversity, changing nature of **W**ork, **T**echnological change.
- **Ethical & environmental issues (H2.2) — "SIRRI"**: **S**ustainable technologies, **I**ntellectual
  property protection, **R**ights & responsibilities of the designer, **R**esponsibilities to
  society, **I**mpact on Australian society.

## Episodes (H2 module)

### H2-01 — What Makes an Innovation Succeed (the TEMPLE factors)
- **Theory focus:** the H3.1 core — **factors that impact on the success of innovation: timing,
  available and emerging technologies, historical and cultural, political, economic and legal
  factors, marketing strategies** — taught as **TEMPLE**. *Differentiating* the factors behind
  success vs failure. This is the spine of every Section III answer.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Analyse the factors that contributed to the success or failure of
  an innovation you have studied."* Weak: "it was a good idea and sold well." Strong: walks each
  TEMPLE factor for the chosen innovation, weighs which mattered most, and *contrasts* a failure
  where the same factor went wrong (Flow Hive's timing vs the Edsel's misread market).
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "factors that impact on success of innovation
  including: timing, available and emerging technologies, historical and cultural, political,
  economic and legal factors, marketing strategies" + "differentiate between factors which have
  contributed to the success or failure of innovations."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **TEMPLE** — reused *verbatim* from the CASE plan.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** cash in **`case_flow_hive`** (the success graded factor-by-factor)
  against **`case_ford_edsel` / `case_apple_newton` / `case_microsoft_zune`** (failures); back to
  H1.1 (factors affecting *design* — contrast with factors affecting *innovation*); forward to
  H2-08 (the case-study essay).
- **Worked examples / values:** a full TEMPLE grid for the Flow Hive (timing = "save the bees"
  moment; tech = 3D printing/injection moulding; marketing = crowdfunding video + 200k followers;
  legal = patent before launch). Pull specifics from the Flow Hive notes.

### H2-02 — Entrepreneurial Activity & the Agencies of Innovation
- **Theory focus:** **the nature of entrepreneurial activity and its role in design and
  technological activity; agencies which influence entrepreneurial activity; management and
  entrepreneurial activity; legal and ethical issues** — plus **the role of a variety of agencies
  that influence the development, implementation and acceptance of innovation** (government,
  standards bodies, funding/crowdfunding, IP offices, industry).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Discuss the influence of entrepreneurial activity on successful
  design and innovation."* Weak: "entrepreneurs take risks." Strong: defines entrepreneurship,
  names the agencies that enabled/blocked a real innovation, and weighs the legal/ethical issues
  (IP, risk, responsibility) with an example.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "entrepreneurial activity — nature of entrepreneurial
  activity; role in design and technological activity; agencies which influence entrepreneurial
  activity; management and entrepreneurial activity; legal and ethical issues"; "the role of a
  variety of agencies that may impact upon the success of innovation" + "discuss the influence of
  entrepreneurial activity on successful design and innovation"; "discuss the legal and ethical
  issues related to entrepreneurial activities."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** agencies hook — **"GASP-I"**: **G**overnment,
  **A**ssociations/standards bodies, **S**ponsors/funders (incl. crowdfunding), **P**atent/IP
  offices, **I**ndustry partners.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P3-07 / H1-09 (management & entrepreneurial activity);
  cash in `case_flow_hive` (the Andersons as entrepreneurs; crowdfunding agency; patent strategy);
  forward to H2-05 (legal/ethical issues — IP).
- **Worked examples / values:** the Flow Hive's entrepreneurial path — self-funded R&D → patent →
  Indiegogo record ($12.2M) → manufacture — mapped to the agencies that enabled each step.

### H2-03 — Trends in Society on Design and Production
- **Theory focus:** **trends in designing and producing influenced by social, global, political,
  economic and environmental issues** (SPEEG) and *discussing the issues arising* from them.
  How shifts in society pull design in new directions (sustainability, automation, personalisation,
  globalised manufacture).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Explain how the trend towards [mass production / sustainability /
  automation] affects design and production."* (cf. 2022's mass-production/workplace stem). Weak:
  "things change over time." Strong: names the trend, classifies its driver (which of SPEEG),
  and analyses the *issues it raises* for designers, workers and society, with an example.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "trends in designing and producing, including those
  which are influenced by social, global, political, economic and environmental issues" + "discuss
  the issues arising from trends in design and technological activity."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **SPEEG** (trend drivers, coined above).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P2-02 (mass production as a trend) and P2-05 (environmental/
  social issues); forward to H2-04 (historical/cultural influences) and H6.2 (emerging tech *as* a
  trend driver); cash in `case_microsoft_zune` (mistimed against the iPod/download trend).
- **Worked examples / values:** two contemporary trends (e.g. sustainability-driven design;
  automation/Industry 4.0) classified by SPEEG driver with a real product example for each.

### H2-04 — Historical & Cultural Influences on Design
- **Theory focus:** **historical and cultural influences — changing social trends, cultural
  diversity, the changing nature of work, technological change** (C-CWT) — *identifying and
  acknowledging* how the past and culture shape what gets designed and how. The long view behind
  the "Era" in TEMPLE.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Discuss how historical and cultural influences have shaped a design
  or innovation."* Weak: "in the old days things were different." Strong: names a specific
  historical/cultural shift (e.g. the move to remote/digital work; cultural diversity driving
  inclusive design) and shows it *acknowledged in* a real design decision.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "historical and cultural influences on designing and
  producing, including: changing social trends; cultural diversity; the changing nature of work;
  technological change" + "identify and acknowledge historical and cultural influences on design
  and technological development."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **C-CWT** (coined above).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to H2-03 (trends) and the "Era" factor of TEMPLE (H2-01);
  forward to H2-05 (cultural diversity → ethical responsibility); cash in `case_flow_hive`
  (centuries-old smoke-the-bees tradition vs a new way — cultural/historical influence in action).
- **Worked examples / values:** one innovation set against its historical/cultural moment (e.g.
  the smartphone and the changing nature of work; the Flow Hive and beekeeping tradition).

### H2-05 — Ethical & Environmental Issues
- **Theory focus:** **ethical and environmental considerations for designers and society;
  sustainable technologies; protection of intellectual property; rights and responsibilities of
  the designer; impact on Australian society** (SIRRI) — *critically analysing* ethical issues and
  *discussing* environmental considerations. The H2.2 outcome, and a perennial Section III topic.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Analyse the ethical and social implications for designers in
  creating solutions to world issues. Refer to TWO of: Health, Food production, Living conditions."*
  (real 2023 Q15). Weak: "designers should be ethical." Strong: picks two examples, applies the
  ethical + environmental lenses (SIRRI), weighs costs and benefits, and reaches a judgement —
  the two-example essay shape.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "ethical and environmental issues — ethical and
  environmental considerations for designers and society; sustainable technologies; protection of
  intellectual property; rights and responsibilities of the designer; impact on Australian
  society" + "critically analyse ethical issues in relation to innovation"; "discuss ethical and
  environmental considerations for designers and society in general"; "identify the factors which
  contribute to the efficiency and sustainability of technologies."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **SIRRI** (coined above); reuse **ISE** (individual/
  society/environment impact) and **LCA** (life cycle analysis, from P1-05).
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P2-05 (environmental & social issues) and P1-05 (LCA);
  forward to H6.2 (LESEE implications of emerging tech) and H2-02 (IP as legal/ethical); cash in
  `case_central_park_sydney` (green claims vs real sustainability) and `case_flow_hive`
  (sustainability and IP as ethical themes).
- **Worked examples / values:** a two-example ethics answer worked end-to-end (e.g. a health
  innovation + a food-production innovation), modelling the 2023 Q15 structure.

### H2-06 — Creativity & Innovative Design Practice
- **Theory focus:** **creativity and innovative design practice — processes undertaken to develop
  innovations; success of innovation; adaptation and development of ideas; responding to
  motivational stimuli; creative thinking** (the H3.2 outcome). What creativity *is* in a design
  context and how it's deliberately produced (back to P3-01's techniques, now at innovation scale).
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Discuss the role of creativity in successful innovation."* Weak:
  "you need to be creative." Strong: defines creativity vs innovation (idea vs *implemented* idea),
  shows a creative process adapting/developing an idea in response to a stimulus, and links
  creativity to the *success* of a real innovation.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "creativity and innovative design practice —
  processes undertaken to develop innovations; success of innovation; adaptation and development of
  ideas; responding to motivational stimuli; creative thinking" + "critically analyse successful
  innovation"; "discuss concepts of quality, innovation and creativity."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** distinction hook — **"creativity = the idea; innovation
  = the idea *delivered*"**; reuse **SCAMPER** (P3-01) as the creative-thinking toolkit.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P3-01 (creative approaches) and H3.1/TEMPLE (creativity
  feeds success); cash in `case_flow_hive` (the vertical-cell creative leap, adapted over a decade)
  and `case_apple_newton` (creative but mistimed); forward to H2-08.
- **Worked examples / values:** the Flow Hive's creative breakthrough traced as a process —
  stimulus (hating to disturb the bees) → adaptation → development → delivered innovation.

### H2-07 — Emerging Technologies
- **Theory focus:** **emerging technologies; factors affecting their development; criteria for
  evaluation; impact on society and the environment; impact on innovation** (the H6.2 outcome).
  *Appraising the ecological, economic, social, ethical and legal implications* (LESEE) of new
  tech, and analysing its impact on innovation. Concrete instances: AI, 3D/additive manufacturing,
  biotechnology, renewable-energy systems, smart materials, IoT.
- **Extended-response prep:** *"Analyse the effects of TWO [emerging technologies / alternative
  energy systems] on society and the environment."* (real 2022 Q15). Weak: "AI is changing
  everything." Strong: picks two technologies, appraises each through **LESEE**, weighs benefits
  against costs, and judges the net impact on society, environment and *innovation itself*.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "emerging technologies — factors affecting their
  development; criteria for evaluation; impact on society and the environment; impact on
  innovation" + "appraise the ecological, economic, social, ethical, and legal implications of new
  and emerging technologies"; "analyse the impact of emerging technologies on innovation."
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** **LESEE** (coined above) — the evaluation framework for
  any emerging tech in the exam.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to P2-06 (computer-based technologies) and H1-08 (emerging
  tech changing industrial practice); cash in `case_flow_hive` (3D printing/injection moulding made
  it possible) and `case_apple_newton` (handwriting recognition before its time → ARM chip legacy);
  forward to H2-08 (an emerging tech can *be* the case-study innovation).
- **Worked examples / values:** two emerging technologies appraised through LESEE (e.g. additive
  manufacturing + a renewable-energy system), mirroring the 2022 Q15. Use the emerging-tech notes
  (`h6-2-emerging-technologies-and-factors-1.md`) for the factor list.

### H2-08 — The Innovation Case Study & the Extended Response
- **Theory focus:** how to **conduct research to examine the success of an innovation and produce
  an investigative report** (H5.2), and how to convert it into a **Section III extended response.**
  Assemble the module's frameworks into one repeatable essay engine: pick the innovation, grade it
  on **TEMPLE**, weigh **LESEE/SIRRI** implications, place it in its **SPEEG/C-CWT** context, and
  judge it. This is the exam-technique capstone.
- **Extended-response prep:** model a full Q15 (15-mark) answer end-to-end on the Flow Hive (or
  the student's own case study): introduction with a thesis, TEMPLE-driven body, ethics/impact
  paragraph, judgement — matched to the marking criteria (*knowledge & understanding · relevant
  examples · logical, cohesive response*). Contrast a band-3 vs band-6 version of the same answer.
- **Syllabus dot-points served (verbatim):** "research methods — data collection, analysis,
  interpretation and application of conclusions" + "conduct research to examine the success of an
  innovation and produce an investigative report" (H5.2), applied to all of H3.1/H2.2/H6.2.
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** the **essay engine** — *"TEMPLE the success, LESEE the
  tech, SIRRI the ethics, SPEEG the trend, then judge."* One sentence that sequences every
  framework in the module.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** back to the entire **CASE module** (`_plans/CASE-PODCAST_PLAN.md`)
  and H1-10 (the investigative-report skill); ties every case study into one exam answer; the
  natural finale before H2-99.
- **Worked examples / values:** a full marked-up Flow Hive extended response, annotated against the
  three marking criteria; a band-3 → band-6 rewrite of one paragraph.

### H2-99 — Module Review: Innovation & Emerging Technologies
- **Theory focus:** assemble the exam arsenal — **TEMPLE (success) · SPEEG + C-CWT (trends &
  history) · SIRRI (ethics/environment) · LESEE (emerging tech) · creativity→innovation · the
  essay engine.** Re-surface every framework and the Q15 structure in one pass (STYLE §5.3). Lean
  hardest of all reviews on exam questions — this is the most heavily examined module.
- **Extended-response prep:** a battery of real-style Section I MCQ + Section II short answers +
  one full Section III, each with model answers, spanning the whole module.
- **Syllabus dot-points served:** all of H2.1/H2.2/H3.1/H3.2/H6.2 (revisited).
- **Memorisable lists needing mnemonics:** full recap of **TEMPLE, SPEEG, C-CWT, SIRRI, LESEE,
  GASP-I**, and the essay-engine sentence.
- **Cross-links / interleaving:** full-scale interleave to H1 (the MDP half) and the CASE module.
  Flag the headline trap: confusing the **11 factors affecting design (H1.1, FFA EQ NEW LOAd)**
  with the **6 factors affecting innovation success (H3.1, TEMPLE)** — different lists, both
  examined, often in the same paper.
- **Worked examples / values:** quick-fire recap of the module's worked examples + a full mock
  Section III with a marked model answer.
