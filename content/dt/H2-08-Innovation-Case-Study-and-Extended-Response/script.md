NARRATOR: Now the Section three format, in Listing 2, because writing to the marking criteria is half the battle. Section three is one question, fifteen marks, about forty minutes. And it's marked on three criteria, which the paper states: one, demonstrate knowledge and understanding relevant to the question; two, communicate ideas and information using relevant examples; and three, present a logical and cohesive response. Read those again, knowledge relevant to the question, relevant examples, logical and cohesive. That tells you exactly what to do: answer the actual question asked, use your case study as the example, and structure it clearly. A brain-dump of everything you know about the Flow Hive scores poorly, an answer shaped to the question, with the Flow Hive as evidence, scores well.

NARRATOR: Here's the essay engine, in Listing 3, the move that assembles the module. Whatever the Section three question asks, you reach for the right framework and apply it to your innovation. If it asks about success, grade your innovation on TEMPLE. If it's about emerging technology, appraise it through LESEE. If it's about ethics and society, analyse through SIRRI. If it's about trends or history, frame with SPEEG and C-CWT. Then you judge. The one-sentence hook: TEMPLE the success, LESEE the tech, SIRRI the ethics, SPEEG and C-CWT the context, then judge. You don't use all of them every time, you select the framework the question calls for, apply it to your case study, and reach a conclusion.

NARRATOR: And the structure, every time: a short introduction that states a thesis answering the question, two or three body paragraphs that each apply a framework to your innovation with specific detail, and a conclusion that delivers a clear judgement. Thesis, framework-driven body, judgement. That structure delivers the third marking criterion, logical and cohesive, automatically.

QUESTION: Question one. What three criteria is a Section three extended response marked on? Three marks. Pause the player, write your answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. A Section three response is marked on demonstrating knowledge and understanding relevant to the question; communicating ideas and information using relevant examples; and presenting a logical and cohesive response. Three marks, one per criterion. The practical takeaway is to answer the actual question, use your case study as the example, and structure the response clearly, which is exactly what the three criteria reward.

QUESTION: Question two. Outline how you would structure a fifteen-mark extended response on an innovation. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. I would structure it as: an introduction stating a clear thesis that directly answers the question; two or three body paragraphs, each applying a relevant framework, such as the factors of innovation success or the ethical and environmental implications, to my chosen innovation with specific supporting detail; and a conclusion that delivers a reasoned judgement. This ensures the response is logical, cohesive and focused on the question. Three marks: introduction with thesis, framework-driven body paragraphs with evidence, and a judgement conclusion. The marker rewards a clear, question-focused structure.

QUESTION: Question three. Analyse the factors contributing to the success of an innovation you have studied, and discuss its impact on society and the environment. Fifteen marks. This is a full Section three question, so pause the player and plan and write a full response before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer skeleton, using the Flow Hive, and the full version is in Listing 4. Introduction: state that the Flow Hive succeeded through several innovation-success factors and has had a largely positive but mixed social and environmental impact. Body one, success, apply TEMPLE: timing, the save-the-bees movement; emerging technologies, three-D printing and injection moulding enabling the design; marketing, the record-breaking crowdfunding campaign raising over twelve million dollars; and legal, the patent filed before launch, weigh marketing and timing as decisive. Body two, impact, apply SIRRI and the three impact levels: socially it engaged the public with beekeeping and supported hobbyists; environmentally it promotes bee welfare and sustainable home food production, against the footprint of its plastic components; ethically the inventors protected their intellectual property responsibly. Conclusion: the Flow Hive is a successful innovation whose benefits to society and the environment outweigh its modest costs. Fifteen marks are awarded for analysing success with named factors and specific evidence, discussing impact across society and environment with balance, and a logical, cohesive response with a clear judgement. Telling the story without frameworks or judgement caps in the middle bands.

QUESTION: Question four. Why is it important to answer the specific question asked rather than writing everything you know about your innovation? Two marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. It is important because the first marking criterion requires knowledge and understanding relevant to the question, so marks are awarded only for material that actually addresses what was asked. Writing everything you know, regardless of the question, wastes time on irrelevant content and produces an unfocused, less cohesive response, whereas selecting and shaping your innovation knowledge to the specific question demonstrates understanding and earns the relevance and cohesion marks. Two marks: state that marks require relevance to the question, and that a focused, shaped answer scores while a brain-dump does not. This is the single most important exam-technique point.
