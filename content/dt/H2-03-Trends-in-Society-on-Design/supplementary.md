# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the trend drivers,
the issues, the worked examples and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — Trends and their drivers (SPEEG)
```text
Designers must MONITOR trends to stay relevant. Trends are driven by:
  S - Social        (demographics, multiculturalism, social conscience, values)
  P - Political     (government policy, regulation, political climate)
  E - Economic      (economic conditions, wealth, socio-economic status, cost pressure)
  E - Environmental (sustainability, climate, resources)
  G - Global        (globalisation, international markets, global supply chains, world events)

(Historical & cultural influences = so important they get their own episode — the "era" of TEMPLE.)
```

### Listing 2 — How each driver shifts design
| Driver | Example trend → design shift |
|---|---|
| Social | Inclusion → universal / accessible design |
| Political | Renewable incentives → clean-energy products |
| Economic | Downturn → cheaper, value-focused design |
| Environmental | Sustainability → recyclable, low-footprint design |
| Global | Globalised manufacture → outsourced production, worldwide products |

> First step of a strong answer: ask which SPEEG driver is behind the trend.

### Listing 3 — Issues arising from trends (the "discuss" mark)
```text
Trends create OPPORTUNITIES and PROBLEMS — "discuss" = analyse both:
  Mass production / automation -> workplace issues (job losses, deskilling) + efficiency gains.
  Sustainability               -> cost issue (greener = pricier) = environmental vs financial.
  Globalised production        -> ethical issues (outsourcing, labour conditions) + cheaper goods.

Name the trend -> classify the driver -> DISCUSS the issues (both ways).
```

### Listing 4 — Worked example: two contemporary trends classified by SPEEG
```text
SUSTAINABLE DESIGN
  Driver: Environmental (+ social conscience).
  Shift: recyclable materials, energy efficiency, life-cycle thinking.
  Issue: cost — sustainable options often more expensive (environmental vs financial).

AUTOMATION / INDUSTRY 4.0
  Driver: Economic + Global.
  Shift: products made by smart, connected machines.
  Issue: workplace disruption (job losses, deskilling vs new technical roles); efficiency/consistency gains.
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "How does a trend affect design & production?"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "More things get made / things change." -> barely engages.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - NAME the trend + classify its DRIVER (SPEEG).
  - DISCUSS the issues both ways (benefits + problems for designers/workers/society).
  - Reach a balanced judgement.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — Influences that drive trends.
  Social, political, economic, environmental, global (SPEEG) + historical & cultural.

Q2 (3 marks) — How has an environmental trend influenced design?
  Sustainability concern -> recyclable/renewable materials, energy efficiency, life-cycle thinking
  (e.g. design for recyclability/repair). Issue: cost (greener = pricier) = environmental vs financial.

Q3 (5 marks) — Discuss the issues arising from automation.
  Driver: economic + global. Benefits: efficiency, consistency, lower cost, complex precise designs.
  Problems: job losses, deskilling (vs new technical roles), community impact. Balanced judgement:
  efficiency gains vs real social costs. (Naming without discussing caps low.)

Q4 (2 marks) — Why monitor trends?
  Trends shape consumer needs/wants -> monitoring keeps designs relevant & timely; ignoring them risks
  outdated/mistimed products (an innovation launched as the market moves away).
```
