# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken in the audio — it reinforces
the definitions, contrasts and model answers from the narration.

### Listing 1 — What design produces: product, system or environment
| Outcome | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Product | A physical object | Chair, water bottle, headphones |
| System | A set of parts/steps organised to do a job | School recycling system, café queue management, app checkout |
| Environment | A designed space | Bedroom layout, playground, retail store interior |

> Design meets an **identified need or opportunity**.
> *Need* = an existing problem you respond to. *Opportunity* = a gap you reach for (often before
> demand exists). Use the word — product / system / environment — that actually fits.

### Listing 2 — Art versus Design (the examinable contrast)
| | **Art** | **Design** |
|---|---|---|
| Primary purpose | Self-expression | Solving an identified need / problem |
| Answers to | The artist | The user / client / need |
| Driven by | The maker's idea or emotion | Function, constraints, brief, budget |
| Success measured by | Expressive / aesthetic impact | Does it work — does it meet the need? |
| Freedom | Can be whatever the artist wants | Bound by constraints and a purpose |

> One-line answer: *Art is made to be expressed; design is made to be used.* Lead with **primary
> purpose** (design also has aesthetics; art can also be useful — it's about what comes first).
> Use **"whereas"** in a "distinguish/compare" question.

### Listing 3 — Design theory versus design practice
| | **Design theory** | **Design practice** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The knowledge, principles and concepts that inform design | The application of that knowledge to make real solutions |
| In a sentence | What a designer **knows** | What a designer **does** |
| Includes | Principles of good design, elements & principles (line, shape, colour, balance, proportion), material properties, the factors affecting design, the design process | Researching, sketching, prototyping, testing, refining, producing |

### Listing 4 — Headline question: "Explain the relationship between design theory and practice"
```text
WEAK answer (band ~3 — bare restatement):
  "Theory is the ideas and practice is the making."
  -> defines the two but never explains the RELATIONSHIP the question asked for.

STRONG answer (band ~6 — names the two-way loop):
  - Design theory = the knowledge/principles that inform design decisions.
  - Design practice = the application of that knowledge to create real solutions.
  - They are INTERDEPENDENT (two-way):
      * Theory disciplines practice -> decisions are informed, not arbitrary.
      * Practice tests & refines theory -> real outcomes reveal which principles
        hold and feed back to improve understanding.
  - A skilled designer moves continually between the two.

The third mark is the LOOP, not the two definitions.
```

### Listing 5 — The design process (preview): DRIPER
```text
DRIPER — the design process is a CYCLE, not a straight line:
  D — Define the need / brief
  R — Research
  I — Ideate (generate ideas)
  P — Produce / realise
  E — Evaluate
  R — Refine  (loops back to the start)

It is the theory<->practice loop turned into named steps.
Taught in full in P1-03. Reused across every module and the Major Design Project.
```

### Listing 6 — The range of design professions (grouped by WHAT they design)
| Group (what they design) | Professions | Examples of their work |
|---|---|---|
| Objects (products) | Industrial / product designer | Furniture, appliances, tools |
| Images & communication | Graphic / multimedia designer | Logos, packaging, websites, signage |
| Spaces (environments) | Architect / interior designer | Buildings, room layouts, environments |
| Garments & textiles | Fashion / textile designer | Clothing, fabrics |
| Systems & how things are made | Engineer / technologist | Structures, mechanisms, production systems |

> Designers come from a *range* of fields (Work of Designers, §1.2); they often work
> **collaboratively** with engineers, clients and manufacturers (the **interaction and overlap**
> of professions → studied later under collaborative approaches). All professions share one
> **design process** and one set of **factors affecting design**.

### Listing 7 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (1 mark) — Define design.
  Design is the purposeful process of creating a product, system or environment to meet an
  identified need or opportunity.

Q2 (2 marks) — Distinguish between art and design.
  Art is primarily self-expression and answers to the artist, WHEREAS design is primarily
  problem-solving for a user, shaped by constraints such as function, cost and the brief.

Q3 (3 marks) — Explain the relationship between design theory and design practice.
  Theory = the knowledge/principles that inform design; practice = applying that knowledge to
  make real solutions. They are interdependent: theory disciplines practice (informed, not
  arbitrary decisions); practice tests and refines theory (real outcomes feed back). A designer
  moves continually between the two.

Q4 (2 marks) — Using an example, distinguish between a need and an opportunity.
  Need = an existing problem requiring a solution (e.g. wheelchair users unable to reach high
  shelves). Opportunity = a possibility a designer pursues, often before demand (e.g. combining
  phone + camera + computer). Needs are responded to; opportunities are reached for.

Q5 (3 marks) — Identify three design professions and outline the work in each.
  e.g. Industrial/product designer -> physical objects (furniture, appliances), balancing
  function, aesthetics, manufacturability. Graphic/multimedia designer -> visual communication
  (logos, packaging, websites). Architect/interior designer -> spaces and environments
  (buildings, layouts), considering how people use them.  [any 3 valid, 1 mark each]
```
