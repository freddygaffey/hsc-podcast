# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode (the most-examined topic in the course). Nothing here is
spoken — it reinforces the factors, the success/failure method, the case-study evidence, the worked
comparison and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — The eleven factors (FFA EQ NEW LOAd), applied to the MDP
```text
DOES ITS JOB:      Function · Needs · Appropriateness
FITS THE USER:     Aesthetics · Ergonomics
AFFORDABLE & SAFE: Finance · Work Health & Safety
WELL MADE:         Quality
DON'T WRECK THE    short/long-term Environmental consequences · Obsolescence · Life Cycle Analysis
  PLANET:

In the MDP: every justified decision names one of these. Your success CRITERIA = the factors made
specific. They are the VOCABULARY OF JUSTIFICATION (decision -> reason -> factor -> evidence).
```

### Listing 2 — Critically analysing success/failure (the H1.1 method)
```text
A design SUCCEEDS when it satisfies the factors that matter (above all the NEED);
it FAILS when it falls short on one or more critical factors.

TO ANALYSE (not just describe):
  1. Identify the SPECIFIC factor(s) that drove the outcome.
  2. JUDGE the design against them.
  3. Reach a reasoned VERDICT.

"Good/bad" = a verdict with no reason (low). Naming the factor + explaining = analysis (high).
```

### Listing 3 — The five case studies as factor evidence
| Product | Verdict | Decisive factor(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Flow Hive | **Success** | Met a genuine need; function; marketing & timing |
| Ford Edsel | Failure | **Needs** — wrong car/wrong market/wrong time (~64k units yr 1) |
| Apple Newton | Noble failure | **Obsolescence / timing** — tech (handwriting recognition) not ready; too early |
| Microsoft Zune | Failure | **Timing / marketing** — arrived after the iPod locked up the market |
| Central Park Sydney | Flawed | **Environmental / life cycle** — green image > measured performance |

> Keep ONE success + a flop per major factor ready -> answers any success/failure question.

### Listing 4 — Worked comparison: Flow Hive (success) vs Ford Edsel (failure)
| Factor | Flow Hive | Ford Edsel |
|---|---|---|
| Needs | Met a real, unsolved need (harvest without disturbing bees) | Misread the market entirely |
| Function | Worked elegantly | Functioned, but offered nothing wanted |
| Marketing & timing | Record crowdfunding; rode interest in bees | Over-hyped, then mistimed vs economical cars |
| Finance | Crowdfunding raised millions pre-production | Cost a fortune for ~64k sales |

> Verdict writes itself: Flow Hive satisfied the factors that mattered; the Edsel missed the most
> important one — the NEED. (The same factor, contrasted, = a top-band answer.)

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "Critically analyse a success AND a failure"
```text
WEAK answer (caps ~half): "The iPhone was good, the Edsel was bad." -> assertion, no factors.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - Analyse the SUCCESS against named factors (Flow Hive: need + function + marketing/timing).
  - Analyse the FAILURE against named factors (Edsel: needs — wrong car/market/time).
  - Reach a VERDICT for each + ideally CONTRAST on a shared factor (needs).
```

> **Headline trap:** the 11 factors affecting DESIGN (H1.1, FFA EQ NEW LOAd) ≠ the 6 factors affecting
> INNOVATION SUCCESS (H3.1, TEMPLE). Different lists, often both in one paper.

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (1 mark) — What is critically analysing a design?
  Examining it against the factors affecting design, judging how well it meets each and reaching a
  reasoned verdict — not just describing/asserting good or bad.

Q2 (3 marks) — A failed design + the factor that caused it.
  Ford Edsel — failed on NEEDS: misread the market, medium-priced car as tastes shifted to economical
  cars ("wrong car/market/time"), ~64k units yr 1. Pin failure to a named factor.

Q3 (6 marks) — Critically analyse a success AND a failure.
  Flow Hive (success): met a genuine need + function + crowdfunding marketing & timing. Edsel
  (failure): NEEDS — misread market, mistimed. Contrast: the need the Flow Hive met is the one the
  Edsel missed -> meeting the need is the most decisive factor. (Good/bad caps ~half.)

Q4 (2 marks) — Design factors vs innovation-success factors.
  Design factors = the 11 a designer weighs (function/needs/aesthetics... H1.1). Innovation factors =
  6 conditions for market success (timing/tech/marketing/political/legal-economic/era — TEMPLE, H3.1).
  Design itself vs conditions around an innovation.
```
