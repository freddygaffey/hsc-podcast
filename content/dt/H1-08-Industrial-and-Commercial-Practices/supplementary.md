# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the four
practices, the relate-the-principle skill, the worked mapping and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — H6.1: the four industrial & commercial practices (SPPC)
```text
S - Safe work practices using selected resources   (the safety content, as professionals apply it)
P - Production techniques                           (casting, moulding, machining, finishing)
P - Process selection                               (the process appropriate to the need/scale)
C - Collaborative designing & design teams          (teamwork, from module 3)

Four lenses for comparing YOUR production to industry.
```

### Listing 2 — Relating practice to the MDP: adapt the PRINCIPLE, not the scale
```text
Study what an industrial/commercial setting would do -> relate it to what YOU did -> JUSTIFY.

The PRINCIPLE is often the same even when the scale differs hugely:
  Industry: CNC machines for repeatable parts   ->  You: a jig/template for hand repeatability.
  Industry: formal QC sampling                  ->  You: check each part vs your criteria.

Don't copy the SCALE — adapt the PRINCIPLE to your project. That comparison, justified, = the skill.
(= module 2 settings DCIC + scales JBM applied: MDP is usually job production in a domestic setting.)
```

### Listing 3 — Industrial practice vs MDP (same principle, different scale)
| Industrial practice | Principle | Your MDP adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| CNC machining | Repeatability & precision | Hand jig / template |
| QC sampling on a line | Quality control | Check each part vs success criteria |
| Division of labour (team) | Efficiency | Sequence your own tasks / collaborate where allowed |
| Spray finishing on a line | Consistent finish | Careful hand-finishing to the same standard |

### Listing 4 — Worked example: jigs (industrial) → MDP
```text
INDUSTRIAL: furniture makers use jigs + CNC -> every component identical, fast accurate assembly.
PRINCIPLE: repeatability & precision.
MY MDP (one-off, school): made a simple drilling jig for the chair legs -> all 4 legs drilled
identically -> consistent assembly I couldn't achieve reliably by hand.
JUSTIFICATION: adapted the industrial PRINCIPLE of repeatability to my scale & resources.
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "Relate an industrial production technique to your MDP"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "Factories use big machines and I used a drill." -> scale contrast only.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - NAME a specific industrial technique (e.g. jigs/CNC).
  - Explain its PRINCIPLE (repeatability/precision).
  - Show how you ADAPTED that principle in your production (a hand jig), with justification.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — Industrial/commercial practices relating to the MDP.
  Safe work practices; production techniques; process selection; collaborative design teams. [SPPC]

Q2 (3 marks) — How does studying industry help justify your processes?
  Industry = a proven benchmark. Relating your choices to its principles (technique/QC/safety) shows
  they're sound & deliberate, even at a smaller scale (jig vs CNC) -> justifies them as appropriate.

Q3 (5 marks) — Relate an industrial production technique to your MDP.
  Jigs/CNC ensure repeatable, precise, fast assembly (principle = repeatability). In my one-off MDP I
  made a hand drilling jig -> all legs identical -> consistent assembly. Adapted the PRINCIPLE to my
  scale = justification. (Scale-contrast alone caps low.)

Q4 (3 marks) — Why is the process the same in a student project vs industry, despite scale?
  Both define -> research -> ideate -> produce -> evaluate (fundamental). Differs only in scale/
  resources (machinery, divided workforce, formal QC, mass vs one-off). Same process & principles,
  different scale/formality.
```

## Glossary

| Term | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| CNC | Computer Numerical Control | The computer control of machine tools to (repetitively) manufacture complex parts for a product |
| **DCIC** | Domestic, Community, Industrial, Commercial | The four settings in which design and production happen (P2.1) |
| **JBM** | Job (one-off), Batch, Mass | The three scales of production (“one, some, millions”) |
| MDP | Major Design Project | The HSC project — a product, system or environment plus its design folio — that the course is built around |
| QC | Quality Control | Checking output against quality standards (e.g. line sampling) |
| **SPPC** | Safe work practices, Production techniques, Process selection, Collaborative design teams | The industrial & commercial practices related to the MDP (H6.1) |
