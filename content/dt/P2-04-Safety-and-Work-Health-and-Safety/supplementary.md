# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces hazard vs risk,
the WHS framework, the hierarchy of control, a worked risk assessment, and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — Hazard vs risk
| | Hazard | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Anything with the **potential** to cause harm | The **likelihood** harm occurs + how **severe** it would be |
| Example | A spinning saw blade; a toxic chemical | How likely you are to be cut, and how badly |

> Manage safety by: identify the **hazard** → assess the **risk** → control it → review.

### Listing 2 — The WHS legislative framework
```text
Law:        Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW; commenced 1 Jan 2012; replaced the OHS Act)
Regulator:  Safe Work Australia

RIGHTS & RESPONSIBILITIES (two-sided — name both):
  Employer MUST provide: safe workplace, PPE, training, maintained machinery, lighting/ventilation.
  Employee has the RIGHT to a safe environment, and the RESPONSIBILITY to follow procedures, wear
    PPE, and not use machinery untrained.
  (For a student, the workshop IS your workplace.)
```

### Listing 3 — The hierarchy of control (ES-EAP): most → least effective
| Rank | Control | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **E**liminate | Remove the hazard entirely (best) | Buy timber pre-cut so no cutting is needed |
| 2 | **S**ubstitute | Replace with something safer | Water-based solvent instead of a toxic one |
| 3 | **E**ngineering | Physically guard/isolate the hazard | Blade guard, fume extraction, barrier |
| 4 | **A**dministrative | Change how people work | Training, signage, safe operating procedures |
| 5 | **P**PE | Gear worn on the body (LAST resort) | Safety glasses, gloves, ear muffs, dust mask |

> **PPE is last** because it only protects the individual wearing it (and only if worn correctly),
> whereas eliminating a hazard protects everyone. "Wear goggles" alone = bottom of the hierarchy.

### Listing 4 — Practical safety tools
```text
PPE  - anything worn to minimise risk: safety glasses, gloves, ear muffs, hard hat, dust mask,
       overalls, covered footwear.
MSDS - Material Safety Data Sheet: supplier-provided safe use/storage/disposal/first-aid info for
       a chemical. Reading it = part of safe practice.
SOP/SWP - Safe Operating Procedure / Safe Work Procedure: step-by-step safe-use instructions.
Folio - put your safety RESEARCH + a safety PLAN in your MDP folio; rethink/outsource a step if it
        is unsafe to make in a school workshop.
```

### Listing 5 — Worked risk assessment: cutting timber on a bandsaw
| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Hazard | Moving blade (serious cuts); wood dust (respiratory) |
| Risk | Contact moderately likely without controls; harm severe → HIGH risk |
| Control — Eliminate | Buy timber pre-cut to size (removes the cutting hazard) |
| Control — Substitute | Use a hand saw / guided machine instead |
| Control — Engineering | Blade guard + dust extraction |
| Control — Administrative | Training, safe operating procedure, supervision |
| Control — PPE (last) | Safety glasses + dust mask |

> Structure ANY risk-assessment answer as: hazard → risk → controls **top-down** the hierarchy.

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — Distinguish hazard from risk.
  Hazard = potential source of harm (saw blade, chemical). Risk = likelihood + severity of harm
  occurring. (The thing vs the chance/seriousness of it hurting someone.)

Q2 (4 marks) — Outline the hierarchy of control; why is PPE least preferred?
  Eliminate -> Substitute -> Engineering -> Administrative -> PPE (most→least effective). PPE last:
  it only protects the wearer (if worn correctly) and doesn't remove the hazard, whereas elimination
  protects everyone.

Q3 (5 marks) — Principles underlying safe working practices & environments.
  (1) Legal duty of care under the WHS Act 2011 — rights & responsibilities of employers/employees.
  (2) Risk management — identify/assess/control via the hierarchy of control (eliminate before PPE).
  (3) Design safety IN, not on — anticipate hazards in manufacture & use; safer materials, ergonomics.
  (Weak answer = "wear PPE"; strong = the underlying principles.)

Q4 (3 marks) — Risk assessment for using a hot soldering iron.
  Hazards: hot tip (burns), solder fumes (inhalation). Risk: moderate-high. Controls (top-down):
  engineering (soldering stand, fume extractor); administrative (training, SOP); PPE (safety glasses)
  last. Structure: hazard -> risk -> controls top-down.
```

## Glossary

| Term | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **ES-EAP** | Eliminate, Substitute, Engineering, Administrative, PPE | The hierarchy of control, most to least effective (PPE last) |
| MDP | Major Design Project | The HSC project — a product, system or environment plus its design folio — that the course is built around |
| MSDS | Material Safety Data Sheet | Describes the identity, hazard information, precautions for use and safe handling of a hazardous substance |
| NSW | New South Wales | The Australian state (e.g. the NSW WHS Act 2011) |
| OHS | Occupational Health and Safety | Workplace safety; the former framework/Act replaced by WHS (also written OH&S) |
| PPE | Personal Protective Equipment | A device or appliance designed to be worn individually to protect the user against potential hazards (the last resort in the hierarchy of control) |
| SOP | Safe Operating Procedure | Step-by-step safe-use instructions for a tool or process |
| SWP | Safe Work Procedure | Step-by-step safe-work instructions (used like an SOP) |
| WHS | Work Health and Safety | Rights & responsibilities for a safe workplace; the WHS Act 2011 sets the framework of duties workplaces must comply with |
