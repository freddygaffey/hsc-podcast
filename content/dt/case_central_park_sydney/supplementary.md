# Central Park Sydney — reading companion

![Central Park, Sydney, with its cantilevered heliostat and vertical gardens](content/dt/case_central_park_sydney/central-park.jpg)

*Central Park, Sydney — the cantilevered heliostat and Patrick Blanc's vertical gardens.*

The **"looks green but underdelivers"** case — a celebrated, award-winning design that fails to
meet its own promises over time. The best case for **life cycle analysis**, **sustainability vs
greenwashing**, and the gap between **design intent and design performance**. Use it for H1.1
(factors / success and failure) and H2.2 (impact of design on society and the environment).

## The gap between intent and performance

| Design intent | Real-world performance |
|---|---|
| Living vertical gardens (~30,000 plants) | High-maintenance; sections die and need replanting |
| Heliostat reflects light into shaded areas | Real impact debated; limited effectiveness vs the promise |
| Sustainable, low-impact "green" building | Some features use more energy/resources than expected |
| Façade greenery + shading cool the apartments | Reports of **overheating**, ventilation and privacy complaints |
| Iconic modern aesthetic | Aesthetic decline over time as green features deteriorate |
| Commercially vibrant mixed-use precinct | Retail/public spaces have struggled for consistent foot traffic |

## The big idea for the exam

> A design is judged by its **performance over its whole life**, not its intentions on opening
> day. Sustainability is a promise you must keep through years of operation and maintenance —
> **life cycle analysis** — not a rating you earn once. Central Park shows the **short-term** and
> **long-term** environmental story can diverge, and how **aesthetics can fight function** (and
> the users pay in comfort).

## Key facts

```text
What:        mixed-use urban-renewal development, Broadway/Chippendale Sydney (former brewery site)
Built:       2013; LEED Gold + 5-star green rating
Designers:   architect Jean Nouvel; vertical gardens by botanist Patrick Blanc
Features:    ~30,000-plant green walls; cantilevered heliostat (mirror array); recycled-water
             irrigation; smart lighting/ventilation; mixed residential/retail/office/dining
Shortfalls:  costly green-wall upkeep & die-off; heliostat effectiveness debated; overheating &
             comfort complaints; some "green" systems resource-hungry; aesthetic decline
Lesson:      intent ≠ performance; sustainability must be proven across the life cycle
```

## Factors affecting design illustrated

- **Short- & long-term environmental consequences** and **life cycle analysis** (the headline)
- **Aesthetics vs function** (and **ergonomics** — resident comfort, overheating, privacy)
- **Finance / quality** (high maintenance and operating costs; durability in Sydney's climate)

## Exam framing

- *Evaluate a design that claims environmental benefits — does it deliver across its life cycle?*
- *Analyse the tension between aesthetics and function in a real design.*

A strong "compare" answer pairs Central Park (sustainability that under-delivers) with the **Flow
Hive** (genuine, audited environmental responsibility — FSC timber, pollinator house, real reuse).

## Glossary

|---|---|---|
