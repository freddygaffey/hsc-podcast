QUESTION: Question one. Identify three factors that should be considered when selecting resources for a design project. Two marks. Pause the player, write your answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Three key factors are safety, whether the resource is safe to work with, use and dispose of; ethical issues, whether it is ethically sourced without exploitation; and environmental issues, whether it is sustainable and recyclable. Material properties suited to the function and cost are also valid. Two marks for naming the factors, SEE captures the three the syllabus emphasises for H4.2. Including a material property or cost shows breadth, but the marker wants safety, ethical and environmental named.

QUESTION: Question two. Explain the principles underlying safe working practices and environments. Five marks. This wording also appears in H6.1, so pause the player and write a full answer before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Safe working practices rest on several principles. First, a duty of care under work health and safety legislation, the designer is responsible for the safety of themselves and anyone affected by the work. Second, risk management: hazards must be identified, the risks they pose assessed, and then controlled. Third, the hierarchy of control, which prioritises the most effective controls, eliminating a hazard, then substituting it, then engineering controls such as guards and extraction, then administrative controls such as training and procedures, and only finally personal protective equipment, because PPE protects only the wearer while elimination removes the hazard for everyone. And fourth, the principle of designing safety in, anticipating and reducing hazards at the design and resource-selection stage rather than relying on protective gear. Five marks: name the duty of care, risk assessment, the hierarchy of control with PPE last, and designing safety in. This is the same answer that scores in H6.1.

QUESTION: Question three. Justify the selection and safe use of a resource in a design project. Five marks. This is the headline question, so pause the player and write a full answer before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer, using a finish for a children's toy. I selected a water-based, non-toxic paint, justified against the responsibility factors: it is safe, non-toxic for a child to handle and low in fumes for me to apply; ethically sourced from a supplier with fair labour practices; and environmentally preferable, with lower emissions and easier disposal than an oil-based alternative. This selection was confirmed by research into toy-safety standards and a durability test recorded in my folio. I used the resource safely by applying it in a well-ventilated area, an engineering and administrative control, in line with the hierarchy of control, and consulting the material safety data sheet. The choice is therefore justified on safety, ethical and environmental grounds, supported by research, and used safely. Five marks: justify the selection against safety, ethical and environmental factors, cite research evidence, and explain safe use via the hierarchy of control. The weak "it was cheap and available" caps low.

QUESTION: Question four. Explain why a designer should justify resource selection using research. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. A designer should justify resource selection using research because it makes the choice evidence-based rather than an assumption, demonstrating that the resource genuinely suits the function and satisfies the safety, ethical and environmental requirements. Research and experimental results, such as a material strength test or investigation into ethical sourcing, provide objective support for the decision, which is exactly what the markers reward, and reduce the risk of a poor choice failing later in the project. Justifying from research turns a preference into a defensible design decision. Three marks: state that research makes the choice evidence-based, that it provides objective support the marker rewards, and that it reduces risk. This links resource selection to the research episode.
