QUESTION: Question two. Explain how the changing nature of work has influenced design. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. The changing nature of work, from manual and factory labour toward automated, digital and increasingly remote work, has influenced design by creating demand for new products and environments suited to how people now work. For example, the shift to remote and digital work has driven the design of lightweight portable laptops, video-conferencing software and ergonomic home-office furniture, while automation has changed the design of factories and the tools workers use. Designers respond to how work changes by creating solutions appropriate to the new way of working. Three marks: identify the shift in the nature of work, explain how it changes design demand, and give an example. The marker wants the link from how-we-work to what-gets-designed.

QUESTION: Question three. Discuss how historical and cultural influences have shaped a design or innovation you have studied. Five marks. This is the headline question, so pause the player and write a full answer before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer, using the Flow Hive. The Flow Hive can only be understood against the long cultural history of beekeeping. For centuries, the established cultural practice was to harvest honey by opening the hive and using smoke to calm the bees, a tradition passed down through generations of beekeepers. The Flow Hive's innovation was a deliberate break with that history: it redesigned the honeycomb frame so honey could be drained by turning a tap, without opening the hive or disturbing the bees. This was also enabled by technological change, three-D printing and injection moulding made the complex frame possible, and it tapped a changing social trend toward sustainability and gentler treatment of nature. So historical tradition, technological change and changing social values together shaped the innovation, which is meaningful precisely as a departure from a centuries-old cultural practice. Five marks: identify specific historical and cultural influences, classify them, and show how they shaped the real innovation, acknowledging the tradition it engaged with. Vague "things changed" caps low.

QUESTION: Question four. Why must designers acknowledge cultural influences responsibly? Two marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Designers must acknowledge cultural influences responsibly because cultural material, especially Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander designs, stories and symbols, carries deep significance, and using it without understanding, permission or credit is cultural appropriation, which is disrespectful and ethically and sometimes legally wrong. Acknowledging culture responsibly means drawing on it with sensitivity and proper credit, respecting its significance to the community it belongs to. Two marks: state that cultural material has significance and that using it without respect or permission is appropriation, and that responsible acknowledgement means sensitivity and credit. The Indigenous-significance point is the high-value detail.
