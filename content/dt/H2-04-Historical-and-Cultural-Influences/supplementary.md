# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the four
influences, the acknowledge responsibility, the worked examples and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — Historical & cultural influences (C-CWT)
```text
C - Changing social trends   - society's values shift over time; design follows.
C - Cultural diversity        - multiculturalism brings diverse influences (+ a sensitivity duty).
W - changing nature of Work   - manual -> factory -> automated -> digital/remote; design responds.
T - Technological change      - each era's available tech shapes what can be designed.

These are the DEEP, long-term version of trends = the "era" (2nd E) of TEMPLE.
```

### Listing 2 — How each influence shapes design
| Influence | Example |
|---|---|
| Changing social trends | Shifting values → what's desirable/acceptable changes |
| Cultural diversity | Multicultural influences; e.g. Indigenous storytelling in graphic design (respectfully) |
| Changing nature of work | Remote/digital work → laptops, video-conferencing, home-office furniture |
| Technological change | Miniaturised electronics/touchscreens/wireless → the smartphone |

### Listing 3 — Acknowledging cultural influence (a responsibility)
```text
The verb is IDENTIFY *and* ACKNOWLEDGE — acknowledge = recognise + RESPECT.

Cultural material (esp. Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander designs/stories/symbols) carries deep
SIGNIFICANCE + etiquette. Using it without permission/understanding/credit = CULTURAL APPROPRIATION
(disrespectful; ethically & sometimes legally wrong).

Acknowledge responsibly = draw on culture with sensitivity & proper credit. (Links to ethics — H2-05.)
```

### Listing 4 — Worked examples: innovation vs its historical/cultural moment
```text
SMARTPHONE — changing nature of WORK + TECHNOLOGICAL CHANGE:
  as work became mobile/digital, and as miniaturised electronics/touchscreens/wireless emerged,
  the smartphone became both possible and necessary — a product of its technological era.

FLOW HIVE — historical/cultural TRADITION:
  for centuries beekeepers harvested by opening the hive + smoking the bees. The Flow Hive's
  innovation = a deliberate BREAK with that tradition (harvest by a tap, no disturbance).
  Only understandable against the cultural history it departed from.
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "How have historical & cultural influences shaped a design?"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "In the past things were different and design changed." -> vague.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - NAME a specific historical/cultural influence + classify it (C-CWT).
  - Show how it shaped a REAL design/innovation, ACKNOWLEDGING the source.
  - Note both the historical AND cultural dimension where relevant.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — Historical & cultural influences on design.
  Changing social trends; cultural diversity; the changing nature of work; technological change. [C-CWT]

Q2 (3 marks) — How has the changing nature of work influenced design?
  Manual/factory -> automated/digital/remote work -> demand for new products: laptops, video-
  conferencing, ergonomic home-office furniture; automation reshapes factories & tools. How-we-work
  drives what-gets-designed.

Q3 (5 marks) — How have historical & cultural influences shaped an innovation?
  Flow Hive: breaks a centuries-old beekeeping TRADITION (open hive + smoke); enabled by TECHNOLOGICAL
  CHANGE (3D printing/injection moulding); taps a CHANGING SOCIAL TREND toward sustainability. Tradition
  + tech + values together shaped it — meaningful as a departure from cultural practice. (Vague caps low.)

Q4 (2 marks) — Why acknowledge cultural influences responsibly?
  Cultural material (esp. Indigenous) has deep significance; using it without permission/credit =
  appropriation (disrespectful, ethically/legally wrong). Acknowledge = sensitivity + credit.
```
