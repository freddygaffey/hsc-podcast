# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces need vs
opportunity, the needs analysis, the proposal (NICe), the worked example and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — Need vs opportunity (+ niche)
```text
NEED        - usually CONSUMER-DRIVEN; the designer responds to a real problem/demand of a market.
OPPORTUNITY - a GAP the designer spots and chooses to pursue (may offer profit / serve needs better).
NICHE       - a small, specialised group of consumers for whom NO adequate product exists
              (a strong basis for an MDP).
```

### Listing 2 — The needs analysis (a questioning exercise)
```text
PURPOSE: clarify and define the design task BEFORE designing; confirm a genuine need; find the gap.

KEY QUESTIONS:
  - Situation/context: what provoked this need? external influences?
  - Type of need: personal / community / environmental / other?
  - Multiple needs? prioritise them.
  - Users: who is the target market + their demographics?
  - Required impact: what must the solution do?
  - The gap: what existing products are there & how do they FAIL to meet the need? (a niche?)

Confirm the need is genuine via initial (market) research.
```

### Listing 3 — The project proposal: NICe
| Letter | Component | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **N** | Need | Identification & exploration of the need — stated, evidenced (who, what problem) |
| **I** | Investigation areas | What you'll research to solve it (materials, products, users, techniques) |
| **C** | Criteria to evaluate success | Measurable standards the project must meet (functional + aesthetic) |

> Builds on the module-3 brief (NCC). The CRITERIA here = what you evaluate against in phase 3
> (proposal ↔ evaluation BOOKENDS) → so they must be MEASURABLE.

### Listing 4 — Worked MDP proposal: a space-saving raised garden planter
```text
NEED (identified & explored):
  Gardeners with limited space/mobility struggle to garden at ground level. Needs analysis of older
  & apartment gardeners + research (existing raised planters are expensive, hard to assemble)
  confirms a genuine gap.

AREAS OF INVESTIGATION:
  ergonomics of working height · lightweight durable weatherproof materials · simple assembly ·
  water management · cost.

CRITERIA TO EVALUATE SUCCESS (measurable):
  - allows gardening seated/standing without bending (~waist height)
  - assembled by one person, no tools, < 30 min
  - withstands outdoor weather >= 5 years
  - retails < $150
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "How was a need identified & developed into a proposal?"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "I wanted to make a lamp, so I designed one." -> a wish, not an analysed need.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - Identify need (consumer-driven) OR opportunity (gap/niche).
  - EXPLORE via a needs analysis (situation, target market, required function, gap vs existing
    products) + confirm by research.
  - FORMULATE the NICe proposal DERIVED from the analysis (need + investigation areas + measurable
    criteria).
  Analyse the problem BEFORE naming the solution.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (3 marks) — Three components of a proposal.
  Identification & exploration of the need; areas of investigation; criteria to evaluate success. [NICe]

Q2 (3 marks) — Purpose of a needs analysis.
  A questioning exercise that clarifies/defines the task; examines situation, target market, required
  function, gap vs existing products; confirms a genuine need & identifies the niche; sets what the
  solution must do.

Q3 (5 marks) — How is a need identified & developed into a proposal?
  Identify need/opportunity -> explore via needs analysis (target market, gap) confirmed by research
  -> formulate NICe proposal (explored need + investigation areas + measurable criteria) derived from
  the analysis. (Jumping to "I wanted to build X" caps low.)

Q4 (3 marks) — Why must success criteria be measurable?
  They're evaluated against in phase 3 (proposal<->evaluation bookends). Measurable (e.g. "supports
  100 kg", "< $80") -> objective judgement; vague ("good quality") -> can't be evaluated.
```
