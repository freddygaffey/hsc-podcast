# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the three plans,
formulate-apply-evaluate, the trade-off, the worked scenario and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — The three management plans (ATF) — H5.1
| Plan | What it is |
|---|---|
| **A**ction plan | Documented list of every task, broken down & sequenced (first step) |
| **T**ime plan = Gantt chart | Tasks scheduled vs calendar; start/duration/end; dependencies; **critical path** |
| **F**inance plan | Documented budget of all costs, tracked to stay affordable |

> All three are DOCUMENTED in the folio (process evidence — H1-01).

### Listing 2 — Formulate → Apply → Evaluate (the H5.1 verbs)
```text
FORMULATE - make the three plans at the start.
APPLY     - use them to actually run the project.
EVALUATE  - MONITOR actual progress & spending vs the plans; REVISE when a task slips / cost overruns.

A Gantt chart drawn once and never checked is NOT management. The monitoring & revising = half the marks.
```

### Listing 3 — The time / money / quality trade-off
```text
TIME, MONEY and QUALITY are interdependent — change one, affect the others.
  A task overruns -> spend more $ OR lower quality OR extend time (but the HSC DEADLINE is fixed).
=> Manage time & finance to PROTECT QUALITY: plan realistically, catch slippage EARLY, absorb it.
```

### Listing 4 — Worked MDP management scenario (a slipped task)
```text
ACTION: research -> design -> source materials -> build -> finish -> evaluate (broken into tasks).
TIME (Gantt): build depends on materials depends on design = the CRITICAL PATH.
FINANCE: materials $60 + finishing $20 = $80, tracked.

SLIP: timber supplier out of stock 1 week -> Gantt shows build + everything after threatened.
RESPONSE (evaluate & revise): bring forward independent tasks (finalise drawings, prep finish) ->
delay absorbed, deadline protected, still within budget.

= all three plans formulated, applied AND evaluated -> on time, on budget, at quality.
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "How did management plans ensure successful completion?"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "I made a timeline so I knew what to do." -> one plan, no monitoring.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - Three plans: action (tasks) + Gantt (schedule + critical path) + finance (budget).
  - APPLIED & EVALUATED: monitor vs the plan; when a material was delayed, re-sequence independent
    tasks to protect the deadline AND quality.
  Formulate, apply, and especially EVALUATE.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (3 marks) — Three management plans.
  Action plan (list & sequence tasks); time plan = Gantt chart (schedule vs calendar); finance plan
  (budget & track costs). [ATF]

Q2 (4 marks) — Why is a Gantt chart valuable?
  Schedules tasks vs time (start/duration/end); shows dependencies & parallel tasks; identifies the
  CRITICAL PATH (minimum duration); used to MONITOR actual vs planned & re-sequence -> protects the
  deadline & quality.

Q3 (5 marks) — How do management plans ensure successful completion?
  Action (all tasks) + Gantt (schedule, critical path) + finance (budget). FORMULATE, APPLY & EVALUATE:
  monitor vs the plans; a delayed material -> re-sequence independent tasks -> protect deadline &
  quality. On-time, on-budget completion. (A "timeline" answer caps low.)

Q4 (3 marks) — Relationship between time, finance & quality.
  Interdependent: a task overrun -> spend more / lower quality / extend time (deadline usually fixed).
  Good management plans realistically & catches slippage early to PROTECT quality within time & budget.
```
