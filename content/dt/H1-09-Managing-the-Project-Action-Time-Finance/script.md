QUESTION: Question one. Identify the three types of management plan used in a major design project. Three marks. Pause the player, write your answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. The three management plans are the action plan, which lists and sequences all the tasks; the time plan, usually a Gantt chart, which schedules the tasks against the calendar; and the finance plan, which budgets and tracks all the costs. Three marks, one per plan with its purpose, ATF. Naming the Gantt chart as the time plan and noting the finance plan tracks costs shows you understand what each does.

QUESTION: Question two. Explain why a Gantt chart is a valuable management tool for the major design project. Four marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. A Gantt chart is valuable because it schedules every task against a timeline, showing each task's start, duration and end, and makes the dependencies visible, which tasks must be completed before others can begin, and which can run in parallel. It identifies the critical path, the chain of dependent tasks that determines the minimum time to complete the project, so the student knows which delays would threaten the deadline. Above all, it is used throughout to monitor actual progress against the plan, so that when a task slips the student can re-sequence work and protect the completion date and the quality of the project. Four marks: explain scheduling and dependencies, the critical path, and monitoring and re-planning. The monitoring and critical path lift it to full marks.

QUESTION: Question three. Explain how management plans are used to ensure the successful completion of a major design project. Five marks. This is the headline question, so pause the player and write a full answer before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. The three management plans work together to keep the project on track. An action plan lists and sequences every task so nothing is overlooked. A time plan, a Gantt chart, schedules those tasks, reveals their dependencies and identifies the critical path, allowing the student to see whether the project is on schedule. A finance plan budgets and tracks costs so the project remains affordable. Crucially, these plans are not just formulated but applied and evaluated throughout: the student monitors real progress and spending against them, and when a problem arises, such as a delayed material delivery, the Gantt chart immediately reveals the impact, so the student re-sequences independent tasks to absorb the delay and protect both the deadline and the quality of the final product. By formulating, applying and evaluating the action, time and finance plans, the student manages the project to a successful, on-time, on-budget completion. Five marks: cover the three plans, and stress the apply-and-evaluate monitoring with an example of revising in response. A "made a timeline" answer caps low.

QUESTION: Question four. Explain the relationship between time, finance and quality in managing a project. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Time, finance and quality are interdependent in project management, changing one affects the others. If a task overruns its time, the student must either spend more money, for example on faster materials, accept a reduction in quality, or extend the deadline, which on a fixed HSC submission date is usually not possible. Good management therefore plans realistically and monitors progress so that slippage is caught early and absorbed, for example by re-sequencing tasks, in order to protect the quality of the final project within the available time and budget. Three marks: state that the three are interdependent and trade off, that the deadline is often fixed, and that management protects quality by catching slippage early. The insight is the trade-off, with quality as what you protect.
