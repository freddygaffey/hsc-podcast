# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken in the audio — it reinforces
the stages, the cyclic nature, the worked example and the model answers from the narration.

### Listing 1 — DRIPER: the six stages of the design process
| Stage | Name | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **D** | Define | Identify and state the need/opportunity; write the design brief and criteria for success ("initial contact with clients") |
| **R** | Research | Investigate: market research, existing products, materials & properties, users; analyse how similar problems were solved |
| **I** | Ideate | Generate many ideas — sketching, brainstorming, SCAMPER; quantity before quality |
| **P** | Produce | Develop the chosen idea; model, prototype, and realise the solution (design practice) |
| **E** | Evaluate | Test against the criteria — *continually*, not just at the end |
| **R** | Refine | Improve based on evaluation and **loop back** to an earlier stage; repeat the cycle |

### Listing 2 — Why it is a CYCLE, not a line (the "analyse" mark)
```text
LINEAR (wrong):  Define -> Research -> Ideate -> Produce -> Evaluate -> Refine -> DONE
CYCLIC (right):  the designer RETURNS to earlier stages; evaluation is CONTINUAL.

Feedback loops:
  - Evaluate finds a fault -> loop back to IDEATE (rethink the idea)
  - A deeper fault         -> loop back to RESEARCH (revise the approach)
  - Re-Produce -> Re-Evaluate -> repeat until the criteria are met.

Textbook: "often a cyclic process, with the designer returning to different stages...
not always in the same order. Successful designers test, reflect and evaluate constantly."
Effect of the looping: the design is PROGRESSIVELY IMPROVED until it meets the need.
```

### Listing 3 — Headline question: "Describe AND analyse the processes undertaken when designing"
```text
WEAK answer (band ~3-4 — describe only):
  Lists DRIPER as a fixed recipe, top to bottom, once. No looping.

STRONG answer (band ~6 — describe + analyse):
  - DESCRIBE: name and explain each stage (Define/Research/Ideate/Produce/Evaluate/Refine).
  - ANALYSE: explain it is ITERATIVE/CYCLIC -> evaluation is continual -> failures loop the
    designer back to an earlier stage -> the cycle repeats -> the design is progressively
    improved until it meets the criteria.

The verb tells you how much: "describe" = the stages; "analyse" = the looping behaviour too.
```

### Listing 4 — Worked example: a reusable drinks bottle through DRIPER
| Stage | What the designer does | Possible loop-back |
|---|---|---|
| Define | Need: durable, leak-proof, reusable bottle to cut single-use plastic. Criteria: ~750 mL, leak-proof, keeps cold for hours, easy to clean | — |
| Research | Existing bottles & weaknesses; materials (stainless steel vs aluminium vs plastic) & their properties + environmental footprint; user complaints (leaky lids, cup-holder fit) | — |
| Ideate | Sketch many concepts: lid mechanisms, shapes, single- vs double-wall insulation | — |
| Produce | Build prototype: double-walled stainless steel, screw lid | — |
| Evaluate | Test vs criteria → **screw lid leaks on its side** (fails a criterion) | → back to **Ideate** (new lid) |
| Refine → loop | Silicone-sealed flip lid → re-Produce → re-Evaluate → insulation underperforms | → back to **Research** (wall design) |

> Each loop improves the bottle. Describing these loop-backs IS the "analyse" mark.

### Listing 5 — The same process across settings (preview of P2 / DCIC)
| Setting | Who / scale | The process | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic | An individual at home | DRIPER | Small scale, limited resources, informal, little documentation |
| Community | A club, school, local group | DRIPER | Shared effort, modest resources |
| Industrial | A factory, mass production | DRIPER | Large budgets, teams, specialised equipment, rigorous documented evaluation |
| Commercial | A business designing for market | DRIPER | Driven by selling; market research & marketing prominent |

> Key point: the **process is the same**; only the **scale, resources, formality and
> documentation** change. (Settings expanded in P2-01.)

### Listing 7 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (3 marks) — Outline the stages of the design process.
  Define (need/brief) -> Research/investigate -> Ideate (generate ideas) -> Produce/prototype
  -> Evaluate vs criteria -> Refine. Add: "the process is cyclic — the designer returns to
  earlier stages."

Q2 (3 marks) — Explain why the design process is iterative.
  Not completed once in a fixed order; evaluation is continual; failures loop back to an earlier
  stage (regenerate ideas / revise research) and the cycle repeats -> progressively improves the
  design until it meets the need.

Q3 (6 marks) — Describe AND analyse the processes undertaken when designing a product.
  DESCRIBE the stages applied to a real product (e.g. reusable bottle: define criteria; research
  materials/users; ideate concepts; produce prototype; evaluate; refine). ANALYSE the loop: a
  leaking lid sent the designer back to ideate; an insulation failure back to research; continual
  evaluation + looping improved it until all criteria were met. (Half marks if you only describe.)

Q4 (4 marks) — Compare the design process in industrial vs domestic settings.
  Same process (DRIPER) in both. Industrial: teams, large budgets, formal management of action/
  time/finance, specialised equipment, rigorous documented evaluation (mass production, costly
  failure). Domestic: individual, small scale, informal, little documentation. Only scale &
  formality change, not the process.
```
