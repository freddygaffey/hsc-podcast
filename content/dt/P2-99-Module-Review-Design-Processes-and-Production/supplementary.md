# Supplementary Materials

The one-page revision sheet for module P2: the production pipeline, the six mnemonics, key terms,
traps, the forward map, and integrated model answers. Nothing here is spoken.

### Listing 1 — The P2 production pipeline (the synthesis)
```text
WHERE / WHO       (setting)      -> DCIC: domestic / community / industrial / commercial
HOW MANY          (scale)        -> JBM: job / batch / mass
WHAT FROM         (materials)    -> CPA FACE; match PROPERTIES to FUNCTION
MADE SAFELY       (WHS)          -> hazard/risk; hierarchy of control (ES-EAP), PPE last
WITH WHAT IMPACT  (social/env)   -> 3 levels (individual/society/environment); PC SCIE
USING WHAT TOOLS  (computers)    -> MR SCP; CAD -> CAM -> 3D printing

A strong production answer can WALK this chain: where -> how many -> what from -> how safely ->
what impact -> what tools.
```

### Listing 2 — The six module mnemonics
```text
DCIC     four settings: Domestic, Community, Industrial, Commercial (community = common good, not profit)
JBM      three scales: Job (one-off), Batch, Mass  ("one, some, millions")
CPA FACE material selection: Characteristics, Properties, Availability, Function, Appearance, Cost, Environmental
ES-EAP   hierarchy of control: Eliminate, Substitute, Engineering, Administrative, PPE (most→least; PPE last)
PC SCIE  social/env issues: Personal values, Cultural beliefs, Sustainability, Safety & health,
                            Community needs, Individual needs, Equity
MR SCP   computer applications: Modelling, Research, Simulation & graphics, Communication, Presentation
```

### Listing 3 — Key terms by topic
| Topic | Must-know |
|---|---|
| Settings (P2-01) | Same process in all four; scale/resources/formality/QC differ; community = common good not profit |
| Scales (P2-02) | Job = one-off/flexible/high cost-per-unit; batch = limited runs w/ jigs; mass = standardised/interchangeable/economies of scale |
| Materials (P2-03) | Characteristics (what it's like) vs properties (how it behaves); match properties to function; CPA FACE |
| Safety (P2-04) | Hazard (potential harm) vs risk (likelihood + severity); WHS Act 2011; hierarchy of control, PPE last |
| Impact (P2-05) | 3 levels (individual/society/environment); sustainability = present needs w/o compromising future; equity = fair access |
| Technology (P2-06) | MR SCP; CAD→CAM→3D printing; simulation replaces physical prototypes; discriminate |

### Listing 4 — Traps across the module
| Trap | Keep straight |
|---|---|
| Setting vs scale | Setting = where/by whom (DCIC); scale = how many (JBM). Related but independent. |
| Batch vs mass | Batch = limited, flexible runs; mass = large, standardised, costly to change |
| Hazard vs risk | Hazard = the potential source of harm; risk = likelihood + severity |
| PPE first | PPE is the LAST resort — work the hierarchy from the top (eliminate first) |
| Vague sustainability | = meeting present needs without compromising the future (not just "green") |
| Characteristics vs properties | What a material is like vs how it behaves |

### Listing 6 — Integrated model answer (setting + scale + materials → impact)
```text
Q: How did the choice of setting, materials and processes affect a product's impact on society
   & the environment? (6 marks) — mass-produced single-use plastic bottle

- Setting + scale: industrial, mass production -> millions of identical units cheaply via automated
  injection moulding (economies of scale).
- This drove MATERIAL choice: cheap, light, mouldable polymer suited to high volume.
- IMPACT: socially accessible/cheap (benefit) but a throwaway culture; environmentally, scale
  MULTIPLIES harm -> waste, microplastics, fossil fuels (severe long-term impact on LCA).
- Judgement: the same choices that made it affordable drive its environmental damage at scale.

Marks reward LINKING setting/scale -> material -> impact, with a judgement (not 4 separate topics).
```

### Listing 7 — Model answers to the other review questions
```text
Q2 (5) — Compare domestic vs industrial (scale, resources, QC).
  Same process. Scale: one-off vs mass. Resources: basic tools/1 person vs machinery/divided workforce/
  capital. QC: informal vs rigorous & documented. Example: home chair-maker vs chair factory.

Q3 (6) — Select & safely use a material (criteria + hierarchy of control).
  Select: match properties to function + CPA FACE + experimentation (e.g. stainless steel for a bottle).
  Safe use: risk assessment + hierarchy (eliminate -> substitute -> engineering -> admin -> PPE last).

Q4 (3) — Why is PPE the least effective control?
  Only protects the wearer (if worn correctly); doesn't remove the hazard. Higher controls act on the
  hazard for everyone (elimination removes risk entirely). PPE = last line of defence.

Q5 (5) — How have computer-based technologies changed design?
  CAD modelling (test without model-making); simulation (virtual testing saves time/cost/waste);
  communication (remote collaboration); CAD->CAM->3D printing (digital model drives manufacture);
  must discriminate. Faster, more accurate, collaborative, testable.

Q6 (2) — Distinguish production setting from production scale.
  Setting = where/by whom (domestic/community/industrial/commercial). Scale = how many (job/batch/mass).
  Related but independent.

Q7 (5) — Assess the environmental impact of mass production (sustainability).
  Both sides: per-unit efficiency + optimisable waste/energy VS scale multiplies harm (raw materials,
  energy, throwaway waste — via LCA). Conditional judgement: durable/recyclable mass goods can be
  sustainable; cheap single-use ones are not. Frame with sustainability = present needs w/o
  compromising the future.

Q8 (3) — Recommend a scale for 200 identical wooden toys + justify.
  Batch production — a limited defined quantity (too many for job, too few to justify mass tooling).
  Jigs/templates reproduce the 200 consistently & efficiently; design still variable for a future
  batch. Match scale to quantity with a reason.
```

## Glossary

| Term | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| AI | Artificial Intelligence | An emerging technology |
| CAD | Computer-Aided Design | An automated system for the design, drafting and display of graphic information |
| CAM | Computer-Aided Manufacturing | A computer program that makes manufacturing data from CAD drawings to automate manufacture by a computer-controlled machine |
| **CPA FACE** | Characteristics, Properties, Availability, Function, Appearance, Cost, Environmental | Criteria for selecting a material (P4.2) |
| **DCIC** | Domestic, Community, Industrial, Commercial | The four settings in which design and production happen (P2.1) |
| **ES-EAP** | Eliminate, Substitute, Engineering, Administrative, PPE | The hierarchy of control, most to least effective (PPE last) |
| **JBM** | Job (one-off), Batch, Mass | The three scales of production (“one, some, millions”) |
| LCA | Life Cycle Analysis | Evaluation of the resources and impacts across a product's whole life (cradle-to-grave); one of the factors affecting design |
| MDP | Major Design Project | The HSC project — a product, system or environment plus its design folio — that the course is built around |
| **MR SCP** | Modelling, Research, Simulation & graphics, Communication, Presentation | The five applications of computer-based technologies (P6.2) |
| **PC SCIE** | Personal values, Cultural beliefs, Sustainability, Safety & health, Community needs, Individual needs, Equity | The seven environmental & social issues (P2.2) |
| PPE | Personal Protective Equipment | A device or appliance designed to be worn individually to protect the user against potential hazards (the last resort in the hierarchy of control) |
| QC | Quality Control | Checking output against quality standards (e.g. line sampling) |
| WHS | Work Health and Safety | Rights & responsibilities for a safe workplace; the WHS Act 2011 sets the framework of duties workplaces must comply with |
