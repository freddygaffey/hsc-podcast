# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the two halves of
H4.3, the worked evaluation, and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — H4.3: the two halves of evaluation
```text
HALF 1 — Evaluate against the CRITERIA FOR SUCCESS (set in the proposal, H1-02):
  functional + aesthetic; use a CRITERIA CHECKLIST; criteria must be MEASURABLE; analyse criteria
  (some matter more than others). -> closes the PROPOSAL ↔ EVALUATION bookend.

HALF 2 — Evaluate the IMPACT of the project (see Listing 3).

Both halves are required.
```

### Listing 2 — Evaluation is ONGOING (BMA)
```text
B - BEFORE: set the criteria (at the proposal).
M - MID:    evaluate continually during design & production (test, refine).
A - AFTER:  final judgement of the finished product vs all criteria.

Evaluation is ongoing & occurs throughout — not just a final report.
```

### Listing 3 — Impact of the MDP (ISE) — costs AND benefits
```text
Assess impact at THREE levels (ISE), LOCAL and GLOBAL, weighing COSTS and BENEFITS:
  INDIVIDUAL  - the user (benefit: useful product; cost: purchase price)
  SOCIETY     - the wider community (benefit: inclusive/useful; cost: resources/social)
  ENVIRONMENT - materials, production, disposal footprint (vs renewable/recyclable choices)

"It helps people" is not enough — weigh positive AND negative at each level.
```

### Listing 4 — Worked example: evaluating an accessible storage unit
```text
AGAINST CRITERIA (success):
  - supports 100 kg            -> MET (load tested)
  - tool-free assembly < 30 min-> MET (25 min)
  - cost < $150                -> PARTLY MET ($160, minor overrun)

IMPACT (ISE, costs & benefits):
  Individual  - accessible storage, improves independence | cost: purchase price
  Society     - promotes inclusive design, benefits many  | cost: resources consumed
  Environment - timber/fixings footprint (extraction/disposal) | offset by renewable/recyclable choice

Overall: met most criteria; net positive impact with a modest environmental cost.
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "Evaluate vs criteria AND assess impact"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "It worked well and people liked it." -> no criteria, no impact analysis.

STRONG answer (band ~6) — TWO halves:
  1. Judge vs EACH named criterion, with evidence (load test; finish fell short...).
  2. Assess IMPACT across Individual/Society/Environment, with COSTS and BENEFITS.
  Reach an overall judgement.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — What is a finished MDP evaluated against?
  The criteria for success from the proposal — functional (what it does) + aesthetic (how it looks),
  judged objectively (measurable).

Q2 (3 marks) — Why should evaluation be ongoing?
  Catches problems early (cheap to fix), drives refinement, keeps alignment to criteria. Before (set
  criteria) / mid (test & refine) / after (final judgement) — not end-only. [BMA]

Q3 (6 marks) — Evaluate vs criteria AND assess impact (individual/society/environment).
  CRITERIA: met 100 kg (load test), tool-free assembly in 25 min; partly met cost ($160). IMPACT:
  individual (accessible storage / purchase cost), society (inclusive design / resource use),
  environment (material footprint / renewable choice). Overall: met most criteria, net positive impact.
  Both halves + judgement.

Q4 (3 marks) — Why weigh both costs and benefits?
  Most designs have positive AND negative impacts; "evaluate" demands a balanced judgement. Benefits-
  only overstates value & ignores real costs; costs-only misses the good. Weigh both at each level ->
  fair judgement.
```

## Glossary

| Term | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **BMA** | Before, Mid, After | Evaluation is continual — before, during and after, not only at the end (H4.3) |
| **ISE** | Individual, Society, Environment | The three levels at which to assess a design's impact (H4.3 / H2.2) |
| MDP | Major Design Project | The HSC project — a product, system or environment plus its design folio — that the course is built around |
