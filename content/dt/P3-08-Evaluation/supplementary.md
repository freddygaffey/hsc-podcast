# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces what you evaluate
against, the methods, the continual nature, the worked example and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — What you evaluate against
```text
You evaluate a design against its CRITERIA FOR SUCCESS — the functional and aesthetic criteria
set in the design brief (P3-03).

Evaluation = determining HOW SUCCESSFULLY those criteria are met.
-> only works if the criteria are MEASURABLE.
```

### Listing 2 — The criteria-checklist method
| Criterion | How success is measured |
|---|---|
| (Functional) must not leak | Invert full bottle for 1 min — no drips |
| (Functional) keeps drink cold 6 h | Measure temperature after 6 hours |
| (Aesthetic) premium look, colour range | Client/consumer feedback; 4+ colours available |

> Tick each criterion off as you check it — turns "is it good?" into "did it meet criteria 1,2,3?"

### Listing 3 — Evaluation is CONTINUAL (BMA) + the Evaluate–Refine loop
```text
B - BEFORE: establish the criteria (at the brief stage) — define what success looks like.
M - MID:    evaluate continually DURING design & production — test, check, refine, amend
            materials/methods. (This is the part students forget — and it's worth the most.)
A - AFTER:  final judgement of the finished product against all criteria.

Continual evaluation = the EVALUATE -> REFINE loop of DRIPER running over and over.
```

### Listing 4 — Methods of evaluation
```text
- Testing the product (leak test, load test) — does it physically perform?
- Checking against the CRITERIA CHECKLIST
- Client FEEDBACK (does it meet their need?)
- Surveying CONSUMERS / users
- KEEP EVIDENCE: e.g. samples of failed test runs as proof of your evaluation (folio gold).

(Overlaps with research methods — testing & surveys are used here to evaluate.)
```

### Listing 5 — Worked example: before/mid/after evaluation of a drink bottle
```text
Criteria: (1) no leak  (2) cold for 6 h  (3) cost < $25

BEFORE: criteria set in the brief (measurable).
MID:    prototype leak-tested -> FAILS inverted -> identify lid -> refine to sealed flip lid.
        mid-build costing -> over budget -> substitute a cheaper material.
AFTER:  finished bottle tested vs all 3 -> no leak, cold 7 h, costs $22 -> success on all three.
        (Failed first-lid test kept as evidence.)

Mid-project evaluation changed TWO decisions before they became costly mistakes.
```

### Listing 6 — Headline question: "Why should evaluation be ongoing?"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low — contradicts the point):
  "So you can check at the end if it worked."

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - Catches problems EARLY (cheap to fix vs after build).
  - Drives the REFINE loop -> design improves progressively.
  - Keeps the project ALIGNED to the brief's criteria throughout.
  - Builds an EVIDENCE TRAIL of tests/decisions (markers reward it).
  + example: a mid-project leak test failing -> lid redesign before production.
```

### Listing 7 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — What/how does a designer evaluate against?
  Against the brief's criteria for success (functional + aesthetic), via a criteria checklist +
  testing/client feedback/consumer surveys.

Q2 (2 marks) — Three evaluation methods.
  Any 3: testing (leak/load); criteria checklist; client feedback; consumer surveys.

Q3 (5 marks) — Why evaluate throughout, not only at the end?
  Catches problems early (cheap to fix); drives the refine loop (progressive improvement); keeps
  alignment to the brief's criteria; builds an evidence trail. Example: mid-project leak test -> lid
  redesign before production. (Contrast with too-late end-only evaluation.)

Q4 (3 marks) — Relationship between the brief and evaluation.
  Bookends: the brief SETS the criteria for success at the start; evaluation JUDGES the design
  against exactly those criteria (during & at the end). Hence criteria must be measurable.
```
