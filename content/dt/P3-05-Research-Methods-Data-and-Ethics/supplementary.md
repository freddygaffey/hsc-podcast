# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the
qualitative/quantitative split, the methods, data, ethics, the worked example and model answers.

### Listing 1 — Qualitative vs quantitative research
| | Qualitative | Quantitative |
|---|---|---|
| Gathers | Feelings, opinions, reasons — the **why** | Counts & measurements — the **how many/much** |
| Analysis | Grouped by theme; descriptive | Statistical |
| Presented as | Text | Graphs, tables |
| Classic method | Interview | Survey |

> Validating a design usually needs BOTH — numbers to prove a trend + opinions to explain it.

### Listing 2 — Research methods (QISOTS) + information research
| Letter | Method | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| **Q** | Questionnaire (open or closed questions; reaches many) | Either |
| **I** | Interview (conversation; formal/informal; depth) | Qualitative |
| **S** | Survey (systematic, often numerical, across a sample) | Quantitative |
| **O** | Observation (watch real behaviour/use) | Qualitative |
| **T** | Tests & experiments (measure real performance) | Quantitative |
| **S** | Statistical analysis (find patterns/trends) | Quantitative |

> Plus **information research** (print & electronic sources) = secondary research (P3-04).

### Listing 3 — Choose the method to suit the question
```text
"How strong is this joint?"           -> test/experiment   (quantitative; table)
"Do users find it comfortable?"       -> interview/observe  (qualitative)
"How many would buy it, at what price?" -> survey            (quantitative; graph)

The method follows the QUESTION. A good project uses a VARIETY of methods.
```

### Listing 4 — Interpreting & presenting data
```text
Quantitative -> tables, graphs, spreadsheets (patterns visible; statistically analysed)
Qualitative  -> summarised & grouped by theme

VALIDITY   = does the research measure what it claims to?
RELIABILITY = would it give consistent results if repeated?
  -> use adequate, representative samples; avoid leading questions.
APPLY the data: research only has value when findings CHANGE a design decision.
```

### Listing 5 — Ethics in research (CVA + honesty + no harm)
```text
C - informed CONSENT      (participants understand & agree to take part)
V - VOLUNTARY participation (no pressure; can withdraw)
A - ANONYMITY & privacy    (protect identities & personal info)
+ HONESTY  (report findings truthfully; no fabrication/cherry-picking)
+ NO HARM  (research must not endanger anyone)

A folio that surveys people should note how consent & privacy were handled.
```

### Listing 6 — Worked example: research questions → methods (a new chair)
| Question | Method | Data / presentation |
|---|---|---|
| How much weight can the frame hold? | Test / experiment | Quantitative → table |
| Is it comfortable over long use? | Observation + interview | Qualitative |
| How many would buy it, at what price? | Survey across a sample | Quantitative → graph |

> Same project, 3 questions, 3 methods — each chosen to fit; data interpreted & applied to refine.

### Listing 7 — Headline question: "How can testing & experimenting improve a project?" (2022)
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "You test it to see if it works."

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - Testing/experimenting is a research method producing OBJECTIVE PERFORMANCE DATA.
  - Test against the success CRITERIA (e.g. load-test a joint; compare materials).
  - Findings are FED BACK to refine the design (evaluate-and-refine loop).
  - Result: more reliable, safer, better-suited final solution. (Ethics if people are involved.)
```

### Listing 8 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (3 marks) — Distinguish qualitative vs quantitative research.
  Qualitative = non-numerical opinions/reasons (the why), via interviews, text. Quantitative =
  numerical, counted & statistically analysed (how many), via surveys, graphs/tables. Descriptive
  vs measurable; use both.

Q2 (3 marks) — Three methods + the data each produces.
  E.g. interview (qualitative), survey (quantitative), observation (qualitative). [QISOTS] Pair each
  method with its data type.

Q3 (4 marks) — How does testing & experimenting improve a project?
  Produces objective performance data; tests against criteria; findings fed back to refine (evaluate-
  and-refine loop); -> more reliable/safe/fit-for-need final solution. (Not just "to check it works".)

Q4 (3 marks) — Why consider ethics in research involving people?
  Protects participants' rights/wellbeing: informed consent, voluntary participation, anonymity/
  privacy; plus honesty (no fabrication) and no harm. Unethical research is also often unreliable/
  invalid. (CVA.)
```
