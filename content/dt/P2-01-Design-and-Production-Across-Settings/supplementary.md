# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces the four
settings, what varies vs stays the same, the worked example and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — The four settings (DCIC): a ladder from small/informal to large/formal
| Setting | Who / where | Scale & nature | Motive | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D**omestic | An individual, in a home or small workshop | Small-scale / one-off; simple tools; low cost | Income / client orders | Faye Cahill, cake designer, starting from home |
| **C**ommunity | A community group or cooperative (co-op) | Scale varies with community need; shared labour | **Common good, NOT primarily profit** | Community garden growing local produce (cuts food miles) |
| **I**ndustrial | A factory; many workers | Large-scale mass production; big machinery | Profit (economies of scale) | Food factory producing thousands of identical units |
| **C**ommercial | A business designing for a market | Driven by selling competitively | Profit via the market | A company designing a product to sell to a target market |

### Listing 2 — Same process, different variables (the backbone of the topic)
```text
STAYS THE SAME across all four settings:
  The DESIGN PROCESS — DRIPER (Define -> Research -> Ideate -> Produce -> Evaluate -> Refine).

CHANGES between settings (the variables):
  - Scale of production (one-off ... mass)
  - Size of the company / plant size
  - Manufacturing techniques & machinery
  - Division of labour (one person does all  ...  each does one specialised task)
  - Level of formality
  - Documentation & quality control (informal  ...  rigorous & documented)
  - Funds / resources available

=> A factory does NOT use a different process from a home maker; it uses the SAME process
   with more scale, resources, division of labour, and rigour.
```

### Listing 3 — Industrial vs Commercial (the distinction to signal)
| | Industrial | Commercial |
|---|---|---|
| Emphasis | Large-scale **making** of goods | The **business of selling** goods |
| Driven by | Manufacturing capacity & efficiency | A market, target customer, competition |
| Prominent concerns | Machinery, production techniques, cost per unit | Market research, marketing, profit |

> They overlap heavily (a company often does both). Signal in an answer that industrial leans on
> *making* and commercial leans on *selling*.

### Listing 4 — One product, three settings (jam)
| Setting | How the jam is made |
|---|---|
| Domestic | One person, a few jars in a home kitchen; simple equipment; one-off; sold locally/to order; maker does every step |
| Community | A local co-op / garden group makes jam together to raise funds or use local fruit; not for profit; shared labour; modest scale |
| Industrial | A food factory mass-produces thousands of identical jars; automated line; divided labour; rigorous QC & documentation; low cost/jar |

> Same product, same underlying process — utterly different scale, resources, labour and formality.

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "Compare two settings"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low):
  "A factory is bigger than a home." -> one vague difference, no structured contrast.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - State the process is the SAME in both.
  - Contrast across SEVERAL dimensions:
      scale · division of labour · equipment/technology · documentation & quality control · motive
  - Give a concrete EXAMPLE for each setting.
  Structure the comparison dimension by dimension — that's what "compare and contrast" rewards.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — Identify the four settings.
  Domestic, community, industrial, commercial (DCIC). Tag each: home / co-op / factory / market.

Q2 (2 marks) — What distinguishes a community setting?
  Motive: run by a community group/co-op for the COMMON GOOD (jobs, local produce), not primarily
  for profit. E.g. a community garden. (Distinguished by motive, not scale.)

Q3 (5 marks) — Compare domestic vs industrial design & production.
  Same process (DRIPER). Differ in: scale (one-off vs mass); labour (one person does all vs divided
  specialised workforce); equipment (basic tools vs large machinery); QC/documentation (informal vs
  rigorous & documented). Example: home baker vs cake factory. Contrast dimension by dimension.

Q4 (3 marks) — Why is the process the same but the scale different?
  The process (define -> research -> ideate -> produce -> evaluate -> refine) is fundamental to any
  design, so it's universal. Scale/resources (operation size, workforce, machinery, funds) differ,
  changing the EXECUTION (volume, formality, rigour) — not the process itself.

Q5 (3 marks) — Contrast the aims of a community vs a commercial setting.
  Commercial = produce to make a PROFIT by selling competitively in a market (driven by market
  research, marketing, cost). Community = run by a group/co-op for the COMMON GOOD (jobs, produce,
  service), not to maximise profit. E.g. commercial bakery chain vs community garden. The community
  setting is the deliberate non-profit exception.
```

## Glossary

| Term | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **DCIC** | Domestic, Community, Industrial, Commercial | The four settings in which design and production happen (P2.1) |
| **DRIPER** | Define, Research, Ideate, Produce, Evaluate, Refine | The six cyclic stages of the design process (an iterative/cyclical process) |
| QC | Quality Control | Checking output against quality standards (e.g. line sampling) |
