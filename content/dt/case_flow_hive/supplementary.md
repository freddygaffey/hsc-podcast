## What this case study proves

The Flow Hive is the course's flagship example of a **successful innovation** (outcome H3.1,
H3.2). Use it whenever a question asks you to explain how an innovation succeeds, how an
innovative product improves quality of life, or how emerging technologies enable design.

## The six success factors (mnemonic: TEMPLE)

| Factor | In the Flow Hive story |
|---|---|
| **T**iming | Launched during the global "save the bees" / colony-collapse moment |
| **E**merging technologies | 3D modelling + 3D printing to prototype; injection moulding to mass-produce |
| **M**arketing strategies | One crowdfunding video → $2M in a day, $12.2M in 8 weeks; 200k+ social following |
| **P**olitical factors | Weak government innovation support (Cedar's frustration with grants) — succeeded *despite* it |
| **L**egal & economic | Provisional **patent filed before** crowdfunding; chose competitive Australian manufacturing |
| **E**ra (historical/cultural) | Broke the centuries-old smoke-suit-centrifuge tradition; answered "lazy beekeeping" critics |

## Ethical and environmental angle (H2.2)

- Bees are **pollinators** first — their decline threatens food systems; the product is framed
  around protecting them.
- **FSC-certified** timber (Western Red Cedar, Australian hoop pine).
- **Flow Pollinator House** made from salvaged off-cut cedar, laser-cut; **100% of profits** to
  habitat restoration.
- Social enterprise: cyclone (Vanuatu, $97k/3 days) and earthquake (Nepal, $130k/4 days) relief;
  rainforest conservation; community-hive grants.

## Key facts worth quoting (be specific — markers reward detail)

```text
Designers:   Stuart & Cedar Anderson (father & son), 3rd-generation beekeepers, near Nimbin NSW
Development: 10+ years; failed combs-on-side, then hex pistons; breakthrough = vertical cell split
Mechanism:   turn a key → cells split → honey flows via S-channels to a jar; no smoke, no opening
Material:    food-grade, BPA/BPS-free moulded plastic frames
Crowdfunding: Indiegogo 2015; goal US$70k; US$2M in one day; US$12.2M in 8 weeks
Scale:       24,000 orders to 130+ countries; manufactured by Evolve Group, Brisbane
Awards:      Good Design Award of the Year (2016)
IP:          provisional patent filed BEFORE launch → counterfeiters later forced to refund
```

## Exam questions this answers

- *Explain how the development of an innovative product may contribute to quality of life.*
  → easier/safer harvesting, more home beekeepers, bee education, pollinator support.
- *Analyse how economic, historical and timing factors determine the success or failure of
  designs.* → use Timing + Legal/economic + Era from the TEMPLE table above.

**Top tip the Andersons themselves give:** file for IP protection *before* a crowdfunding
campaign, plan your marketing, and expect copycats. That single sequence — protect, then
publish — is the most quotable design-management lesson in the course.

## Glossary

| Term | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| BPA | Bisphenol A | A chemical avoided in food-grade plastics (“BPA-free”) |
| BPS | Bisphenol S | A related chemical avoided in food-grade plastics (“BPS-free”) |
