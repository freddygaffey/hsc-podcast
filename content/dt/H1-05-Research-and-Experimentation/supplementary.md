# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces research,
experimentation, responding to findings, the worked experiment and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — Research to develop the MDP
```text
Apply the module-3 toolkit (QISOTS) at HSC depth, guided by the proposal's AREAS OF INVESTIGATION:
  Questionnaires · Interviews · Surveys · Observation · Tests/experiments · Statistical analysis
  + information research (print & electronic).

PRIMARY (you gather: survey users, run experiments) + SECONDARY (existing: specs, standards, studies).
Ethics (CVA) applies when researching people.
```

### Listing 2 — Experimentation = a practical primary research task (two jobs)
```text
Hands-on testing of MATERIALS, TOOLS and TECHNOLOGIES. It does TWO things:
  GENERATES ideas - experimenting reveals what a material/tool can do -> new design possibilities.
  TESTS ideas     - trial a joint/finish/mechanism -> does it actually work?

It is the design process's RESEARCH + IDEATE stages made physical (not just confirming a decision).
```

### Listing 3 — RESPOND to the findings (the skill that scores)
```text
Research/experiment -> ANALYSE & EVALUATE the results -> MODIFY the design in response -> repeat.
(= the EVALUATE-and-REFINE loop of DRIPER.)

Research only has value when it CHANGES a decision.
  A folio of research that never changed the design -> scores poorly.
  Research that visibly DROVE decisions -> scores well.
End every investigation with "...and so I decided ___."
```

### Listing 4 — Worked experiment (a folio page that scores): choosing a seat material
```text
AIM:    find the most suitable material for a chair seat (strength + comfort).
METHOD: test plywood, solid timber, polymer — same size, same load — record deflection & failure
        (fair test: control the variables).
RESULT: plywood held the load with least deflection, no cracking.
DECISION (response): select plywood for the seat; record the evidence.

Structure: AIM -> METHOD -> RESULT -> DECISION. That's experimentation as primary research.
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "How did research & experimentation inform development?"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "I researched materials online and did some experiments." -> activity, no
findings, no consequences.

STRONG answer (band ~6) — METHOD -> FINDING -> RESPONSE:
  "Tested 3 joining methods for strength; mortise-and-tenon performed best; so I selected it for
  load-bearing joints and redesigned the corner — documented in my folio."
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (3 marks) — How does experimentation generate AND test ideas?
  Generates: hands-on trials reveal what materials/tools can do -> new possibilities. Tests: trial a
  joint/finish/mechanism -> does it work, with real evidence, before committing.

Q2 (3 marks) — Why respond to findings?
  Research adds value only when it changes decisions: analyse results & modify the design ->
  evidence-based, problems corrected, better options adopted (evaluate-refine loop). Ignored research
  adds nothing.

Q3 (5 marks) — How did research & experimentation inform development?
  Name specific research/experiments (e.g. test 3 joints), state findings (mortise-and-tenon best),
  show the design decisions made in response (selected it, redesigned corner; raised height after user
  feedback), with folio evidence. Method -> finding -> response.

Q4 (2 marks) — Primary vs secondary research for the MDP.
  Primary = gathered first-hand (user surveys, material experiments) — current, specific. Secondary =
  existing sources (specs, standards, studies) — fast, general. Experimentation = a key primary method.
```
