The syllabus covers emerging technologies, the factors affecting their development, criteria for evaluation, their impact on society and the environment, and their impact on innovation. And the skill is to appraise the ecological, economic, social, ethical and legal implications of new and emerging technologies, and analyse their impact on innovation. By the end you'll be able to define an emerging technology, appraise its implications, and answer the two-technology question. Let me start with what these technologies are.

NARRATOR: An emerging technology, in Listing 1, is a technology that is recently developed or still in the process of being developed, the textbook defines technologies as the processes humans use to increase their control over and understanding of the material environment. So an emerging technology is a new capability changing what we can design and make. The examples you should keep ready: artificial intelligence, additive manufacturing, that's three-D printing, biotechnology and bioplastics, nanotechnology, renewable and alternative energy systems, robotics, including robotic surgery, the internet of things, and smart or programmable materials. NARRATOR: The factors affecting their development, in Listing 2, are worth a mark. The textbook notes the push toward sustainability has driven many new technologies, financial and economic factors, private enterprise seeking an economic advantage, drive development, and crucially, new technologies lead to newer technologies, each advance enables the next. So emerging tech is shaped by environmental demand, economic incentive, and the momentum of technology itself. And their impact runs three ways: on society, on the environment, and on innovation itself, emerging technologies enable new innovations, three-D printing made the Flow Hive's complex frame possible.

NARRATOR: Now the appraisal framework, the heart of H6.2, because the syllabus verb is appraise, and it names exactly five implications to weigh: ecological, economic, social, ethical and legal. We'll hold them with the hook LESEE, in Listing 3: Legal, Ethical, Social, Economic, Ecological. To appraise an emerging technology, you weigh it across all five: its legal implications, regulation, liability, intellectual property; its ethical implications, fairness, safety, who benefits; its social implications, jobs, access, how it changes life; its economic implications, cost, industry, employment; and its ecological implications, environmental footprint, sustainability. LESEE is your evaluation framework for any new technology in the exam, and it overlaps the ethics episode, the legal and ethical implications are the SIRRI concerns applied to technology.

NARRATOR: Now the headline question, straight from the recent exam: "Analyse the effects of two emerging technologies, or two alternative energy systems, on society and the environment." Like the ethics question, this is a two-example essay, in Listing 5. A weak answer says "AI is changing everything and it's good and bad", vague and one-sided, capping low. A strong answer picks two technologies and appraises each through LESEE, weighing the benefits against the costs on society and the environment, and reaches a judgement. The structure mirrors the ethics essay: a thesis, a paragraph appraising each technology, and a conclusion. Two technologies, appraised through LESEE, judged, that's a fifteen-mark answer.

NARRATOR: Let me make it concrete, in Listing 4, with two technologies appraised. First, additive manufacturing, three-D printing. Socially, it democratises manufacturing, anyone can produce custom parts, and enables medical advances like printed prosthetics, but it can disrupt traditional manufacturing jobs. Economically, it lowers prototyping cost and enables on-demand local production, reducing shipping. Ecologically, it can reduce waste by adding material only where needed, though plastic feedstocks raise disposal concerns. Legally and ethically, it raises questions, printed weapons, copying patented designs. Second, a renewable energy system, solar photovoltaics. Ecologically, it dramatically cuts greenhouse emissions in use, a major benefit, though manufacture and disposal of panels have a footprint. Socially, it provides clean power and energy independence. Economically, costs have fallen sharply, disrupting fossil-fuel industries. Two technologies, each weighed across LESEE on society and the environment, with benefits and costs, that's the model.

QUESTION: Question one. Define an emerging technology and give two examples. Two marks. Pause the player, write your answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. An emerging technology is a technology that has been recently developed or is still in the process of being developed, representing a new capability that changes what can be designed and produced. Two examples are additive manufacturing, three-D printing, and artificial intelligence, others include biotechnology, renewable energy systems and robotics. Two marks: define it as recently or still-developing, and give two valid examples. The trap is naming an old, established technology, it must be new or emerging.

QUESTION: Question two. Identify the implications you would consider when appraising an emerging technology. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. When appraising an emerging technology you consider its legal implications, such as regulation and intellectual property; its ethical implications, such as safety and fairness; its social implications, such as effects on jobs and access; its economic implications, such as cost and impact on industry; and its ecological implications, such as environmental footprint and sustainability, the five remembered as LESEE. Three marks for naming the implications, ideally with a word on each. LESEE ensures you cover all five and don't give a one-dimensional answer.

QUESTION: Question three. Analyse the effects of an emerging technology on society and the environment. Six marks. This is the Section-three-style question, so pause the player and write a full answer before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer, using additive manufacturing, three-D printing. Three-D printing has significant and mixed effects. Socially, it democratises manufacturing by allowing individuals and small businesses to produce custom parts and prototypes cheaply, and it has enabled important medical advances such as affordable, custom prosthetics and printed implants, improving lives, however, it can disrupt traditional manufacturing employment and raises ethical and legal concerns, such as the printing of untraceable weapons or copying of patented designs. Environmentally, it can reduce waste because it is additive, building objects layer by layer using only the material needed, and it enables local, on-demand production that cuts transport emissions, but its reliance on plastic feedstocks raises concerns about disposal and microplastics. On balance, three-D printing offers substantial social and environmental benefits in customisation, access and reduced waste, while posing real risks in employment, regulation and plastic use that must be managed. Six marks: appraise social and environmental effects, weigh benefits against costs, draw on the LESEE dimensions, and reach a judgement. A vague one-sided answer caps low.

QUESTION: Question four. Explain how emerging technologies can impact innovation. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Emerging technologies impact innovation by enabling new products and solutions that were previously impossible, and by providing the tools that make designing and producing innovations faster and more capable. For example, three-D printing and injection moulding made the Flow Hive's complex honeycomb mechanism manufacturable, directly enabling that innovation, and more broadly, each new technology tends to open the door to further innovations built upon it. So emerging technologies are both a driver and an enabler of innovation. Three marks: explain that emerging tech enables previously impossible innovations and tools, that one technology enables others, and give an example. The Flow Hive enabling example is strong evidence.
