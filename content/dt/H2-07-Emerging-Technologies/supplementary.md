# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces what emerging
technologies are, LESEE, the worked appraisal and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — Emerging technologies: definition + examples
```text
EMERGING TECHNOLOGY = one recently developed or still being developed — a new capability that
changes what can be designed/made. (Technologies = the processes humans use to control/understand
the material environment.)

Keep examples ready: AI · additive manufacturing (3D printing) · biotechnology/bioplastics ·
nanotechnology · renewable/alternative energy · robotics (e.g. robotic surgery) · IoT · smart/
programmable materials.
```

### Listing 2 — Factors affecting their development
```text
- SUSTAINABILITY push (drives green technologies)
- FINANCIAL/ECONOMIC factors — private enterprise seeking economic advantage
- MOMENTUM — new technologies lead to NEWER technologies (each enables the next)

Impact runs THREE ways: on SOCIETY, the ENVIRONMENT, and INNOVATION itself (e.g. 3D printing
enabled the Flow Hive).
```

### Listing 3 — LESEE: the appraisal framework (the H6.2 verb is "appraise")
| Letter | Implication | Weigh… |
|---|---|---|
| **L** | Legal | regulation, liability, intellectual property |
| **E** | Ethical | safety, fairness, who benefits |
| **S** | Social | jobs, access, how it changes life |
| **E** | Economic | cost, industry, employment |
| **E** | Ecological | environmental footprint, sustainability (LCA) |

> Legal + ethical = the SIRRI ethics concerns applied to technology; ecological = life cycle analysis.

### Listing 4 — Worked two-technology appraisal (LESEE, on society & environment)
```text
ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING (3D printing):
  Social: democratises manufacturing; printed prosthetics/implants — BUT disrupts manufacturing jobs.
  Economic: cheap prototyping; on-demand local production (less shipping).
  Ecological: additive -> less waste; BUT plastic feedstock disposal/microplastics.
  Legal/ethical: printed weapons; copying patented designs.

SOLAR PHOTOVOLTAICS (renewable energy):
  Ecological: huge emissions cut in USE; BUT panel manufacture/disposal footprint.
  Social: clean power, energy independence.
  Economic: costs fallen sharply; disrupts fossil-fuel industries.
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "Analyse the effects of TWO emerging technologies" (2022 Q15)
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "AI is changing everything, good and bad." -> vague, one-sided, one tech.

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - Pick TWO technologies (or two alternative energy systems).
  - Appraise EACH through LESEE — benefits vs costs on SOCIETY & ENVIRONMENT.
  - Reach a JUDGEMENT. (Structure mirrors the ethics two-example essay.)
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — Define an emerging technology + 2 examples.
  Recently/still-developing technology = a new capability changing what can be made. E.g. 3D printing,
  AI (also biotech, renewable energy, robotics). (Not an old/established technology.)

Q2 (3 marks) — Implications when appraising an emerging technology.
  Legal (regulation/IP), Ethical (safety/fairness), Social (jobs/access), Economic (cost/industry),
  Ecological (footprint/sustainability). [LESEE]

Q3 (6 marks) — Analyse an emerging technology's effects on society & environment.
  3D printing — Social: democratises making, prosthetics, BUT job disruption + weapons/IP concerns.
  Environmental: additive -> less waste, local production -> less transport, BUT plastic disposal.
  Balanced judgement: big benefits in customisation/access/waste vs real risks. (Vague/one-sided caps low.)

Q4 (3 marks) — How do emerging technologies impact innovation?
  Enable previously impossible products + better design/production tools; each tech enables more. E.g.
  3D printing/injection moulding enabled the Flow Hive. Driver AND enabler of innovation.
```

## Glossary

| Term | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| AI | Artificial Intelligence | An emerging technology |
| IoT | Internet of Things | Networked smart devices (an emerging technology) |
| IP | Intellectual Property | Ownership of a product of the intellect with commercial value — copyright, patents, business methods and industrial processes |
| LCA | Life Cycle Analysis | Evaluation of the resources and impacts across a product's whole life (cradle-to-grave); one of the factors affecting design |
| **LESEE** | Legal, Ethical, Social, Economic, Ecological | The implications to appraise for a new/emerging technology (H6.2) |
| **SIRRI** | Sustainable technologies, Intellectual-property protection, designer Rights & responsibilities, Responsibilities to society, Impact on Australian society | The ethical & environmental issues a designer must weigh (H2.2) |
