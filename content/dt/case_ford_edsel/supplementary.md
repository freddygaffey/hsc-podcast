# Ford Edsel — reading companion

![A 1957 Edsel advertisement — note the much-mocked vertical "horse-collar" grille](content/dt/case_ford_edsel/ford-edsel.jpg)

![A 1958 Edsel magazine advertisement: "They'll know you've arrived"](content/dt/case_ford_edsel/edsel-1958.jpg)

The course's definitive **design failure** story. Use it for outcome H1.1 (factors affecting
design; critically analysing examples of success and failure) and H2.1 (the influence of trends
and timing).

## Why it failed — mapped to the factors affecting design

| Factor | What went wrong |
|---|---|
| **Needs / appropriateness** | Market research described the *early-50s* buyer; by 1957 that need had moved on |
| **Timing** (trends) | Launched into a recession + the shift to **compact** cars the research ignored |
| **Finance** | $250M over 10 years — enormous sunk cost made the failure catastrophic |
| **Aesthetics** | Polarising, much-mocked styling that couldn't survive the months of hype |
| **Quality** | No dedicated factory; resentful workers; cars shipped with parts missing |
| **Function** | The flagship Teletouch push-button transmission was its biggest mechanical fault |
| **Branding** | Dropped the trusted "Ford" name; chose "Edsel" over 18,000 agency suggestions |

## The big idea for the exam

> Thorough market research is **worthless if it predicts the wrong future**. The Edsel proves a
> design must match the market *at the moment it launches*, not the market that existed when
> planning began. "Focused on the product, not interested enough in the market."

## Key facts (quote the specifics)

```text
Launch:      "E-Day", 4 September 1957
Investment:  10 years, US$250 million; first new Ford line since Mercury (1938)
Range:       18 models; set up as a separate division (no "Ford" name)
Marketing:   "The Edsel is Coming" teaser campaign — hid the car for months
Sales:       hoped 400,000/yr; sold ~64,000 in year one
Innovations: rolling-dome speedometer, self-adjusting brakes, Teletouch push-button transmission
Failure mix: misread research + bad timing (recession + compacts) + poor build quality + weak brand
Outcome:     discontinued after ~3 years
```

## Exam framing

- *Critically analyse an example of a failed design, referring to the factors affecting design.*
- *Explain how timing and market research influence the success or failure of a design.*

Pair the Edsel (failure) with the **Flow Hive** (success) for any "compare success and failure"
question — they're mirror images: one read its moment perfectly, the other read it years too late.
