# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode. Nothing here is spoken — it reinforces what you study,
how you study it, the emulate-the-process distinction, the worked example and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — H1.2: study the designer's PRACTICE and PROCESS (not their products)
```text
You study HOW a designer works:
  - design PRACTICE: how they actually work day to day
  - the PROCESS they use (every designer runs an adapted version of the design process)

Model: Marc Newson — success lies in his FLEXIBILITY & ADAPTABILITY, using the design process as a
TOOL across diverse fields, with the COURAGE to try unfamiliar materials. (A way of working, not a
product.)
```

### Listing 2 — How to study a designer
```text
- Examine CASE STUDIES / documentaries of their work -> understand their process.
- Read or conduct IN-DEPTH INTERVIEWS -> how they work, problems they overcame.
- Analyse their PRODUCTS -> infer the practices behind them.
- Choose a designer in a field SIMILAR to your MDP.
- You can even CONTACT a professional designer directly (email/phone).
```

### Listing 3 — Emulate the PROCESS, not the PRODUCT (the H1.2 distinction)
```text
EMULATE = adopt their way of working in YOUR project — WHERE APPROPRIATE (not blindly copy all).

  Copy the METHOD:   Newson's adaptability / unfamiliar materials; Dyson's iterative prototyping.
  NOT the ARTEFACT:  don't build Newson's chair or Dyson's vacuum.

This is the line between a band-6 answer and a biography.
```

### Listing 4 — Worked example: Dyson's process → emulated in an MDP
```text
DYSON'S PROCESS:
  identify a flaw in existing products -> research the principle (cyclonic separation) ->
  build & test MANY prototypes, refining each -> finalise.

EMULATED IN MY MDP:
  - adopt iterative prototyping: build 3 successive prototypes (not one).
  - evaluate each vs my success criteria; refine in response (e.g. screw fixing -> clip after a
    test showed faster assembly).
  - document each iteration in the folio as evidence.

-> Copied the METHOD (iterative, evidence-based refinement), not the product.
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question: "Relate a designer's practices to your MDP"
```text
WEAK answer (caps low — biography):
  "Marc Newson is a famous Australian designer who made the Lockheed Lounge and Qantas Skybed."

STRONG answer (band ~6):
  - Identify a SPECIFIC practice/process (e.g. Newson's adaptability with unfamiliar materials).
  - Explain it.
  - Show how you ADOPTED it in your MDP, with EVIDENCE (e.g. "I tested glass-reinforced plastic
    rather than defaulting to timber — see folio").
  Relate, don't recount.
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — What does it mean to emulate a designer's practices?
  Adopt their methods/processes (research, prototyping, testing, decisions) in your own work, where
  appropriate — NOT copy their products.

Q2 (3 marks) — How could you study a designer's practices?
  Case studies/documentaries (their process); interviews (how they work + problems overcome); analyse
  their products; choose a designer in a similar field; contact them directly.

Q3 (5 marks) — Relate a designer's practices to your MDP.
  Dyson = iterative prototyping (flaw -> principle -> many prototypes -> refine). Emulated: built 3
  prototypes, evaluated vs criteria, refined (screw->clip), documented each. Copied the METHOD, not
  the product. (Biography alone caps low.)

Q4 (3 marks) — Why is studying designers valuable for the MDP?
  Designers have proven practices to learn from/emulate; studying how they research/prototype/adapt
  improves your own process & solution; exposes you to materials/techniques/approaches you'd miss.
```
