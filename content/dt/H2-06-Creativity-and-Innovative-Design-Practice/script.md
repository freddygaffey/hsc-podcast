NARRATOR: Creativity, innovation and quality are three related but distinct concepts, in Listing 1, and getting them apart earns marks. Creativity is generating new ideas, the original thinking, the idea itself. Innovation is creativity successfully implemented, an idea developed and delivered into the world as a working product or solution that people actually use. And quality is how well it's done, the standard of execution. The one-liner to hold: creativity is the idea, innovation is the idea delivered. A brilliant idea that never gets built is creativity without innovation, and the most examined point is that innovation needs both the creative spark and the successful delivery.

NARRATOR: Now, what creativity actually is, because the textbook corrects a misconception, in Listing 2. Creativity is not starting from nothing, and it's not a mystical gift, it's developing solutions to challenges through methodical reasoning, exactly the disciplined-process point from module three. It works by adapting and developing ideas, often improving or recombining what exists, and it's triggered by responding to motivational stimuli, a stimulus being a problem, a frustration, or an opportunity that sparks the creative response. So creativity is a process you can run, prompted by a stimulus, using techniques like SCAMPER from module three, to develop and adapt ideas into something new. Methodical, stimulus-driven, adaptive, that's creativity.

NARRATOR: And creativity feeds the success of innovation, in Listing 3, but here's the nuance that earns the top mark: creativity is necessary but not sufficient. A creative idea only becomes a successful innovation if the other factors, the TEMPLE factors, also align, timing, marketing, technology and the rest. The Apple Newton was genuinely creative, an early handheld with novel features, but it failed because its timing and technology weren't right. So creativity drives innovation, but innovation succeeds only when creativity meets favourable conditions. That link, creativity plus TEMPLE equals successful innovation, is the sophisticated point.

QUESTION: Question one. Distinguish between creativity and innovation. Two marks. Pause the player, write your answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Creativity is the generation of new and original ideas, the creative thinking that produces an idea, whereas innovation is the successful development and implementation of a creative idea into a real product or solution that is actually used. The distinction is that creativity is the idea, while innovation is that idea successfully delivered into the world. Two marks: define creativity as generating ideas and innovation as implementing them successfully, and state the distinction. The idea-versus-delivered-idea line is the mark.

QUESTION: Question two. Explain what is meant by responding to a motivational stimulus in the creative process. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Responding to a motivational stimulus means that creativity is triggered by something, a problem, a frustration, a need or an opportunity, that prompts the designer to think creatively and generate ideas. Rather than creativity appearing from nowhere, a stimulus provides the starting point and motivation for the creative response. For example, the inventors of the Flow Hive were motivated by their frustration at disturbing the bees during harvesting, and that stimulus prompted the creative idea of a self-draining honeycomb. Three marks: explain that a stimulus is a problem or opportunity that triggers creativity, that creativity responds to it rather than arising from nothing, and give an example. The marker wants the stimulus-as-trigger idea.

QUESTION: Question three. Discuss the role of creativity in the success of an innovation. Five marks. This is the headline question, so pause the player and write a full answer before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer, using the Flow Hive. Creativity plays a central but not sufficient role in successful innovation. Creativity generates the original idea on which an innovation is built: the Flow Hive began with a creative reframing of an age-old problem, the idea of splitting the honeycomb cell so honey could be drained without opening the hive, prompted by the inventors' frustration at disturbing the bees. However, creativity alone does not guarantee success, the creative idea had to be developed methodically over about a decade into a reliable product, and it only succeeded commercially because other factors aligned, excellent timing during the save-the-bees movement and a brilliant marketing campaign. So creativity is the necessary spark, but an innovation succeeds only when that creativity is successfully developed and meets favourable conditions. By contrast, the Apple Newton was creative yet failed because its timing and technology were wrong. Five marks: define creativity's role as generating the idea, explain that it must be developed and meet favourable conditions, make the necessary-but-not-sufficient point, and support with a success and a failure. A circular "you need creativity" caps low.

QUESTION: Question four. Distinguish between creativity, innovation and quality. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Creativity is the generation of new and original ideas. Innovation is the successful development and implementation of a creative idea into a real, used solution. Quality is the standard or excellence with which the design is executed, how well it is made and how well it meets its criteria. So creativity is the idea, innovation is the idea successfully delivered, and quality is how well it is done, the three are related but distinct concepts a designer aims to achieve together. Three marks: define each of the three distinctly. The marker wants all three separated, not blurred.
