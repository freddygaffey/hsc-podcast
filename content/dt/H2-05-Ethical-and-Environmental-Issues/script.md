QUESTION: Question two. Explain what it means for a designer to have ethical responsibilities. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Having ethical responsibilities means a designer must consider the moral impact of their work on individuals, society and the environment, and do what is right, not merely what is legally permitted. This includes using sustainable materials, ensuring products are safe, respecting intellectual property, and avoiding harm such as exploitative labour. It can require difficult choices, for example declining a profitable project that is environmentally damaging or relies on child labour. Ethical responsibility therefore goes beyond legal compliance to a genuine duty of care toward people and the planet. Three marks: state that ethics concerns the moral impact on people and environment, that it goes beyond the law, and give an example of a hard choice. The beyond-the-law point is the mark.

QUESTION: Question three. Analyse the ethical and social implications for designers in creating solutions to a world issue such as health or food production. Six marks. This is the Section-three-style question, so pause the player and write a full answer before you play.

NARRATOR: Model answer, using health. Designers creating health solutions, such as low-cost medical devices for developing regions, carry significant ethical and social implications. Socially, the benefits are substantial: an affordable prosthetic or diagnostic device can restore mobility, dignity and access to healthcare for people who could not otherwise afford it, advancing equity. Ethically, however, the designer must ensure such devices are safe, reliable and of genuine quality, not cheapened to dangerous standards simply because the target users are poor, which would be exploitative. There are also environmental implications, the materials and manufacture must be considered for sustainability and responsible disposal, especially at scale. On balance, designing solutions to health issues is ethically valuable, delivering real social good and equity, but it demands that the designer uphold safety, quality and sustainability rather than cutting corners. Six marks: analyse the social benefits, the ethical responsibilities and risks, and the environmental implications, weighing them to a judgement. A platitude about "helping people" caps low.

QUESTION: Question four. Explain why protecting intellectual property is both a legal and an ethical issue. Three marks. Pause, answer, then play.

NARRATOR: Model answer. Protecting intellectual property is a legal issue because patents and copyright are enforceable rights that legally prevent others from copying a design, and it is an ethical issue because using or copying another person's design, idea or cultural property without permission or credit is dishonest and unfair, denying the creator the recognition and reward for their work. A designer therefore has both a legal duty to respect others' intellectual property and an ethical duty not to appropriate ideas, including being especially careful with culturally significant material such as Indigenous designs. Three marks: explain the legal dimension, enforceable rights, and the ethical dimension, fairness and credit, ideally noting cultural property. The dual legal-and-ethical framing is the mark.
