# Supplementary Materials

The read-along reference for this episode (a Section III favourite). Nothing here is spoken — it
reinforces the themes, the designer's responsibility, the two-example essay and the model answers.

### Listing 1 — Ethical & environmental themes (SIRRI)
```text
S - Sustainable technologies        (minimise footprint; assessed via life cycle analysis)
I - Intellectual property protection (legal-ethical; protect yours + respect others')
R - Rights & responsibilities of the designer (ethical + legal duties to individuals/society/environment)
R - Responsibilities to society      (impact on people: child labour/exploitation, equity)
I - Impact on Australian society      (specifically)

Ethical (people) AND environmental (planet) — the outcome pairs them; weigh BOTH.
```

### Listing 2 — The designer's responsibility (ethics > the law)
```text
Designers have BOTH legal AND ethical responsibilities, and a duty to individuals, society & the
environment. Ethics = doing what is RIGHT, not merely what is PERMITTED.

Hard choices: decline an ethically disturbing client; refuse child-labour materials even if cheaper.
=> An ethical designer takes a STAND, weighing impact on people & planet.
```

### Listing 3 — The two-example essay (Section III, ~15 marks)
```text
e.g. (2023 Q15): "Analyse the ethical and social implications for designers creating solutions to
world issues. Refer to TWO of: Health, Food production, Living conditions."

STRUCTURE:
  - Intro with a thesis.
  - One paragraph PER example: analyse ethical + environmental/social implications, BENEFITS vs COSTS.
  - Conclusion: a judgement.

You MUST use TWO examples (one example caps the answer).
```

### Listing 4 — Worked two-example answer (health + food production)
```text
HEALTH (low-cost prosthetic for developing regions):
  Benefit: restores mobility/dignity, equity (reaches those priced out).
  Ethical/cost: must be SAFE & quality (don't cut corners because users are poor); sustainable supply.

FOOD PRODUCTION (intensive agricultural tech):
  Benefit: feeds a growing population.
  Ethical/social cost: small farmers, animal welfare; ENVIRONMENTAL cost (land clearing, emissions,
  biodiversity) = environmental vs social tension.

JUDGEMENT: designers solving world issues must balance the genuine good against social/environmental
costs; the best solutions deliver the benefit while minimising harm.
```

### Listing 5 — Headline question + tools
```text
WEAK answer (caps low): "Designers should be ethical and help people." -> platitude.

STRONG answer (band ~6): two real examples, each analysed for ethical + environmental/social
implications (benefits vs costs), + a judgement. Tools: ISE (impact levels), life cycle analysis,
case studies (Central Park = green image vs real impact; Flow Hive = sustainability + IP).
```

### Listing 6 — Model answers to the closing exam questions
```text
Q1 (2 marks) — Three ethical/environmental issues.
  IP protection; sustainable technologies / minimise impact; responsibility to society (avoid child
  labour/exploitation). [+ designer's rights/responsibilities; impact on Australian society] — SIRRI.

Q2 (3 marks) — What does it mean to have ethical responsibilities?
  Consider the moral impact on people/environment; do what's RIGHT not just legal; sustainable materials,
  safe products, respect IP, avoid harm; hard choices (decline damaging/child-labour work). Beyond the law.

Q3 (6 marks) — Analyse ethical & social implications of a health/food solution.
  Health (low-cost prosthetic): social benefit/equity BUT must be safe/quality (not cheapened) +
  sustainable. Weigh -> ethically valuable if safety/quality/sustainability upheld. (Platitude caps low.)

Q4 (3 marks) — Why is protecting IP both legal AND ethical?
  Legal: patents/copyright are enforceable rights. Ethical: copying without permission/credit is unfair,
  denies the creator recognition/reward (esp. cultural property). Dual duty.
```
