NARRATOR: First, the two masses on top. The force depends on the product of both masses, which means gravity is mutual. NARRATOR: Third ingredient, big-G, the universal gravitational constant. NARRATOR: Now to the second big idea of the episode, and the one that reframes gravity for the whole rest of the course: the gravitational field. Instead of thinking about the force between two specific masses every time, physicists think of a single large mass, like the Earth, as filling the space around it with a gravitational field, a region in which any other mass will feel a force. The gravitational field strength at a point, given the symbol little-g, is defined as the force per unit mass that a small test mass would feel at that point. Force per unit mass. So its units are newtons per kilogram. Put a two-kilogram mass somewhere and if it feels twenty newtons of gravity, the field strength there is twenty divided by two, ten newtons per kilogram. The field is a property of the space around the big mass, and it exists whether or not there's anything there to feel it, exactly the way we described the electric field around a charge back in Year Eleven, Module Four.

NARRATOR: And now the two ideas snap together in a way you must be able to reproduce. NARRATOR: And that number cracks open the single biggest misconception in this entire module, so this is the one to remember.
